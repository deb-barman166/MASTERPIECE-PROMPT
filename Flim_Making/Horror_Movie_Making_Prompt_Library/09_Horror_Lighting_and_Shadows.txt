1. TITLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HORROR LIGHTING & SHADOWS
Category: Lighting Design & Shadow Architecture
Compatible Engines: Sora, Veo, Kling, Runway, Pika, Luma, Hailuo (video
generation) + GPT/Claude/Gemini/Grok/DeepSeek (lighting reasoning)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Provides a precise system for specifying light SOURCE, QUALITY, DIRECTION,
and — most importantly for horror — SHADOW BEHAVIOR, since shadow is the
genre's primary tool. This template treats darkness as an active
compositional element to be designed with the same rigor as light itself,
rather than as an absence to be filled.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. PROMPT FORMULA
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[LIGHT SOURCE + MOTIVATION] + [LIGHT QUALITY: HARD/SOFT] + [DIRECTION +
KEY ANGLE] + [SHADOW ARCHITECTURE: WHAT HIDES WHERE] + [NEGATIVE SPACE
RATIO] + [PRACTICAL LIGHT BEHAVIOR: FLICKER/DECAY/MOTIVATION] +
[CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN] + [PLATFORM-SPECIFIC SYNTAX WRAPPER]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. VARIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{LIGHT_SOURCE} — specific, motivated source (single practical bulb,
  moonlight through a specific window shape, flashlight beam, distant
  fire, complete absence with only implied ambient)
{LIGHT_MOTIVATION} — why this light exists in-world (never unmotivated
  "movie lighting" — every source should be traceable to something in
  the scene)
{LIGHT_QUALITY} — hard (sharp-edged shadows, single small/distant source)
  vs. soft (diffused, wrapped, larger/closer source) — and why this choice
  serves the scene's intended dread
{DIRECTION_AND_KEY_ANGLE} — where light originates relative to subject
  (underlight for threat-dominance wrongness, top-light for isolation/
  exposure, side/raking light for texture and half-concealment, backlight
  for silhouette/mystery)
{SHADOW_ARCHITECTURE} — explicitly define what the shadow conceals (a
  specific area of the frame, a specific part of a character/creature) and
  what it's allowed to almost-reveal
{NEGATIVE_SPACE_RATIO} — approximate proportion of frame in shadow vs. lit
  (horror typically favors 60-80% shadow dominance in tension scenes)
{PRACTICAL_LIGHT_BEHAVIOR} — flicker pattern, dimming, color temperature
  shift, and whether this behavior is caused by anything diegetic
  (motivated) or is itself a wrongness signal (unmotivated = deliberate
  unease)
{COLOR_TEMPERATURE} — warm/cool balance and where it shifts, tied to
  Template 08's overall palette philosophy
{CONSISTENCY_LOCK_TOKEN} — reusable phrase for this lighting setup, scoped
  per-location/per-scene-type as needed
{NEGATIVE_TRAITS} — exclude default AI-generation lighting tendencies (no
  even/flat "product photography" lighting default, no unmotivated rim
  light, no daytime-bright "night" scenes)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Every light source must be motivated — unmotivated "cinematic" lighting
  (light with no in-world source) breaks the naturalism most horror
  depends on, unless the story is deliberately expressionistic (specify
  this explicitly if so).
- Design shadow with the same intent as light — decide precisely what a
  shadow conceals and defend that choice against the scene's needs, rather
  than letting shadow placement be an accident of the light source
  description.
- Favor practical, diegetic light sources (bulbs, flashlights, fire,
  moonlight) over ambient "movie lighting" — practical sources create
  believable, motivated shadow behavior that reads as more unsettling than
  evenly-lit scenes.
- Use NEGATIVE_SPACE_RATIO deliberately and track it — a scene that is
  80% shadow only works if the remaining 20% is meaningfully composed;
  don't let shadow dominance become an excuse for compositional laziness.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. BEST PRACTICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Establish a lighting "rule" per location (from Template 05) early and
  keep it consistent — if a hallway is established as always lit only by
  a single flickering bulb, every future scene in that hallway should
  respect that unless the story explicitly changes it (e.g., a power
  restoration beat).
- Reserve your most extreme lighting choice (total darkness, hardest
  contrast) for the story's most significant scene — don't spend your
  strongest lighting tool on an early, lower-stakes moment.
- When generating with AI video platforms, explicitly state what should
  NOT be visible/lit, since most models default toward filling frames with
  legible detail unless told otherwise.
- Validate flicker/decay light behavior on your target platform with a
  short test generation before committing — timing-based light behavior
  is one of the more platform-variable elements to get right.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. HORROR CINEMATIC TECHNIQUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- THE ALMOST-REVEAL SHADOW: design shadow architecture so a shape is
  suggested but never fully confirmed — the edge of the shadow should be
  precise enough to imply form without confirming it.
- UNDERLIGHT FOR WRONGNESS: light a human face from below only in moments
  meant to signal danger or the uncanny — this angle reads as
  fundamentally unnatural to human perception since it inverts the sun's
  natural top-down key.
- THE DYING LIGHT: use gradually failing practical light (a dimming
  flashlight, a bulb about to burn out) as a built-in tension timer that
  audiences intuitively read as an escalating threat.
- SHADOW THAT MOVES WRONG: specify a shadow behavior that doesn't match
  its light source's implied movement (a shadow shifts when nothing has
  moved) as an explicit, deliberate wrongness signal reserved for key
  reveal moments.
- THE SINGLE LIT EYE: in extreme close-ups, allow only partial facial
  illumination (one eye lit, the rest in shadow) to fragment a face into
  unfamiliar, unsettling parts.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. CAMERA & LIGHTING GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Cross-reference Template 07 explicitly: angle and light direction
  interact (a low camera angle with underlight compounds threat-dominance
  more than either alone; the same angle with top-light creates a
  different, more vulnerable read).
- Lens choice affects shadow rendering — wide lenses can exaggerate shadow
  distortion at frame edges; account for this when combining with Template
  07's lens selections.
- Match this template's DIRECTION_AND_KEY_ANGLE choices against Template
  08's overall CONTRAST_PROFILE to ensure the specific scene lighting
  serves the project-wide visual style rather than contradicting it.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
11. SOUND & ATMOSPHERE GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Practical light sources often carry their own diegetic sound (a bulb's
  electrical hum, a flashlight's switch click, fire's crackle) — specify
  this explicitly for Template 13 continuity.
- Flicker/decay light behavior should be sound-matched where physically
  plausible (a failing bulb's flicker paired with a corresponding
  electrical sound) unless the mismatch itself is the intended wrongness
  signal.
- Total-darkness scenes should specify what sound alone must carry the
  scene's tension, since visual information is by definition withheld.

12. EXAMPLE INPUT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LIGHT_SOURCE: A single overhead incandescent bulb on a pull-chain
LIGHT_MOTIVATION: The room's only fixture, original to the building's 1958
  construction, never replaced
LIGHT_QUALITY: Hard, small-source — sharp-edged, unforgiving shadows
DIRECTION_AND_KEY_ANGLE: Direct overhead, minimal fill, creating heavy
  shadow pooling under brow, nose, and chin on any face beneath it
SHADOW_ARCHITECTURE: The lower half of the room (below waist height on a
  standing figure) remains in near-total shadow at all times; the ledger
  shelves recede into unlit darkness past arm's reach
NEGATIVE_SPACE_RATIO: 70% shadow dominance
PRACTICAL_LIGHT_BEHAVIOR: Single flicker at the scene's climax moment,
  unmotivated by any diegetic cause (no wind, no electrical fault
  established) — a deliberate wrongness signal
COLOR_TEMPERATURE: Warm (2700K equivalent), consistent with Template 08's
  earth-tone palette
NEGATIVE_TRAITS: No even fill light, no visible secondary light source,
  no daytime-bright shadow detail

13. EXAMPLE OUTPUT (Condensed)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LIGHTING BIBLE SUMMARY: A single hard overhead bulb pools warm, unforgiving
light directly downward, carving deep shadow beneath every feature and
surrendering 70% of the frame to darkness the ledger shelves recede into —
the one unmotivated flicker at the scene's peak is the room's only lie.

CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN:
"Single hard overhead incandescent bulb, warm 2700K, heavy downward shadow
pooling, 70% frame in darkness, lower room and shelf depths unlit"

CONCEALMENT MAP: Fully visible = upper portion of Ines's face and the
ledger directly beneath the bulb. Partially visible (implied, not
confirmed) = the shelf depths just past arm's reach. Fully concealed =
everything below waist height, the room's corners, anything beyond
approximately 4 feet from the bulb's direct throw.

PLATFORM ADAPTATIONS:
- Video-model syntax: "A single bare incandescent bulb hangs overhead,
  warm and hard, casting sharp downward shadows across her face — deep
  pools beneath her brow and chin. Below waist height, the room falls into
  near-total darkness. The ledger shelves behind her recede into
  blackness past arm's reach. The bulb flickers once, briefly, though
  nothing in the room moved to cause it."

CONTINUITY CHECKLIST: Bulb position and warm 2700K temperature must match
every other scene set in this location ✓. The unmotivated flicker is
reserved for this specific climax beat only, not repeated elsewhere as a
generic effect ✓.

  scene, define their relative intensity, how their shadows overlap/

{LIGHT_STATE_TRANSITION_SEQUENCE} — for scenes where lighting changes

  techniques, specify the color/contrast adjustments needed to sell it

  the project's visual signature for the antagonist's presence, to be
  referenced in every future scene where it is near

ADVANCED OUTPUT ADDITION: Request a "SHADOW CONSISTENCY AUDIT" — verify
across the growing shot list that concealment logic (what's hidden per
location) hasn't drifted, and that any location established as "certain
areas are always dark" hasn't been contradicted by a later generation.
