────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. TITLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HAUNTED LOCATION GENERATOR
Category: Environment & Setting Design
Compatible Engines: Sora, Veo, Kling, Runway, Pika, Luma, Hailuo (visual
generation) + GPT/Claude/Gemini/Grok/DeepSeek (design reasoning)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Designs a fully realized horror location — architecture, history, spatial
logic, and "wrongness geography" — as a persistent, consistent world that
functions as a character in its own right. The goal is a location the
audience could draw a map of, with specific rooms/zones that carry distinct
emotional registers, avoiding the generic "spooky house" default by forcing
concrete architectural and historical grounding.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. PROMPT FORMULA
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[LOCATION TYPE + ERA] + [ARCHITECTURAL LOGIC] + [HISTORY/ORIGIN OF DREAD] +
[SPATIAL MAP: ZONE-BY-ZONE EMOTIONAL REGISTER] + [WRONGNESS GEOGRAPHY] +
[ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING DETAILS] + [SCALE + LIGHT BEHAVIOR] +
[CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN] + [PLATFORM-SPECIFIC SYNTAX WRAPPER]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. VARIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{LOCATION_TYPE} — specific building/place type (Victorian sanatorium,
  abandoned grain silo, suburban home built on a specific site history,
  underground transit tunnel, rural motel)
{ERA_BUILT} + {ERA_PRESENT_DAY} — when it was built vs. when the story is
  set, and what decayed/changed between
{ARCHITECTURAL_LOGIC} — specific structural details that make the space
  spatially believable (floor plan logic, materials, why rooms are shaped
  as they are)
{HISTORY_OF_DREAD} — the specific historical event(s) that give the
  location its wrongness — avoid vague "bad things happened here"
{SPATIAL_ZONE_MAP} — 4-6 distinct named zones/rooms, each with a specific
  emotional register (the zone that feels safe, the zone that's always
  cold, the zone no character wants to enter alone)
{WRONGNESS_GEOGRAPHY} — specific ways the space defies normal physics/logic
  (a hallway that's longer going one direction, a room that's always 10
  degrees colder, a door that was never there in blueprints)
{ENVIRONMENTAL_STORYTELLING} — specific objects/details that convey history
  without exposition (a child's height marks on a doorframe, a boarded
  window from the inside)
{SCALE_AND_LIGHT_BEHAVIOR} — how natural/artificial light behaves in the
  space (which rooms never get direct sun, where shadows fall unnaturally)
{SEASONAL_TIME_VARIANT} — how the location should look at different times
  of day/year, for scene-to-scene consistency
{CONSISTENCY_LOCK_TOKEN} — reusable phrase capturing the location's
  unmistakable visual/spatial signature
{NEGATIVE_TRAITS} — exclude generic horror-location clichés unless
  specifically subverted (no generic cobwebs-everywhere default, no
  randomly flickering lights without cause)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. INPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Design a fully realized horror location with persistent spatial logic.

LOCATION TYPE: {LOCATION_TYPE}
ERA BUILT / ERA PRESENT: {ERA_BUILT} / {ERA_PRESENT_DAY}
ARCHITECTURAL LOGIC: {ARCHITECTURAL_LOGIC}
HISTORY OF DREAD: {HISTORY_OF_DREAD}
SPATIAL ZONE MAP: {SPATIAL_ZONE_MAP}
WRONGNESS GEOGRAPHY: {WRONGNESS_GEOGRAPHY}
ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING DETAILS: {ENVIRONMENTAL_STORYTELLING}
LIGHT BEHAVIOR: {SCALE_AND_LIGHT_BEHAVIOR}
SEASONAL/TIME VARIANTS NEEDED: {SEASONAL_TIME_VARIANT}
EXCLUDE: {NEGATIVE_TRAITS}

Generate:
1. A CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN for the location's overall identity.
2. A per-zone prompt set — one complete, platform-ready prompt per named
   zone in SPATIAL_ZONE_MAP, each internally consistent with the overall
   architectural logic and lock token.
3. A simple spatial relationship note (which zones connect to which) so
   the location remains navigable and consistent across multiple scenes.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. OUTPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. LOCATION BIBLE SUMMARY (history, architecture, and central wrongness in
   one paragraph)
2. CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN (isolated, bolded)
3. ZONE-BY-ZONE PROMPT SET (one prompt per zone, labeled with its emotional
   register)
4. SPATIAL RELATIONSHIP MAP (simple text-based floor logic: which zones
   are adjacent, which are hidden/hard to find)
5. PLATFORM ADAPTATIONS (image-model / video-model / LLM reasoning syntax)
6. CONTINUITY CHECKLIST (fixed facts: number of floors, key architectural
   features that must remain constant across every future scene prompt)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- A location's dread should come from specific, plausible history, not
  vague "evil energy." Ground HISTORY_OF_DREAD in a believable human event
  even in supernatural stories — audiences fear plausible tragedy more than
  abstract evil.
- Design zones with contrasting emotional registers deliberately — a
  location where every room feels equally dangerous has nowhere to
  escalate to; include at least one "false safe" zone.
- WRONGNESS_GEOGRAPHY should be internally consistent and rule-bound (the
  hallway is always 12% longer going north, not randomly different every
  time) — inconsistent wrongness reads as sloppy rather than uncanny.
- Environmental storytelling details should never require dialogue to
  explain — if a detail needs a character to state its meaning aloud, it's
  not doing its job as environmental storytelling.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. BEST PRACTICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Build the floor plan logic before writing zone descriptions — spatial
  incoherence is one of the fastest ways to break audience immersion in
  location-driven horror.
- Limit WRONGNESS_GEOGRAPHY to 1-2 core impossible rules per location;
  stacking many defies-physics elements dilutes the impact of each and
  strains internal consistency.
- Reuse the same 2-3 signature materials/textures (a specific wood, a
  specific wallpaper pattern, a specific type of window) across all zones
  to keep the location visually unified across many generated shots.
- Design at least one zone that must NEVER be shown in full light/scale in
  any scene, reserved for the location's most significant reveal.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. HORROR CINEMATIC TECHNIQUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- THE FALSE SAFE ROOM: designate one zone that reads as refuge throughout
  the story, then subvert it once, late, for maximum impact.
- GEOGRAPHY THAT LIES: use WRONGNESS_GEOGRAPHY consistently enough that
  attentive viewers can start to detect the pattern, rewarding rewatches.
- THE UNSEEN ZONE: keep one mapped zone entirely off-screen for most of the
  runtime, built up through other characters' dialogue/reactions before
  ever being shown directly.
- MATERIAL MEMORY: let specific materials (water stains, worn floorboards)
  visually "remember" the history of dread without any supernatural
  embellishment — mundane decay can be as unsettling as the impossible.
- VERTICALITY AS DREAD: use up/down movement (into a basement, up into an
  attic) as a structural tension tool — vertical transitions read as
  higher-stakes than horizontal ones.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. CAMERA & LIGHTING GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Assign a distinct light behavior per zone (the zone that only ever has
  hard directional light, the zone with no light source at all) to keep
  Template 09 (Lighting) mapping consistent zone-to-zone.
- Specify preferred lens/framing tendencies per zone type: wide shots for
  established "safe" zones to emphasize openness, tighter/narrower framing
  for wrongness zones to emphasize confinement.
- Note where natural light sources exist (windows, skylights) precisely,
  since their position determines shadow behavior across the entire
  shooting day/night cycle for continuity.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
11. SOUND & ATMOSPHERE GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Assign a distinct ambient soundscape per zone (structural creaks specific
  to that zone's materials, a hum only present in one room) for Template
  10 and Template 13 continuity.
- Note where sound should behave abnormally as part of WRONGNESS_GEOGRAPHY
  (a room with unnatural echo, a zone with total sound dampening).
- Flag the zone reserved for the location's primary "silence cue" — the
  space where ambient sound should drop out entirely for maximum dread.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
12. EXAMPLE INPUT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LOCATION_TYPE: A decommissioned rural telephone exchange building
ERA_BUILT: 1958 / ERA_PRESENT_DAY: Present day, abandoned since 2003
ARCHITECTURAL_LOGIC: Single-story brick utility building with a
  disproportionately deep basement level (original relay equipment
  storage), narrow service corridors designed for machines, not people
HISTORY_OF_DREAD: A technician went missing during a night shift in 1974;
  official record states he "walked off," but his shift-end call was
  logged forty minutes after his badge-out time
SPATIAL_ZONE_MAP: 1) Front office (false-safe, dusty but mundane) 2) Main
  relay hall (oppressive, rows of dead switching equipment) 3) Narrow
  service corridor (claustrophobic, single-file only) 4) Basement relay
  vault (coldest, source of the wrongness) 5) Roof access (isolated,
  overlooks nothing but empty fields)
WRONGNESS_GEOGRAPHY: The basement stairwell always has exactly one more
  step going down than coming back up, consistently, every time
ENVIRONMENTAL_STORYTELLING: A logbook open to the technician's final
  entry, pen still uncapped beside it after 50 years; a single office
  chair facing the relay hall door instead of the desk
SCALE_AND_LIGHT_BEHAVIOR: No windows in relay hall or basement; only light
  source is battery emergency lighting with a slow amber flicker cycle
SEASONAL_TIME_VARIANT: Winter, night exteriors only for establishing shots
NEGATIVE_TRAITS: No generic cobwebs-everywhere default, no random unmotivated
  flickering unrelated to the emergency lighting logic

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
13. EXAMPLE OUTPUT (Condensed)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LOCATION BIBLE SUMMARY: A 1958 rural telephone exchange, abandoned since
2003, whose basement relay vault holds the specific, quiet wrongness of a
staircase that never has the same number of steps twice — a small,
consistent impossibility that mirrors the missing forty minutes in a
technician's 1974 disappearance.

CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN:
"Abandoned 1958 brick telephone exchange, amber emergency-light flicker,
narrow machine-scale corridors, basement stairwell with one extra step
descending"

ZONE PROMPT — Basement Relay Vault (Wrongness core):
[Lock Token] + "basement relay vault, rows of dead switching equipment
receding into darkness, single amber emergency light casting long slow-
flickering shadows, visible breath in the cold air, stairwell entrance
frames the shot, no windows, oppressive low ceiling"

ZONE PROMPT — Front Office (False-safe):
[Lock Token] + "front office area, dusty reception desk, faded 1970s
wall calendar, weak grey daylight through a single dirty window, mundane
and quiet, contrasts sharply with the windowless interior beyond"

SPATIAL RELATIONSHIP MAP: Front Office → connects to → Main Relay Hall →
connects to → Narrow Service Corridor → descends to → Basement Relay Vault
(the wrongness zone) | Roof Access is reached via a separate exterior
ladder, isolated from the interior zones entirely.

PLATFORM ADAPTATIONS:
- Video-model syntax: "The camera moves slowly down a narrow basement
  stairwell into a relay vault filled with rows of dead switching
  equipment. A single amber emergency light flickers slowly, casting long
  shadows across the machines. The air is cold enough to see breath."

14. ADVANCED VERSION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Add for multi-location or franchise-scale worldbuilding:

{MULTI_LOCATION_CONNECTIVITY} — if the story involves multiple linked
  locations, define how their wrongness geographies relate or contrast
{DEGRADATION_TIMELINE} — how the location's physical state should change
  across a story spanning multiple days/weeks (mold spread, structural
  failure) for shoot-order continuity
{PRACTICAL_LOCATION_SCOUTING_NOTE} — a note translating the designed space
  into real-world location-scouting criteria, for hybrid AI-generated/
  practical production pipelines
{VIRTUAL_BLUEPRINT_EXPORT} — request a simple top-down text-based floor
  plan description suitable for conversion into an actual diagram/blueprint
  reference image

ADVANCED OUTPUT ADDITION: Request a "GEOGRAPHY CONSISTENCY AUDIT" — a
review pass checking that every zone prompt in the set uses matching
materials, light sources, and scale language, flagging any zone that
would visually contradict the established architectural logic if generated
as-is.
