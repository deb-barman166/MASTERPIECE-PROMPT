2. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Specifies CUT LOGIC — where scenes begin and end, how transitions between
shots manipulate pacing and dread, and how rhythm itself becomes a horror
tool. Unlike the generation-focused templates, this template governs
ASSEMBLY: given a set of generated clips (from Template 06), how they
should be sequenced, trimmed, and transitioned to maximize the tension
architecture established across the entire project.

4. VARIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{SEQUENCE_CONTEXT} — the scene/sequence being edited, with its beat map
  from Template 02/06
{CUT_TYPE_PER_TRANSITION} — select per transition point:
  - HARD CUT: default, neutral, or can be jarring if timed against
    expectation
  - MATCH CUT: visual/compositional similarity across the cut, used to
    create uncanny connection or seamless dread continuity
  - CONTRAST CUT: deliberate visual/tonal opposition across the cut
    (violence to stillness, chaos to silence) for whiplash effect
  - J-CUT/L-CUT: audio leads or trails the visual cut, used to create
    anticipatory dread (sound of the next scene bleeding in early) or
    lingering unease (sound of the previous scene bleeding out)
  - SLOW DISSOLVE: rare in horror, reserved for specific dreamlike/
    temporal-distortion moments
  - HOLD-THEN-CUT: extending a shot's held duration beyond comfort before
    finally cutting, weaponizing the cut's timing itself
{SHOT_LENGTH_PATTERN} — the rhythm of cut frequency across a sequence
  (long holds building to rapid cutting, or consistently long holds
  throughout for a slow-burn sequence)
{MATCH_CONTRAST_CUT_LOGIC} — specific instances where compositional
  elements should align or oppose across a cut, and why
{SOUND_TO_CUT_SYNC} — whether cuts should land on sound beats, against
  them, or in silence (inherited from Template 13)
{WITHHOLD_REVEAL_VIA_EDIT} — how editing itself controls information
  pacing (cutting away just before a reveal, holding just past the
  expected cut point)
{FALSE_CUT_RHYTHM} — establishing a predictable cutting rhythm, then
  breaking it once at a key moment for maximum disruption
{NEGATIVE_TRAITS} — exclude generic/overused editing defaults (no
  rapid-cut jump-scare montage as a default response to every tense
  moment, no arbitrary quick-cut "style" disconnected from the specific
  scene's needs)

7. PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Editing rhythm should mirror the sequence's tension curve from Template
  06 — builds typically favor longer holds with gradually shortening
  duration as tension increases; releases typically favor a single clean
  cut to a contrasting, calmer shot.
- Reserve rapid-cut montage editing for genuine chaos/climax moments — its
  overuse as a default horror editing style is one of the most common
  ways tension gets diluted rather than heightened.
- Use J-cuts and L-cuts deliberately for anticipatory dread — letting the
  next scene's sound arrive before its picture is one of horror editing's
  most efficient and underused tools.
- Match cut timing to sound design (Template 13) explicitly — a cut
  landing exactly on a sound cue reads very differently than one landing
  slightly before or after; specify which effect is intended.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. BEST PRACTICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Establish a baseline cutting rhythm for "normal world" scenes early in a
  project, so that rhythm changes during tension/horror sequences register
  clearly as deliberate shifts.
- Hold shots slightly longer than feels comfortable during build sequences
  — the instinct to cut away from stillness is usually premature; test
  extending holds before assuming a shot needs to be shorter.
- Plan match cuts and their targets before generation/shooting begins
  where possible, since achieving precise compositional alignment across
  a cut is far easier with advance planning than in post-hoc assembly.
- Break an established cutting rhythm no more than once or twice per major
  sequence — a "false cut rhythm" disruption only works as a surprise if
  it's rare.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. HORROR CINEMATIC TECHNIQUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- THE WITHHELD CUT: extend a shot noticeably past the point where a
  standard edit would cut away, forcing the audience to search the frame
  themselves during the extra duration.
- THE PREMATURE CUT: cut away from an expected reveal slightly before it
  would complete, denying the audience visual confirmation and letting
  imagination fill the gap.
- SOUND-LED ANTICIPATION (J-CUT): let the next scene's sound (particularly
  a MOTIF_TOKEN from Template 13) begin under the tail of the current
  scene, creating dread before the cut even happens.
- THE RHYTHM BREAK: establish a clear, predictable cutting pattern across
  several shots, then break the pattern once at a key story moment — the
  disruption of expectation is itself the scare.
- MATCH CUT AS UNCANNY BRIDGE: use a match cut between two visually similar
  but contextually very different images (a spinning object in a safe
  scene matched to a similar shape in a dangerous one) to create a
  subconscious connection between disparate moments.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. CAMERA & LIGHTING GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Cut points should respect the camera movement established in Template
  07 — cutting mid-movement (during a push-in or pan) produces a very
  different effect than cutting only at movement completion; specify
  which is intended.
- Match cuts work best when Template 08's cinematography style
  (contrast, color) is deliberately aligned or opposed across the cut —
  coordinate explicitly with the project's established visual style.
- Consider how Template 09's lighting states transition across a cut — a
  hard cut between very different lighting states can itself function as
  a jarring effect; a match cut between similarly-lit shots creates
  smoother, more uncanny continuity.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
11. SOUND & ATMOSPHERE GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- SOUND_TO_CUT_SYNC decisions should be made in direct coordination with
  Template 13's cue sheet — specify explicitly whether each cut lands on,
  before, or after its associated sound cue.
- Silence cues from Template 13 often pair with the HOLD-THEN-CUT
  technique — extending a shot within an established silence before
  cutting compounds the tension of both techniques.
- Consider using a sudden, hard sound cut (audio drops to silence exactly
  on a picture cut) as a distinct technique from a gradual audio fade,
  since the abruptness itself carries a different psychological effect.
