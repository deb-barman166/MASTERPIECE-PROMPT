────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Specifies the complete audio landscape of a horror scene — score, diegetic
sound, ambient bed, and SILENCE as an active compositional choice. Horror
is arguably more dependent on sound design than any other genre; this
template treats audio with equal rigor to the visual templates, defining
exact cue placement, frequency character, and — critically — where sound
should be deliberately withheld.

4. VARIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{SCENE_TENSION_CURVE} — inherited from Template 06, where the scene's peak
  intensity falls, to align audio escalation with visual pacing
{AMBIENT_BED_DESIGN} — the continuous background sound layer (room tone,
  environmental hum, wind, mechanical drone) and its specific character
{DIEGETIC_SOUND_INVENTORY} — specific in-world sounds present (footsteps
  on a specific surface, a specific door's hinge quality, clothing rustle)
  described precisely enough for consistency
{SCORE_APPROACH} — orchestral/traditional vs. drone/ambient vs. musique
  concrète/processed-sound vs. silence-dominant with minimal scoring
{INSTRUMENTATION} — if scored, specific instrument choices and why (low
  strings for dread, prepared piano for wrongness, extended techniques for
  the uncanny)
{FREQUENCY_TEXTURE_CHARACTER} — low-frequency rumble (subconscious unease,
  often below conscious hearing threshold) vs. high-frequency tension
  (physical discomfort) vs. mid-range naturalism
{SILENCE_CUE_PLACEMENT} — exact moments where sound should drop out
  entirely or near-entirely, inherited from Template 02/06 script
  breakdown
{STINGER_HIT_TIMING} — precise moment(s) for a sudden sound accent, and
  explicit calibration of how loud/sudden relative to the preceding bed
  (avoid overuse; reserve for genuine peak moments)
{MOTIF_TOKEN} — the antagonist/threat's specific recurring sound signature
  (inherited from Templates 01/04), to be placed consistently
{DYNAMIC_RANGE_STRATEGY} — overall loud/quiet contrast philosophy across
  the scene or full project
{NEGATIVE_TRAITS} — exclude generic horror audio defaults (no generic
  "jump scare stinger" overuse, no continuous droning tension score with
  no variation, no telegraphed musical cues that announce a scare before
  it happens)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Treat silence as a designed element with explicit start/end points, not
  as an absence that happens by default when no other instruction is
  given — unplanned silence often gets filled with generic ambient bed by
  default in automated audio pipelines.
- Reserve stingers/sudden accents for genuine narrative peaks — frequency
  of use inversely correlates with effectiveness; a project with constant
  stingers trains the audience to stop reacting to them.
- Low-frequency sound (often at or near the edge of conscious perception)
  is one of horror's most effective and most underused tools — consider
  it deliberately for build/dread beats rather than defaulting to audible
  melodic tension cues.
- Never let score telegraph a scare before it visually happens — if music
  swells noticeably before a reveal, the audience anticipates and braces,
  reducing impact; sync stinger placement to the moment of reveal, not
  before it.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. BEST PRACTICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Establish the MOTIF_TOKEN early and use it with strict discipline —
  never let the antagonist's signature sound play when the antagonist is
  not present or imminent, or the audience will stop trusting the cue.
- Build dynamic range deliberately: a scene at constant volume/intensity
  has nowhere to escalate; plan quiet stretches specifically so louder
  moments have contrast to work against.
- When generating with AI music/audio tools, provide explicit timing and
  structural instructions (not just mood descriptors), since most audio
  generation platforms need concrete structural guidance to produce usable,
  syncable output.
- Cross-check DIEGETIC_SOUND_INVENTORY against Template 05's location
  materials and Template 09's practical light sources — sounds should be
  physically plausible given the established environment.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. HORROR CINEMATIC TECHNIQUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- THE FALSE QUIET: bring the ambient bed down to near-silence, hold it
  there longer than comfortable, then resolve it with something mundane —
  training the audience that silence doesn't always mean danger, so a
  later true silence-to-threat cut lands harder.
- SUB-AUDIBLE DREAD: introduce a very low-frequency tone, barely
  perceptible, beneath a scene's ambient bed during build sequences,
  creating subconscious unease the audience can't easily identify.
- THE MOTIF WHISPER: play MOTIF_TOKEN at extremely low volume, almost
  buried in the ambient bed, before its first clear appearance — rewards
  close listening/rewatching.
- SOUND WITHOUT SOURCE: introduce a specific diegetic sound with no visible
  or logical source in-frame, left unresolved, as an inexpensive and
  highly effective unease generator.
- THE DELAYED STINGER: place a sound accent slightly after the visual
  moment it's associated with, rather than in perfect sync, creating a
  subtle sense of wrongness or lag.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. CAMERA & LIGHTING GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Sync stinger timing precisely to Template 07's cut/hold timing — a
  stinger landing on a hard cut has a different effect than one landing
  mid-hold on a static shot.
- Match FREQUENCY_TEXTURE_CHARACTER to the scene's established
  Cinematography (Template 08) — high-stylization visual sequences often
  pair with more processed/synthetic sound design, naturalistic visual
  sequences with more grounded, recorded-feeling sound design.
- Silence cues often pair most effectively with locked-off static camera
  work (Template 07) — combining visual and audio stillness compounds the
  effect more than pairing silence with camera movement.

  faintly underneath the silence from beat 4 onward, rising slightly by
  the final beat

  from 0:00-0:10; the three-beat motif hum plays once, quietly, at 0:04
STINGER_HIT_TIMING: No traditional stinger; instead, the low sustained
  tone begins precisely when the bulb flickers at 0:10
MOTIF_TOKEN: The three-beat low communal hum (established in Template 01),
  played once, quiet and distant, not attention-grabbing

  silent to only slightly less silent) — no loud moment at all in this
  scene, reserving louder dynamics for later scenes
NEGATIVE_TRAITS: No jump-scare stinger, no swelling orchestral tension cue,
  no obvious "scary music" signaling
