────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. TITLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HORROR CAMERA MASTER
Category: Cinematographic Technical Specification
Compatible Engines: Sora, Veo, Kling, Runway, Pika, Luma, Hailuo (all
support natural-language camera direction to varying degrees of fidelity)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Provides a comprehensive, reusable reference system for specifying shot
type, camera angle, movement, and lens choice with the precise psychological
effect each produces in horror. This template functions as a LOOKUP AND
COMPOSITION SYSTEM rather than a single fill-in-the-blank prompt — it lets
a user select the exact technical combination that matches the intended
fear response, then outputs it as ready-to-insert prompt language for
Template 06 scene assembly.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. PROMPT FORMULA
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[INTENDED PSYCHOLOGICAL EFFECT] + [SHOT SCALE SELECTION] + [ANGLE
SELECTION] + [MOVEMENT SELECTION] + [LENS SELECTION] + [FRAMING/COMPOSITION
RULE] + [DURATION AND CUT TIMING] + [PLATFORM-SPECIFIC PHRASING WRAPPER]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. VARIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{INTENDED_EFFECT} — the specific psychological/emotional target (isolation,
  claustrophobia, vulnerability, dread of the unseen, disorientation, false
  security, invasive intimacy)

{SHOT_SCALE} — select and combine as needed:
  - EXTREME WIDE: isolation, smallness against environment
  - WIDE/FULL: establishing wrongness in a space, showing full body
    vulnerability
  - MEDIUM: neutral observation, used to lull before a scale change
  - CLOSE-UP: forced intimacy, discomfort, nowhere to look away
  - EXTREME CLOSE-UP: invasive dread, fragmenting the body/face into
    unfamiliar parts (an eye alone, a hand alone)

{ANGLE} — select:
  - EYE-LEVEL: neutral, used deliberately to make later angle shifts land
  - HIGH ANGLE (looking down): vulnerability, smallness, prey positioning
  - LOW ANGLE (looking up): threat dominance, powerlessness of viewer
  - DUTCH/CANTED: disorientation, wrongness, loss of stable ground
  - OVERHEAD/BIRD'S EYE: clinical detachment, inevitability, god's-eye
    dread

{MOVEMENT} — select:
  - LOCKED-OFF STATIC: stillness as dread, forces the audience to search
    the frame themselves
  - SLOW PUSH-IN: mounting inevitability, discomfort that builds without
    resolution
  - SLOW PULL-BACK: revealing scale or isolation gradually, often revealing
    something was present all along
  - HANDHELD: instability, panic, subjective vulnerability
  - STEADICAM/GLIDING: uncanny smoothness, often used FOR the threat's
    POV to feel inhumanly controlled
  - WHIP PAN: sudden reorientation, jarring discovery
  - RACK FOCUS: redirect attention within a static frame without cutting

{LENS_CHOICE} — select:
  - WIDE ANGLE (14-24mm equivalent): environmental distortion, makes close
    spaces feel warped and wrong, exaggerates depth
  - STANDARD (35-50mm equivalent): neutral, natural human perspective,
    used for grounding scenes before distortion
  - TELEPHOTO (85mm+ equivalent): compression, claustrophobia even in open
    spaces, voyeuristic "being watched" quality
  - SHALLOW DEPTH OF FIELD: isolates subject, blurs threat into
    unrecognizable background presence
  - DEEP FOCUS: everything sharp, nowhere for the threat to hide from
    scrutiny — or nowhere for the viewer to avoid seeing it

{FRAMING_RULE} — select:
  - NEGATIVE SPACE DOMINANT: leave significant empty frame space where a
    threat could emerge, deliberately unbalanced
  - RULE OF THIRDS VIOLATION: center-frame subject placement to create
    unease through compositional wrongness
  - PARTIAL OCCLUSION: foreground objects partially block the frame,
    implying unseen space just out of view
  - MIRROR/REFLECTION FRAMING: doubled imagery for uncanny or reveal effect

{CUT_TIMING} — hold duration relative to expected editing rhythm (held
  2-3x longer than a "normal" scene for dread, or cut unnaturally fast for
  disorientation)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Match camera choice to INTENDED_EFFECT deliberately — never select
  camera language for visual novelty alone; every choice should trace back
  to a specific psychological target.
- Reserve extreme techniques (dutch angle, whip pan, extreme close-up) for
  genuine peak moments — overuse causes audience desensitization and
  reads as stylistic excess rather than earned intensity.
- Establish a "neutral baseline" (eye-level, standard lens, steady
  movement) early in a sequence so that deviations register as
  deliberate wrongness rather than the film's default visual language.
- When writing natural-language prompts for video generation platforms,
  translate technical terms into descriptive action/effect language, since
  most current models respond better to descriptive intent phrasing than
  to raw technical jargon (e.g., "the camera creeps slowly closer" often
  outperforms "slow dolly-in, 50mm" alone — consider including both).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. BEST PRACTICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Build a per-project "camera language bible" early — decide which
  angle/movement combinations are reserved exclusively for the antagonist's
  POV or presence, and never use that combination for any other purpose in
  the same project, so the audience unconsciously learns to fear it.
- Vary shot scale across a sequence rather than holding one scale for an
  entire scene — a scene entirely in close-up loses the contrast that
  makes close-ups intense.
- Pair slow push-ins with locked-off framing rather than handheld
  instability when the goal is inevitability rather than panic — mixing
  these movement philosophies muddies the intended effect.
- Test camera direction language on the actual target platform before
  finalizing a sequence — natural-language camera fidelity varies
  significantly between Sora, Veo, Kling, Runway, Pika, Luma, and Hailuo,
  and what renders correctly on one may need rephrasing for another.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. HORROR CINEMATIC TECHNIQUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- THE CREATURE-CAM SIGNATURE: reserve one specific movement/lens
  combination exclusively for shots implying the antagonist's POV (e.g.,
  gliding steadicam + telephoto compression), training the audience to
  recognize danger from camera language alone before anything is shown.
- THE UNCUT HOLD: hold a static, seemingly empty frame significantly
  longer than narratively "necessary," forcing the audience to actively
  search the image for a threat that may or may not appear.
- FALSE POV MISDIRECTION: use a subjective handheld shot that the audience
  assumes belongs to the protagonist, then reveal it belongs to something
  else.
- THE SLOW REVEAL PULL-BACK: begin tightly framed on a mundane detail and
  pull back slowly to reveal the surrounding wrongness only at the very
  end of the movement.
- COMPOSITIONAL WRONGNESS: violate the rule of thirds specifically at
  moments of dawning dread — dead-center framing reads as clinical and
  inescapable in a way off-center framing does not.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. CAMERA & LIGHTING GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
This entire template IS the camera guide; for lighting pairing
recommendations, always cross-reference Template 09 using the
RECOMMENDED_PAIRING_NOTE output field. General pairing principles:
- Low angle + hard underlight = maximum threat dominance
- High angle + soft overhead light = maximum vulnerability/exposure
- Telephoto compression + practical single-source light = voyeuristic
  claustrophobia
- Wide lens distortion + even ambient light = environmental wrongness
  without relying on shadow

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
11. SOUND & ATMOSPHERE GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Static/locked-off shots pair strongly with near-silence or single
  sustained tones (Template 13) — movement in the soundscape compensates
  for stillness in the frame.
- Handheld/unstable camera pairs with layered, irregular ambient sound to
  reinforce disorientation.
- Slow push-ins often pair with a single rising sustained tone timed to
  match the movement's duration exactly, reinforcing the inevitability
  effect at the audio level.

12. EXAMPLE INPUT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INTENDED_EFFECT: Mounting inevitability and helplessness as the protagonist
  realizes she cannot look away from the ledger
SHOT_SCALE: Medium, transitioning to close-up
ANGLE: Eye-level, static
MOVEMENT: Slow push-in, imperceptibly slow, beginning only in the final
  2 seconds of the shot
LENS_CHOICE: Standard (40mm equivalent), shallow depth of field isolating
  her face against the blurred shelves
FRAMING_RULE: Center-frame, rule-of-thirds violation, deliberate
CUT_TIMING: Held 3x longer than the scene's average shot length

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
13. EXAMPLE OUTPUT (Condensed)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION SUMMARY: Medium-to-close-up, eye-level static
holding into an imperceptibly slow push-in, 40mm equivalent with shallow
depth of field, center-frame composition, held roughly 3x longer than
surrounding shots.

PSYCHOLOGICAL RATIONALE: The eye-level static start denies any visual
"escape" through angle drama, forcing the dread to build purely through
duration and the barely-perceptible push. Center-frame placement (a
deliberate rule-of-thirds violation) makes her feel clinically trapped
rather than naturally observed, while the shallow depth of field blurring
the ledger shelves behind her keeps the true scale of the wrongness just
out of legible focus.

PLATFORM-READY CAMERA DIRECTION LANGUAGE:
- Natural-language: "The camera holds perfectly still at eye level,
  centered on her face, the shelves behind her softly out of focus. Only
  in the final moments does it begin an almost imperceptible creep
  closer, as if drawn in against its own stillness."
- Traditional notation: "MS→CU, EYE-LEVEL STATIC → SLOW PUSH (final 2s
  only), 40mm, shallow DOF, dead-center framing, held 3x average shot
  length"

RECOMMENDED PAIRING NOTE: Pair with Template 09's single warm practical
bulb setup (low-key, minimal fill) and Template 13's single rising
sustained tone timed to begin exactly when the push-in begins.
