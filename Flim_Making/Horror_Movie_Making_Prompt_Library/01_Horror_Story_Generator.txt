1. TITLE

HORROR STORY GENERATOR

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
This template generates a complete, structurally sound horror narrative —
premise, plot architecture, character arcs, thematic core, and ending — that
functions as the root document for every other template in this library
(screenplay, scene, dialogue, VFX, etc.). It is built to produce ORIGINAL
horror fiction that avoids clichés through forced specificity: every variable
demands a concrete choice instead of a generic placeholder, so the AI cannot
default to "haunted house + jump scares." Use this first, before any visual
or screenplay template, so every downstream file inherits a consistent story
world, tone, and internal logic.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. PROMPT FORMULA
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[ROLE ANCHOR] + [SUBGENRE + INFLUENCE STACK] + [CORE FEAR MECHANIC] +
[PROTAGONIST + FLAW] + [ANTAGONIST/THREAT LOGIC] + [SETTING + ISOLATION
VECTOR] + [ESCALATION LADDER: 5 BEATS] + [THEMATIC SPINE] + [ENDING TYPE] +
[TONE CALIBRATION] + [OUTPUT FORMAT LOCK]

Formula logic: Horror fails when the fear is abstract. This formula forces
concreteness at every stage — a "core fear mechanic" instead of "scary
things," an "isolation vector" instead of "characters are alone," and an
"escalation ladder" that prevents flat pacing where every scene has the same
intensity.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. VARIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{SUBGENRE} — psychological, supernatural, slasher, cosmic/Lovecraftian, folk
  horror, body horror, found-footage, gothic, survival horror, techno-horror
{INFLUENCE_STACK} — 2-3 named reference points blended (e.g., "Hereditary x
  The Vanishing x Midsommar's daylight dread")
{CORE_FEAR_MECHANIC} — the specific psychological trigger being exploited
  (loss of bodily autonomy, uncertainty of perception, complicity/guilt,
  inevitability, the uncanny familiar, isolation, loss of control over time)
{PROTAGONIST_NAME} — name
{PROTAGONIST_FLAW} — a specific character flaw that the horror will exploit
  (denial, guilt over a past death, need for control, inability to trust)
{ANTAGONIST_TYPE} — entity / human / environment / concept / self
{ANTAGONIST_LOGIC} — the internal rules governing the threat (what it wants,
  what limits it, what it cannot do — rules create dread; ruleless threats
  create confusion)
{SETTING} — specific physical location, era, and cultural context
{ISOLATION_VECTOR} — the specific mechanism removing escape/help (geographic,
  social, technological, psychological, temporal)
{ESCALATION_BEATS} — 5 numbered turning points from normalcy to climax
{THEMATIC_SPINE} — the human truth the horror is actually about (grief,
  colonialism, addiction, motherhood, generational trauma, etc.)
{ENDING_TYPE} — tragic / ambiguous / Pyrrhic victory / total defeat / cyclical
{TONE} — restrained/slow-burn vs. visceral/maximalist; 1-10 gore scale;
  1-10 ambiguity scale
{RUNTIME_TARGET} — short film / feature-length treatment / limited series
{OUTPUT_LENGTH} — word count or word range

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. INPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
You are a horror story architect trained in the tradition of {INFLUENCE_STACK}.

Generate a {SUBGENRE} horror story with the following locked parameters:

CORE FEAR MECHANIC: {CORE_FEAR_MECHANIC}
PROTAGONIST: {PROTAGONIST_NAME}, whose defining flaw is {PROTAGONIST_FLAW}
ANTAGONIST: A {ANTAGONIST_TYPE} governed by this internal logic:
{ANTAGONIST_LOGIC}
SETTING: {SETTING}
ISOLATION VECTOR: {ISOLATION_VECTOR}
THEMATIC SPINE: The story must be secretly about {THEMATIC_SPINE}
ENDING TYPE: {ENDING_TYPE}
TONE: {TONE}
TARGET LENGTH: {OUTPUT_LENGTH}

Structure the narrative across this escalation ladder — do not skip beats or
compress two beats into one:
{ESCALATION_BEATS}

Constraints:
- Do not use these overused tropes unless subverted: cat jump scares, "it was
  all a dream," generic evil basement, monster fully revealed in act one.
- The antagonist's rules (from ANTAGONIST_LOGIC) must be internally
  consistent for the entire story — no rule may be broken without narrative
  justification established earlier.
- Every scene must either escalate dread, reveal character, or advance the
  ANTAGONIST_LOGIC. Cut anything that does none of the three.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. OUTPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Return the story in this exact structure:

1. LOGLINE (1-2 sentences)
2. THEMATIC STATEMENT (1 sentence — what this story is really about)
3. ANTAGONIST RULESET (bulleted — the fixed internal logic of the threat)
4. FULL NARRATIVE (organized under the 5 escalation beats as headers)
5. ENDING (matching {ENDING_TYPE}, with a final unsettling image as the
   closing line)
6. THEME ECHO (1 paragraph explaining how the ending reflects the thematic
   spine)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Write horror through IMPLICATION before REVELATION. The reader's
  imagination is scarier than any description — describe effects and
  reactions before causes.
- Establish false safety before every major scare (a "breath" beat) so the
  scare has contrast to work against.
- Ground the impossible in mundane sensory detail (smell, texture,
  temperature) — specificity sells the unreal.
- Withhold full monster/antagonist description as long as possible;
  fragment the reveal across multiple beats (a sound, then a shadow, then a
  partial glimpse, then full exposure only near climax if at all).
- Never explain the antagonist's motive in clinical, expository dialogue —
  motive should be inferred through action and environment, not stated.
- Track the protagonist's psychological state as a visible arc: competence →
  doubt → dread → desperation → transformation/defeat.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. BEST PRACTICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- One core fear mechanic per story. Diluting fear across multiple unrelated
  threats weakens all of them — subplots should feed the same fear, not
  compete with it.
- Silence and stillness are tools — call out explicit "quiet beats" in
  escalation so the AI doesn't pace everything at the same volume.
- Give the antagonist a LIMIT. Omnipotent threats remove stakes; audiences
  fear things that can almost, but not quite, be escaped.
- Foreshadow the ending's final image somewhere in Act 1, disguised as
  something innocuous.
- Avoid narrating fear ("she was terrified") — show physiological and
  behavioral symptoms instead (shallow breath, checking locks twice,
  flinching at reflections).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. HORROR CINEMATIC TECHNIQUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- THE THREAT LADDER: sound → shadow/peripheral glimpse → object evidence →
  partial reveal → full confrontation. Never front-load.
- WRONGNESS BEFORE DANGER: establish that something is subtly "off" (a
  repeated detail slightly changed) before anything is overtly threatening.
- THE CALM-BEFORE beat: insert one full page/scene of ordinary life
  immediately before each major escalation to maximize contrast.
- DELAYED CONSEQUENCE: show the result of an act before showing the act
  itself, forcing the reader to reconstruct horror retroactively.
- UNRELIABLE PERCEPTION: seed 1-2 moments where the protagonist's senses are
  provably wrong, so the reader can never fully trust the narration.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. CAMERA & LIGHTING GUIDE (Narrative-to-Visual Bridge)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Even in prose form, write scenes with an implicit "camera" in mind so the
story translates cleanly into Template 07 (Camera Master) and Template 09
(Lighting):
- Mark each escalation beat with an implied shot scale (wide/isolating,
  medium/observational, close/invasive) in a bracketed note.
- Note implied light sources per key scene (practical only, moonlight,
  total darkness + flashlight) so lighting logic is established at the story
  level and stays consistent downstream.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
11. SOUND & ATMOSPHERE GUIDE (Narrative-to-Audio Bridge)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Identify one recurring diegetic sound motif tied to the antagonist (a
  specific sound that always precedes it) for use in Template 13.
- Note where TRUE SILENCE should occur — silence is a sound design choice,
  not an absence of one, and should be flagged explicitly for downstream
  templates.

12. EXAMPLE INPUT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SUBGENRE: Folk horror
INFLUENCE_STACK: Midsommar x The Wicker Man x Hereditary's inherited dread
CORE_FEAR_MECHANIC: Complicity — realizing you are not the protagonist of
  your own rescue, but a chosen participant
PROTAGONIST_NAME: Ines Calder
PROTAGONIST_FLAW: Pathological need to belong after a lifetime of exclusion
ANTAGONIST_TYPE: A rural community bound to a harvest entity
ANTAGONIST_LOGIC: The entity cannot act directly; it can only "invite," and
  every invitation must be voluntarily accepted three times before it takes
  effect — deception is allowed, force is not
SETTING: An agricultural commune in rural Northern England, present day,
  during a week-long "welcome festival"
ISOLATION_VECTOR: Confiscated phones under the guise of "digital detox
  tradition," combined with Ines's own reluctance to leave because she feels
  wanted for the first time
THEMATIC_SPINE: The horror of belonging bought at the cost of self-erasure
ENDING_TYPE: Ambiguous — victory that is indistinguishable from surrender
TONE: Slow-burn, restrained, gore scale 3/10, ambiguity scale 9/10
RUNTIME_TARGET: Short film treatment
OUTPUT_LENGTH: 1200-1500 words

13. EXAMPLE OUTPUT (Condensed Illustrative Excerpt)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LOGLINE: A lonely woman finally finds the community she's always wanted —
and slowly realizes every kindness they've shown her is the second of three
invitations she doesn't know she's accepting.

THEMATIC STATEMENT: True belonging cannot be purchased with the erasure of
the self that wanted to belong.

ANTAGONIST RULESET:
- The entity cannot compel; it can only offer, three times, in escalating
  intimacy
- Each acceptance is binding regardless of whether the offer was understood
- The commune members are not villains — they are also accepted, and believe
  they are saving Ines from a lonelier fate

[BEAT 1 — NORMALCY]
[Implied shot: wide, establishing — golden wheat fields, Ines arriving alone]
Ines had rehearsed the drive so many times she almost missed the turn when
it finally appeared — a gap in the hedgerow with no sign, exactly as the
invitation had promised...

[Recurring sound motif for Template 13: a low communal hum, always three
beats long, that precedes each "offer"]

[Full narrative would continue through all 5 escalation beats to the
ambiguous ending, following the same technique density shown above.]

THEME ECHO: The final image — Ines humming the three-beat tone to a new
arrival at the hedgerow gap — completes her transformation from excluded
outsider to unknowing instrument of the same offer once made to her,
confirming that belonging here has always meant becoming a door for someone
else's disappearance.

14. ADVANCED VERSION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
For multi-part or feature-length development, extend the formula with:

{SUBPLOT_MIRROR} — a secondary character whose smaller arc mirrors/foreshadows
  the protagonist's fate at a lower stakes level
{RED_HERRING_LOGIC} — one deliberate false threat that the story allows the
  audience to suspect, then subverts, to weaponize misdirection
{MULTI_POV_SPLIT} — if using multiple viewpoints, specify which beats are
  seen through which character and why the choice matters
{CALLBACK_ARCHITECTURE} — 3 specific objects/phrases/images planted in Act 1
  that must reappear transformed by the climax
{SEQUEL_HOOK} (optional) — an unresolved thread suggesting the
  ANTAGONIST_LOGIC extends beyond this story's frame

ADVANCED OUTPUT ADDITION: Request a parallel "ANTAGONIST POV FRAGMENT" — a
single scene (200-300 words) told from the threat's perspective or
adjacent to it, revealing the ANTAGONIST_LOGIC in action without breaking
the withheld-reveal pacing of the main narrative. This fragment is for
writer's-room use only and should be flagged as NOT part of the final cut
unless the ENDING_TYPE specifically calls for a POV break.

Chain this Advanced Version output directly into Template 02 (Horror
Screenplay Generator) as its {SOURCE_MATERIAL} input for seamless
story-to-script conversion.
