────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. TITLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BLOOD, FOG, SMOKE & WEATHER EFFECTS
Category: Elemental & Particulate Effects Design
Compatible Engines: Sora, Veo, Kling, Runway, Pika, Luma, Hailuo (video
generation) + GPT/Claude/Gemini/Grok/DeepSeek (effects reasoning)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Provides precise technical specification for the four elemental effect
categories that most frequently render as generic or physically
unconvincing in AI-generated horror: blood behavior, fog/mist density and
movement, smoke behavior, and weather intensity. Unlike Template 10
(overall atmospheric world-state), this template drills into the specific
PHYSICS and MATERIAL BEHAVIOR of each element so it renders with
believable weight, viscosity, and motion rather than as a flat visual
overlay.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. PROMPT FORMULA
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[ELEMENT TYPE] + [PHYSICAL BEHAVIOR SPECIFICATION] + [INTERACTION WITH
LIGHT/SURFACE] + [MOTION AND TIMING LOGIC] + [INTENSITY CALIBRATION] +
[STORY-MOTIVATED CAUSE] + [CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN] + [PLATFORM-SPECIFIC
SYNTAX WRAPPER]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. VARIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{ELEMENT_TYPE} — blood / fog-mist / smoke / rain / snow / wind-driven
  debris (select one per prompt instance; combine multiple instances for
  scenes requiring layered elements)

FOR BLOOD:
{BLOOD_VISCOSITY_STAGE} — fresh (bright, high-flow, reflective) vs. oxidized
  (darkening, tackier, less reflective) vs. dried (matte, dark, flaking)
{BLOOD_FLOW_PHYSICS} — how it moves given the specific surface/gravity
  context (pooling rate on a given material, drip pattern, spatter
  distribution logic)
{BLOOD_LIGHT_INTERACTION} — reflectivity and color shift under the
  established lighting (Template 09) — fresh blood reflects specularly,
  oxidized blood absorbs more light

FOR FOG/MIST:
{FOG_DENSITY_PROFILE} — thin/atmospheric vs. moderate/obscuring vs. dense/
  near-opaque, with visibility distance specified in approximate meters
{FOG_MOVEMENT_LOGIC} — how it moves relative to established wind conditions
  (Template 10) — including any deliberate WRONGNESS (moving against wind)
{FOG_LIGHT_INTERACTION} — how it scatters/diffuses specific light sources
  (a flashlight beam becoming visible as a cone, headlights creating a wall
  of glare)

FOR SMOKE:
{SMOKE_SOURCE_LOGIC} — the specific combustion/origin source and how that
  determines color, density, and rise behavior
{SMOKE_BEHAVIOR} — rise rate, dispersal pattern, and interaction with air
  currents established in the scene

FOR WEATHER (RAIN/SNOW/WIND):
{PRECIPITATION_INTENSITY} — specific intensity level with real-world
  reference points (light drizzle vs. heavy downpour, with associated
  visual effects like puddle formation rate, sound implications)
{WIND_INTENSITY_AND_DIRECTION} — specific and consistent across all
  affected elements in a scene (hair, clothing, environmental objects)

UNIVERSAL:
{STORY_MOTIVATED_CAUSE} — why this element is present now (never
  unmotivated atmospheric decoration)
{CONSISTENCY_LOCK_TOKEN} — reusable phrase for this specific effect state
{NEGATIVE_TRAITS} — exclude generic defaults (no cartoonish bright-red
  blood with no viscosity logic, no generic "fog machine" flat white fog,
  no rain that doesn't affect visible surfaces/characters)

6. OUTPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. EFFECT PHYSICS SUMMARY (paragraph describing the specific physical
   behavior and why it was calibrated this way)
2. CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN (isolated, bolded)
3. LIGHT/SURFACE INTERACTION NOTE (how this element should render given

4. PLATFORM-READY GENERATION PROMPT (video-model natural-language,

   must not contradict across scenes without a specified in-story reason)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Every elemental effect needs a real-world physical reference point —
  describe blood, fog, or rain with the same precision a practical effects
  supervisor would use, since AI video models produce noticeably more
  convincing results when given specific physical behavior rather than
  vague mood descriptors.
- Blood viscosity stage must match time-since-injury logic established in
  Template 14 — fresh, bright, high-flow blood immediately after an injury
  should oxidize and darken believably if the scene continues or a later
  scene revisits the same wound.
- Fog and smoke must respect the wind conditions established in Template
  10 unless a deliberate WRONGNESS signal is intended — inconsistent
  particulate movement is one of the most common tells of ungrounded AI
  generation.
- Calibrate GORE_CALIBRATION-adjacent blood effects (viscosity, spatter
  extent) against Template 14's overall gore calibration and Template 02's
  rating target for tonal consistency.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. BEST PRACTICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Build a short reference library of real-world physical behavior notes
  per element type (how blood pools on wood vs. concrete vs. fabric; how
  fog behaves near ground level vs. at height) to reuse across a project
  for consistency.
- Layer elements deliberately when combining multiple effects in one scene
  (rain plus wind plus a practical light source) — specify their
  interaction explicitly rather than listing them as independent,
  unconnected elements.
- Reserve maximum-intensity versions of any element (densest fog, heaviest
  downpour, most extensive blood) for the story's highest-stakes moments,
  building intensity progressively rather than starting at maximum.
- Validate that element behavior renders consistently on your specific
  target platform with a short test generation, since fluid/particulate
  simulation quality varies meaningfully between Sora, Veo, Kling, Runway,
  Pika, Luma, and Hailuo.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. HORROR CINEMATIC TECHNIQUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- BLOOD AS TIMELINE: use blood's viscosity/oxidation stage as a silent,
  readable indicator of how much time has passed since an off-screen
  event, letting the audience infer story information without dialogue.
- FOG AS REVEAL CONTROL: use fog density specifically to control exactly
  how much of a creature/threat (Template 04's reveal map) is legible at
  a given moment, adjusting density progressively as the reveal
  progresses.
- WEATHER AS ESCALATION CLOCK: intensify weather conditions (wind picking
  up, rain increasing) in sync with the story's escalation ladder
  (Template 01), giving the environment itself a sense of building toward
  something.
- THE WRONG-DIRECTION ELEMENT: have smoke or fog drift in a direction that
  contradicts all other visible wind indicators in frame, reserved for a
  specific, deliberate wrongness beat rather than general use.
- BLOOD IN NEGATIVE SPACE: let blood spatter or pooling extend into frame
  areas the audience wasn't tracking, discovered rather than centered,
  for a more unsettling reveal than direct display.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. CAMERA & LIGHTING GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Blood's reflectivity (fresh vs. oxidized) interacts directly with
  Template 09's light source — specify explicitly how the established key
  light should catch or fail to catch the blood's surface at its current
  viscosity stage.
- Fog/smoke density affects effective depth of field and shot legibility
  — coordinate with Template 07's lens choices, since dense atmospheric
  elements naturally compress perceived depth and may require adjusted
  shot scale to maintain composition clarity.
- Rain/wind effects on camera (implied lens moisture, motion blur on fast-
  moving precipitation) should be noted if the intended aesthetic calls
  for handheld, in-the-elements camera work per Template 07.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
11. SOUND & ATMOSPHERE GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Each element type carries a specific, physically accurate sound
  signature (blood's wet sound on specific surfaces, fog's characteristic
  near-silence, rain's intensity-dependent sound on specific materials,
  wind's pitch varying with speed and obstruction) — specify these for
  Template 13 continuity.
- Weather intensity should scale its sound presence proportionally —
  don't pair visually heavy rain with a quiet sound bed or vice versa,
  unless the mismatch is a deliberate wrongness signal.
- Cross-reference Template 10's atmospheric sound notes to ensure this
  template's elemental effects don't contradict the broader environmental
  soundscape already established.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
12. EXAMPLE INPUT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ELEMENT_TYPE: Fog/mist
FOG_DENSITY_PROFILE: Dense, near-opaque; visibility reduced to
  approximately 15-20 meters
FOG_MOVEMENT_LOGIC: Completely still, unmoving, despite no windbreak that
  would naturally explain the total absence of drift (deliberate
  wrongness, established in Template 10's example)
FOG_LIGHT_INTERACTION: A handheld flashlight beam becomes clearly visible
  as a solid-seeming cone of light within the fog; distant sources (the
  exchange building's silhouette) are reduced to soft, indistinct shapes
STORY_MOTIVATED_CAUSE: Fourth consecutive day of unseasonal fog
  (established atmospheric wrongness from Template 10), correlating with
  the story's inciting event
NEGATIVE_TRAITS: No flat, uniformly white "fog machine" look; no fog that
  moves inconsistently shot-to-shot

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
13. EXAMPLE OUTPUT (Condensed)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
EFFECT PHYSICS SUMMARY: A dense, unnaturally still fog reduces visibility
to roughly fifteen meters, thick enough to turn a flashlight beam into a
visible, solid-seeming cone, while remaining completely motionless despite
having no windbreak that would explain such total stillness.

CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN:
"Dense unmoving fog, ~15-20m visibility, flashlight beam visible as solid
cone, distant shapes reduced to soft silhouette, no drift despite open
terrain"

LIGHT/SURFACE INTERACTION NOTE: Per Template 09's established practical
light sources, any flashlight or bulb light passing through this fog
density should scatter visibly, creating a defined light cone rather than
a clean beam — this scattering effect should intensify with fog density
and remain consistent across all scenes sharing this atmospheric state.

PLATFORM-READY GENERATION PROMPT: "Thick, unmoving fog reduces visibility
to about fifteen meters in every direction, completely still despite the
open, windbreak-free terrain. A flashlight beam cuts through it as a
visible, solid cone of scattered light. The building ahead is reduced to
a soft, indistinct grey shape."

CONTINUITY CHECKLIST: Fog density and total stillness must remain
identical across every scene set within the established "day four" window
✓. Any scene depicting days 1-3 should show visibly different, lighter,
moving fog for contrast per Template 10 ✓.

14. ADVANCED VERSION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Add for complex multi-element or escalating-severity sequences:

{MULTI_ELEMENT_LAYERING_MAP} — for scenes combining multiple elements
  (blood plus rain, fog plus wind-driven debris), specify their interaction
  physics explicitly (rain diluting/spreading blood, wind affecting fog
  density unevenly)
{ESCALATION_INTENSITY_CURVE} — a full-story chart of how a given element
  (weather severity, blood extent) should intensify across the escalation
  ladder from Template 01
{PRACTICAL_EFFECTS_MATERIAL_REFERENCE} — real-world practical effects
  material equivalents (corn syrup blood formulations, glycerin fog
  density equivalents) for hybrid AI/practical productions
{CROSS_SCENE_ELEMENT_CONTINUITY_LOG} — a tracking document ensuring
  element states (a specific pool of blood, a specific fog bank) remain
  positionally and physically consistent if a location is revisited later
  in the story

ADVANCED OUTPUT ADDITION: Request a "PHYSICAL PLAUSIBILITY AUDIT" — a
verification pass confirming the described element behavior is consistent
with real-world physics at the stated intensity, flagging any element
description that would read as physically implausible even accounting for
supernatural story elements (unless WRONGNESS is explicitly intended and
flagged).
