────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. TITLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FEAR, EMOTION & ACTING
Category: Performance Direction
Compatible Engines: Sora, Veo, Kling, Runway, Pika, Luma, Hailuo (video
generation, for performance rendering) + GPT/Claude/Gemini/Grok/DeepSeek
(performance reasoning and direction notes)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Directs believable, PHYSIOLOGICALLY GROUNDED fear performance rather than
generic "scared face" defaults. Real fear reads through specific,
observable physical behavior — most AI-generated horror performance fails
because it defaults to exaggerated screaming or blank generic distress.
This template forces a physiology-first approach: what does the body
actually do under this specific type and intensity of fear, moment by
moment.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. PROMPT FORMULA
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[FEAR TYPE + INTENSITY LEVEL] + [PHYSIOLOGICAL RESPONSE MAP] +
[MICRO-EXPRESSION SEQUENCE] + [BODY LANGUAGE PROGRESSION] + [BREATH AND
VOCAL CONTROL STATE] + [RESTRAINT VS. RELEASE CALIBRATION] + [CHARACTER-
SPECIFIC FEAR SIGNATURE] + [PLATFORM-SPECIFIC SYNTAX WRAPPER]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. VARIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{FEAR_TYPE} — specific fear category (acute startle, mounting dread,
  paralytic freeze, controlled suppression/masking, creeping realization,
  panic/fight-or-flight activation, existential/ontological dread)
{INTENSITY_LEVEL} — 1-10 scale, with explicit note that horror performance
  is almost always more effective below a 7 than above it — restraint
  reads as more real
{PHYSIOLOGICAL_RESPONSE_MAP} — specific bodily response for THIS fear type
  at THIS intensity (pupil dilation, shallow rapid breathing, muscle
  tension location, skin pallor, trembling location and degree)
{MICRO_EXPRESSION_SEQUENCE} — 2-4 sequential facial micro-expressions in
  order (a flicker of confusion before fear registers, a brief attempt to
  suppress before it breaks through)
{BODY_LANGUAGE_PROGRESSION} — how posture/positioning changes across the
  fear beat (from open/normal to closed/defensive to frozen or fleeing)
{BREATH_AND_VOCAL_STATE} — breathing pattern and vocal control (held
  breath, broken speech patterns, voice pitch change, or notably the
  ABSENCE of vocalization when silence would be more realistic)
{RESTRAINT_VS_RELEASE} — whether this character suppresses or expresses
  fear openly, and why (tied to Template 03's character psychology)
{CHARACTER_FEAR_SIGNATURE} — a specific, personal, recurring way THIS
  character physically manifests fear (inherited from Template 03's
  PSYCHOLOGICAL_TELL), distinguishing them from generic fear performance
{SCENE_CONTEXT} — what specifically is happening to trigger this fear
  response, pulled from Template 06
{NEGATIVE_TRAITS} — exclude generic/overused performance defaults (no
  generic wide-eyed scream-face default, no cartoonish freeze-frame
  terror, no melodramatic overacting unless the character/tone specifically
  calls for it)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. INPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Direct a physiologically grounded fear performance for this scene.

FEAR TYPE: {FEAR_TYPE}
INTENSITY (1-10): {INTENSITY_LEVEL}
PHYSIOLOGICAL RESPONSE: {PHYSIOLOGICAL_RESPONSE_MAP}
MICRO-EXPRESSION SEQUENCE: {MICRO_EXPRESSION_SEQUENCE}
BODY LANGUAGE PROGRESSION: {BODY_LANGUAGE_PROGRESSION}
BREATH/VOCAL STATE: {BREATH_AND_VOCAL_STATE}
RESTRAINT VS. RELEASE: {RESTRAINT_VS_RELEASE}
CHARACTER FEAR SIGNATURE: {CHARACTER_FEAR_SIGNATURE}
SCENE CONTEXT: {SCENE_CONTEXT}
EXCLUDE: {NEGATIVE_TRAITS}

Sequence the performance as a time-ordered progression (not a static
description) matching the scene's duration, ensuring the CHARACTER_FEAR_
SIGNATURE appears at the appropriate escalation point rather than being a
constant background trait.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. OUTPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. PERFORMANCE DIRECTION SUMMARY (one paragraph: the emotional arc of this
   specific fear beat)
2. TIME-SEQUENCED PERFORMANCE BREAKDOWN (numbered micro-beats matching
   scene duration, each describing physical/vocal state at that moment)
3. PLATFORM-READY PERFORMANCE PROMPT (natural-language description
   suitable for insertion into Template 06's ACTION_SEQUENCE, written as
   observable physical behavior only — no internal-state language)
4. ACTOR/ANIMATION REFERENCE NOTE (a brief note translatable to human
   actor direction, useful if AI generation is later combined with or
   replaced by practical performance)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Describe fear through OBSERVABLE PHYSICAL BEHAVIOR exclusively, never
  through internal-state adjectives — "her hand tightens on the door
  frame" renders in video generation; "she feels a wave of terror" does
  not translate to any visual output.
- Default toward restraint. Real fear in life-threatening situations is
  often quiet, controlled, and physically small (frozen stillness,
  suppressed breathing) rather than loud and expansive — reserve high-
  intensity release for genuine peak moments only.
- Sequence micro-expressions realistically: fear rarely appears instantly
  at full intensity in real physiology — build in a brief prior beat
  (confusion, denial, a false composure) before the fear response fully
  registers.
- Always tie performance back to CHARACTER_FEAR_SIGNATURE — generic fear
  performance disconnected from the specific character established in
  Template 03 reads as flat regardless of technical execution quality.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. BEST PRACTICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Keep INTENSITY_LEVEL calibrated relative to the scene's place in the
  overall story arc — a level-8 performance early in the story leaves no
  room to escalate further at the actual climax.
- Vary RESTRAINT_VS_RELEASE across different characters in an ensemble
  scene — not everyone should express fear the same way; contrast between
  characters' fear responses adds realism and can itself become a source
  of dramatic tension.
- Test physiological descriptions against real human fear response
  research rather than genre-convention defaults — genuine physiological
  grounding consistently outperforms "movie version" fear behavior.
- Build a short, reusable "fear vocabulary" per major character early
  (their specific tells, their specific restraint pattern) and reuse it
  consistently rather than reinventing performance language per scene.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. HORROR CINEMATIC TECHNIQUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- THE DELAYED REGISTER: show the triggering stimulus, then a beat of the
  character NOT yet reacting (processing lag), before the fear response
  begins — this delay is often more unsettling than an instant reaction.
- CONTROLLED UNRAVELING: for suppression-type characters, show the
  suppression mechanism visibly failing in small, specific increments
  rather than collapsing all at once.
- THE WRONG CALM: occasionally direct a character to respond to something
  objectively terrifying with unnervingly little visible fear, signaling
  to the audience that something is wrong with the character themselves,
  not just the situation.
- STILLNESS AS TERROR: for freeze-type fear responses, hold the performance
  in near-total physical stillness longer than feels natural, using only
  breath and eye movement to carry the entire beat.
- THE FEAR ECHO: have a character's fear signature from an earlier, lower-
  stakes scene reappear at higher intensity during the climax, creating a
  performance callback that rewards attentive viewers.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. CAMERA & LIGHTING GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Match shot scale to fear intensity via Template 07: subtle, restrained
  fear performances are often best served by close-up framing that can
  register small physical detail; larger physical fear responses (fleeing,
  collapsing) may need wider framing to show full-body behavior.
- Consider lighting's role in performance legibility (Template 09) — a
  suppressed, restrained performance in heavy shadow may lose critical
  detail; ensure enough key light reaches the performer's face for the
  micro-expression sequence to register.
- For extreme close-up fear beats, coordinate with Template 07's lens
  choice (shallow depth of field) to isolate the performance from
  distracting background detail.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
11. SOUND & ATMOSPHERE GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Breath and vocal control state should be explicitly passed to Template
  13 for accurate foley/vocal sound design — a held breath needs to be
  audible or its absence needs to be a deliberate silence choice.
- Consider whether the character's fear signature has an audible component
  (a specific verbal tic, a habitual sound) that should recur consistently
  across scenes as an audio-performance signature.
- Match ambient sound intensity to performance restraint level — a highly
  restrained, quiet performance is often best served by a correspondingly
  sparse soundscape that doesn't compete for attention.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
12. EXAMPLE INPUT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FEAR_TYPE: Creeping realization (not acute startle)
INTENSITY_LEVEL: 5/10 — restrained, internal, building slowly
PHYSIOLOGICAL_RESPONSE_MAP: Slight pallor increase, breathing shifts from
  normal to shallow and slightly faster, no trembling yet at this
  intensity, slight muscle tension visible in jaw and shoulders
MICRO_EXPRESSION_SEQUENCE: 1) Neutral concentration while reading 2) brief
  confusion/re-checking flicker 3) stillness as recognition lands 4) a
  swallow, suppressed
BODY_LANGUAGE_PROGRESSION: Relaxed kneeling posture → gradual stiffening
  through the spine → complete stillness, hands stop moving entirely
BREATH_AND_VOCAL_STATE: Breathing audible but controlled; one aborted,
  unfinished word ("Okay, that's—") then silence; no further vocalization
RESTRAINT_VS_RELEASE: Full restraint — Ines's established flaw (need to
  belong) makes her suppress visible fear even when alone, from years of
  masking distress to seem agreeable
CHARACTER_FEAR_SIGNATURE: Ines touches the inside of her own wrist lightly
  when suppressing visible distress (established in Template 03)
SCENE_CONTEXT: Discovering her own name repeated across decades in the
  commune ledger (from Template 06's Scene 14)
NEGATIVE_TRAITS: No gasping, no wide-eyed exaggeration, no crying, no
  raised voice

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
13. EXAMPLE OUTPUT (Condensed)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PERFORMANCE DIRECTION SUMMARY: This is fear that arrives as stillness, not
motion — Ines's years of practiced agreeableness mean even a discovery
this destabilizing surfaces as controlled breath and a private, almost
invisible gesture rather than any outward display.

TIME-SEQUENCED PERFORMANCE BREAKDOWN:
0:00-0:02 — Relaxed kneeling posture, neutral concentration, scanning
  the page normally
0:02-0:04 — Slight confusion flicker; re-reads the same line; posture
  begins almost imperceptibly stiffening through the spine
0:04-0:07 — Recognition lands; complete physical stillness; hands stop
  mid-motion on the page; breathing becomes visibly shallower and faster
0:07-0:09 — She touches the inside of her own wrist lightly (fear
  signature); begins to speak — "Okay, that's—" — and stops
0:09-0:12 — Total stillness resumes; one controlled swallow; no further
  movement or sound

PLATFORM-READY PERFORMANCE PROMPT: "She goes still mid-motion, her hands
frozen on the page. Her breathing becomes shallow and quick, though she
makes no sound. She touches the inside of her own wrist lightly. She
starts to speak — 'Okay, that's—' — and stops herself, swallowing once,
her body otherwise completely motionless."

ACTOR/ANIMATION REFERENCE NOTE: Direct as internalized shock, not
outward panic — think held breath and stopped motion rather than gasping;
the wrist-touch gesture should read as small and almost unconscious, not
a deliberate, visible action.

14. ADVANCED VERSION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Add for ensemble scenes or extended psychological arcs:

{MULTI_CHARACTER_CONTRAST_MAP} — for scenes with multiple characters
  present, define how each character's fear response should visibly
  contrast with the others, avoiding uniform group reactions
{FEAR_ARC_ACROSS_RUNTIME} — a full-story chart tracking how a single
  character's fear signature and restraint/release balance should evolve
  from Act 1 through the climax
{TRAUMA_RESPONSE_ACCURACY_NOTE} — for stories involving specific
  psychological trauma responses, a note ensuring the depicted response
  aligns with realistic, respectful physiological/psychological patterns
  rather than dramatized stereotypes
{DIRECTOR_ALT_TAKE_SET} — request 2-3 alternate performance calibrations
  (more restrained / more expressive / different fear type) for the same
  beat, to give production flexibility in final scene assembly

ADVANCED OUTPUT ADDITION: Request a "PHYSIOLOGICAL PLAUSIBILITY CHECK" — a
brief verification that the described response sequence is consistent with
realistic human fear physiology at the stated intensity level, flagging
any beat that reads as exaggerated beyond what the INTENSITY_LEVEL would
realistically produce.
