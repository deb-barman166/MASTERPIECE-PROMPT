────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. TITLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HORROR DIALOGUE GENERATOR
Category: Dialogue & Subtext Writing
Compatible Engines: GPT-4/5, Claude, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, and future LLMs

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Generates horror dialogue built on SUBTEXT and WITHHOLDING rather than
exposition. The most common failure in AI-generated horror dialogue is
characters explaining the plot, their feelings, or the threat directly to
each other. This template enforces the opposite discipline: characters
should almost never say what they mean, and dialogue should do its real
work through what's avoided, interrupted, or said about something else
entirely.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. PROMPT FORMULA
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[CHARACTER VOICE PROFILES] + [SCENE OBJECTIVE + SUBTEXT LAYER] +
[WHAT-IS-NOT-SAID MAP] + [CONVERSATIONAL DEFLECTION PATTERN] + [DIALOGUE
RHYTHM CALIBRATION] + [SILENCE AND INTERRUPTION PLACEMENT] +
[NEGATIVE INSTRUCTION SET] + [OUTPUT FORMAT LOCK]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. VARIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{CHARACTER_VOICE_PROFILES} — per character: speech rhythm, vocabulary
  register, verbal tics, and specifically what topics/words they avoid
  (inherited from Template 02/03)
{SCENE_OBJECTIVE} — what each character actually wants from this
  conversation (stated for the writer, never for the characters to voice
  directly)
{SUBTEXT_LAYER} — what's really being negotiated/feared/hidden beneath the
  surface topic of conversation
{SURFACE_TOPIC} — the ostensible, mundane subject characters are
  "actually" discussing (weather, a chore, a minor logistical detail) while
  the subtext operates beneath it
{WHAT_IS_NOT_SAID} — the specific information/feeling that must never be
  stated directly in this scene, regardless of how tense it gets
{DEFLECTION_PATTERN} — how characters redirect when conversation
  approaches the unsaid thing (changing subject, answering a different
  question than asked, over-literal responses to avoid emotional ones)
{DIALOGUE_RHYTHM} — pace and structure (short clipped exchanges vs. one
  character over-explaining to fill silence vs. long pauses between lines)
{SILENCE_INTERRUPTION_PLACEMENT} — where specifically a character should
  trail off, be cut off, or leave a silence unfilled
{POWER_DYNAMIC} — who has conversational control and how it shifts during
  the scene
{NEGATIVE_TRAITS} — exclude generic horror dialogue defaults (no "did you
  hear that?", no characters narrating obvious danger aloud, no on-the-nose
  emotional statements, no expository monologuing about backstory/rules)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. INPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Write horror dialogue built entirely on subtext and withholding.

CHARACTERS AND VOICE PROFILES: {CHARACTER_VOICE_PROFILES}
SURFACE TOPIC (what they appear to discuss): {SURFACE_TOPIC}
SUBTEXT LAYER (what's really happening): {SUBTEXT_LAYER}
WHAT MUST NEVER BE SAID DIRECTLY: {WHAT_IS_NOT_SAID}
SCENE OBJECTIVES (per character, for writer reference only): {SCENE_OBJECTIVE}
DEFLECTION PATTERN: {DEFLECTION_PATTERN}
DIALOGUE RHYTHM: {DIALOGUE_RHYTHM}
SILENCE/INTERRUPTION PLACEMENT: {SILENCE_INTERRUPTION_PLACEMENT}
POWER DYNAMIC: {POWER_DYNAMIC}
EXCLUDE: {NEGATIVE_TRAITS}

Write the scene so that a reader/viewer who is paying close attention can
infer WHAT_IS_NOT_SAID entirely through subtext, deflection, and behavior —
but the dialogue itself never states it. If a line risks stating the unsaid
thing directly, rewrite it as a deflection or partial statement instead.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. OUTPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. SUBTEXT MAP (a brief writer's-reference summary of what's really
   happening beneath the dialogue, NOT part of the final scene)
2. FULL DIALOGUE SCENE (properly formatted, ready for screenplay
   integration via Template 02)
3. SUBTEXT VERIFICATION (confirms no line accidentally states WHAT_IS_NOT_
   SAID directly; flags any line that risks being too on-the-nose)
4. ALTERNATE LINE OPTIONS (2-3 alternate versions of the scene's single
   most important line, offering different levels of directness for
   creative flexibility)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Characters should discuss the SURFACE_TOPIC with full attention and
  specificity — subtext works because the surface conversation is
  genuinely about something concrete, not a vague placeholder waiting to
  be replaced by the "real" conversation.
- Use DEFLECTION_PATTERN consistently per character — a character who
  deflects through humor should do so every time they approach the unsaid
  thing, not switch deflection styles arbitrarily.
- Withhold explanation of rules, threats, or backstory through dialogue
  wherever possible — if the audience needs this information, prefer
  environmental storytelling (Template 05) or visual implication over a
  character stating it aloud.
- Trust silence. A character's unfinished sentence or an unanswered
  question left hanging is frequently more effective than any line that
  could complete it.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. BEST PRACTICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Read dialogue aloud (or have it read) checking specifically whether any
  line could be cut entirely without losing information the audience
  needs — if so, cut it.
- Vary DEFLECTION_PATTERN across different characters in the same story so
  each character's avoidance style remains distinct and recognizable.
- Keep exchanges shorter than feels natural in early drafts, then only add
  words back where genuinely needed — horror dialogue trends toward
  economy far more than naturalistic conversation does.
- Reserve any moment where a character DOES say the unsaid thing directly
  (if ever) for one specific, high-stakes turning point — breaking the
  pattern only works if the pattern has been consistently established
  first.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. HORROR CINEMATIC TECHNIQUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- THE MUNDANE MASK: have characters discuss something trivial (what to
  make for dinner) while the actual scene tension is about something life-
  threatening, letting the mismatch generate dread through contrast.
- THE UNANSWERED QUESTION: end a scene on a direct question that goes
  unanswered on-screen, forcing the audience to sit with the same
  uncertainty as the character who asked it.
- OVER-LITERAL DEFLECTION: have a character respond to an emotionally
  loaded question with an oddly literal, factual answer, signaling
  avoidance without stating it.
- THE THIRD-PARTY CONFESSION: let a character reveal fear/knowledge only
  by talking ABOUT a third party or a hypothetical rather than themselves
  directly ("someone could get scared out here" instead of "I'm scared").
- REPETITION AS TELL: have a character repeat a specific, seemingly
  innocuous phrase multiple times across a scene/story as an unconscious
  verbal tic revealing suppressed anxiety, without ever naming the anxiety.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. CAMERA & LIGHTING GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Dialogue-heavy scenes benefit from Template 07's close-up/medium shot
  guidance to register the small facial tells that carry subtext when
  words don't.
- Consider pairing SILENCE_INTERRUPTION_PLACEMENT moments with a
  corresponding camera hold (Template 07's locked-off static technique) so
  the visual treatment reinforces the conversational silence rather than
  cutting away from it.
- Lighting (Template 09) during withholding dialogue often benefits from
  partial facial illumination, letting only part of a character's
  reaction be visible as they deflect.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
11. SOUND & ATMOSPHERE GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Silences within dialogue should be passed explicitly to Template 13 as
  SILENCE CUE moments, distinguished from ambient continuous background
  sound.
- Consider whether ambient sound should subtly intensify during moments a
  character approaches WHAT_IS_NOT_SAID, creating a sound-based tension
  cue that mirrors the conversational avoidance.
- Vocal delivery notes (pace, pitch, volume) from this template should
  feed directly into any voice-generation or performance-direction layer
  for consistency with Template 11.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
12. EXAMPLE INPUT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CHARACTER_VOICE_PROFILES: Ines — clipped, self-interrupting when anxious,
  avoids first-person fear statements. Warden Voss — measured, unhurried,
  answers questions with questions when avoiding a direct answer
SURFACE_TOPIC: Whether Ines should stay for one more day of the festival
SUBTEXT_LAYER: Voss is confirming whether Ines will accept the third and
  final "invitation"; Ines is beginning to suspect something is wrong but
  cannot articulate what
WHAT_IS_NOT_SAID: That staying constitutes final, binding acceptance of
  the entity's offer
DEFLECTION_PATTERN: Voss deflects by answering questions with questions;
  Ines deflects by focusing on logistics (train times, her return ticket)
  instead of her actual unease
DIALOGUE_RHYTHM: Slow, unhurried, long pauses between Voss's lines
  specifically
SILENCE_INTERRUPTION_PLACEMENT: Ines starts to ask "Is there something
  I'm supposed to—" and is gently, warmly interrupted by Voss before she
  finishes
POWER_DYNAMIC: Voss maintains total conversational control throughout,
  never raising his voice, never rushing
NEGATIVE_TRAITS: No direct mention of "invitation" or "entity," no
  villain monologue, no raised voices

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
13. EXAMPLE OUTPUT (Condensed)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SUBTEXT MAP: Voss is confirming Ines's third acceptance without her
awareness that acceptance is even being requested; Ines is trying to
locate her unease in something practical (train times) because she can't
yet name what's actually wrong.

FULL DIALOGUE SCENE:

VOSS
You have a return ticket, I imagine.

INES
Tomorrow evening. The six-fifteen.

VOSS
(a long pause)
The harvest supper is tomorrow evening.

INES
I know. I just — I told my sister I'd be back by Thursday.

VOSS
Does she worry about you often?

INES
(deflecting)
The six-fifteen gets in around nine. That should still work, if I—

VOSS
If you leave in the morning, you'll miss the supper.

INES
(a beat)
Is there something I'm supposed to—

VOSS
(warmly, before she finishes)
Stay for supper, Ines. Sisters worry. That's what they're for.

SUBTEXT VERIFICATION: No line states the invitation/acceptance mechanic
directly ✓. Ines's unfinished question is interrupted before any risk of
revealing the rule ✓. Voss's deflection pattern (question-for-question)
is consistent with his established profile ✓.

ALTERNATE LINE OPTIONS (for Voss's final line):
1. (as written) "Stay for supper, Ines. Sisters worry. That's what they're
   for." — warm, deflecting through domestic reassurance
2. "You've come this far." — colder, more overtly ominous if a harder tone
   is wanted
3. "One more night costs you nothing." — more direct pressure, useful if
   escalating urgency is needed for pacing

14. ADVANCED VERSION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Add for multi-scene dialogue arcs or ensemble conversation dynamics:

{DIALOGUE_ARC_ACROSS_STORY} — track how a specific character's willingness
  to say the unsaid thing evolves across the full story, mapped to
  escalation beats from Template 01
{GROUP_CONVERSATION_DYNAMICS} — for scenes with 3+ speaking characters,
  define how subtext operates differently in group settings (who can speak
  freely, who must perform for an audience within the scene)
{LANGUAGE_AND_DIALECT_LAYER} — for period pieces or specific cultural
  settings, define period-appropriate or dialect-consistent speech patterns
  that must be maintained throughout
{CALLBACK_LINE_TRACKER} — identify specific lines/phrases planted early
  that should recur, transformed, at later key moments (connects to
  Template 01's CALLBACK_ARCHITECTURE)

ADVANCED OUTPUT ADDITION: Request an "ON-THE-NOSE AUDIT" — a dedicated
review pass scanning the full dialogue draft for any line that states
emotion, threat, or plot information more directly than the established
DEFLECTION_PATTERN would allow, with rewrite suggestions for each flagged
line.
