────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. TITLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HORROR SCENE GENERATOR
Category: Scene-Level Composition
Compatible Engines: Sora, Veo, Kling, Runway, Pika, Luma, Hailuo (video
generation) + GPT/Claude/Gemini/Grok/DeepSeek (scene reasoning)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Assembles all prior design layers (story, character, creature, location)
into a single, complete, generation-ready SCENE prompt — the unit that
actually gets fed to a video model. This template is the assembly point of
the library: it doesn't invent new elements, it composes locked tokens from
Templates 01-05 with scene-specific beat logic, camera intent, and timing
into one coherent, production-ready instruction block.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. PROMPT FORMULA
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SCENE CONTEXT + BEAT TYPE] + [LOCKED CHARACTER/CREATURE TOKENS] +
[LOCKED LOCATION ZONE TOKEN] + [ACTION SEQUENCE: BEAT-BY-BEAT] + [TENSION
CURVE MARKER] + [CAMERA INTENT SUMMARY] + [LIGHT/SOUND STATE] + [DURATION +
PACING] + [PLATFORM-SPECIFIC SYNTAX WRAPPER]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. VARIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{SCENE_NUMBER_AND_CONTEXT} — where this scene falls in the larger story
{BEAT_TYPE} — build / release / false-release / climax (inherited from
  Template 02 scene breakdown)
{CHARACTER_TOKENS} — pasted consistency lock tokens from Template 03 for
  every character present
{CREATURE_TOKEN} — pasted consistency lock token from Template 04, at the
  correct REVEAL_FRAGMENT_MAP stage for this point in the story
{LOCATION_ZONE_TOKEN} — pasted zone-specific token from Template 05
{ACTION_SEQUENCE} — 3-6 numbered micro-beats describing exactly what
  happens, in order, within the scene
{TENSION_CURVE} — where the scene's peak intensity falls (opening / middle
  / final second) described explicitly
{CAMERA_INTENT_SUMMARY} — brief note on primary camera approach (detailed
  shot list belongs in Template 07; this is a scene-level directive only)
{LIGHT_SOUND_STATE} — the specific lighting and ambient sound state
  inherited from Templates 05/09/13 for this exact moment
{SCENE_DURATION} — target length in seconds (critical for video model
  prompting, since most platforms generate in short clips)
{CONTINUITY_ANCHORS} — specific facts that must match the immediately
  preceding and following scenes (character position, time of day, injury
  state)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. INPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Assemble a complete, generation-ready horror scene prompt.

SCENE CONTEXT: {SCENE_NUMBER_AND_CONTEXT}
BEAT TYPE: {BEAT_TYPE}
CHARACTERS PRESENT (locked tokens): {CHARACTER_TOKENS}
CREATURE STATE (locked token, correct reveal stage): {CREATURE_TOKEN}
LOCATION (locked zone token): {LOCATION_ZONE_TOKEN}
ACTION SEQUENCE: {ACTION_SEQUENCE}
TENSION CURVE: {TENSION_CURVE}
CAMERA INTENT: {CAMERA_INTENT_SUMMARY}
LIGHT/SOUND STATE: {LIGHT_SOUND_STATE}
DURATION: {SCENE_DURATION}
CONTINUITY ANCHORS: {CONTINUITY_ANCHORS}

Compose these into ONE cohesive scene description per target platform,
never contradicting any locked token, and respecting the specified
BEAT_TYPE pacing logic (builds should feel unresolved at the cut point;
releases should land on a clear image; false-releases should end on a
detail the audience may not consciously register as wrong yet).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. OUTPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. SCENE SUMMARY (one line: what happens and why it matters to the larger
   story)
2. MICRO-BEAT BREAKDOWN (numbered, matching ACTION_SEQUENCE, each beat
   flagged with its approximate timestamp within SCENE_DURATION)
3. FULL COMPOSED PROMPT — PLATFORM VARIANTS:
   - Sora/Veo/Kling/Runway/Pika/Luma/Hailuo natural-language cinematic
     prompt (single flowing paragraph, present tense, descriptive prose)
   - Image-model keyframe variant (for generating a key still/reference
     frame, comma-separated descriptor style)
4. CONTINUITY VERIFICATION (confirms no contradiction with prior/next scene
   anchors)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Never re-describe a character/creature/location from scratch here — this
  template's discipline is COMPOSITION, not invention. Paste locked tokens
  verbatim and add only scene-specific action/state.
- Match verb tense and descriptive register to the target platform: video
  models respond best to present-tense, single-flow cinematic prose;
  keyword-stacked syntax under-performs on most current video generators
  compared to descriptive sentences.
- Respect SCENE_DURATION realistically — most video generation platforms
  produce short clips (typically 4-20 seconds); a scene with 6 major
  action beats needs to be split into multiple generation calls rather
  than compressed into one over-stuffed prompt.
- The TENSION_CURVE placement changes what the LAST described moment in the
  prompt should be — always end the written prompt on the image you want
  the clip to end on, since video models weight recency in prompts heavily.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. BEST PRACTICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Keep ACTION_SEQUENCE micro-beats simple and physical — "she reaches for
  the handle" not "she nervously considers her options" — video models
  render physical action far more reliably than internal states.
- When a scene requires precise timing (a reveal must happen at a specific
  second), state it explicitly in the prompt ("in the final moment...")
  rather than relying on the model to infer pacing from description order.
- Batch scenes with matching LIGHT_SOUND_STATE together in your generation
  queue where possible — this reduces the chance of unwanted lighting
  drift between visually adjacent scenes.
- If a platform-specific generation looks inconsistent with locked tokens,
  revise the SCENE_DURATION or ACTION_SEQUENCE complexity down before
  changing the tokens themselves — over-stuffed prompts are a more common
  failure cause than weak tokens.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. HORROR CINEMATIC TECHNIQUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- THE UNRESOLVED CUT: for BUILD beats, deliberately end the described
  action mid-motion (a door beginning to open) rather than on a completed
  action, so the generated clip carries unresolved tension into the edit.
- BREATH BEFORE SCARE: for scenes preceding a major scare, include one
  explicit "nothing happens" micro-beat (nearly-still environment, minimal
  character motion) as the second-to-last beat.
- ENVIRONMENTAL FOREGROUNDING: place a small, specific environmental detail
  in the foreground of the composed prompt that will pay off in a later
  scene, creating subconscious dread on first viewing and payoff on rewatch.
- THE WRONG DETAIL LAST: for false-release beats, place a single subtly
  incorrect detail as the very last described element in the prompt so it
  lands on the final frame without being narratively explained.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. CAMERA & LIGHTING GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- CAMERA_INTENT_SUMMARY here should be a single directive sentence (e.g.,
  "slow push-in throughout, never cutting away") — full technical shot
  specification belongs in Template 07 and should be merged in before
  final platform submission.
- LIGHT_SOUND_STATE must exactly match the values locked in Templates 05
  and 09 for this location/time — treat any mismatch as a continuity bug
  to fix before generation, not a creative variation to allow.
- For scenes spanning a light-state transition (day to night, lit to
  power-outage), explicitly sequence the transition within ACTION_SEQUENCE
  rather than describing a static single state.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
11. SOUND & ATMOSPHERE GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Confirm the antagonist/creature sound motif (from Templates 01/04) is
  referenced at the correct micro-beat if the entity is present or
  imminent in this scene.
- Explicitly note any SILENCE CUE inherited from Template 02's script
  breakdown so downstream audio generation/scoring respects it.
- If LIGHT_SOUND_STATE calls for ambient ramp-up or ramp-down across the
  scene, sequence it as its own micro-beat rather than a static background
  descriptor.

12. EXAMPLE INPUT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENE_NUMBER_AND_CONTEXT: Scene 14 — Ines finds the ledger (mid-Act 2)
BEAT_TYPE: Build
CHARACTER_TOKENS: [Ines Calder lock token: "young woman, dark bob haircut,
  oversized borrowed cardigan, restless hands, clipped anxious speech"]
CREATURE_TOKEN: N/A (human-antagonist story; Elial Voss token used instead
  in adjacent scenes)
LOCATION_ZONE_TOKEN: [Record House Back Shelf token from Template 05
  equivalent: "dust-heavy back room, single bulb, floor-to-ceiling leather
  ledgers, cold despite the season"]
ACTION_SEQUENCE:
  1. Ines enters the back shelf room alone, flashlight in hand
  2. She scans the ledger spines, finds the most recent one
  3. She opens to the final page, finds her own name and today's date
  4. She flips backward through the pages, finding her name repeating at
     earlier and earlier dates
  5. The single bulb flickers once, does not go out
  6. She closes the ledger slowly and does not move for a moment
TENSION_CURVE: Peak at final second (the flicker + stillness)
CAMERA_INTENT_SUMMARY: Static locked-off camera for the entire scene; no
  movement until the very last beat, where a very slow, almost
  imperceptible push-in begins
LIGHT_SOUND_STATE: Single practical bulb, warm but dim, heavy shadow;
  three-beat low hum plays once, distant, during beat 4
SCENE_DURATION: 12 seconds
CONTINUITY_ANCHORS: Ines's cardigan must match prior scene (unchanged,
  clean); time of day is night, consistent with Scene 13's ending

13. EXAMPLE OUTPUT (Condensed)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SCENE SUMMARY: Ines discovers she was named in the commune's ledger years
before she ever arrived — the scene's dread comes entirely from stillness
and dawning realization, not action.

MICRO-BEAT BREAKDOWN:
0:00-0:02 — Enter, flashlight in teeth, scanning shelf
0:02-0:04 — Finds newest ledger, opens to final page
0:04-0:07 — Sees her own name and today's date; reacts with stillness
0:07-0:10 — Flips backward, name repeats at earlier dates
0:10-0:11 — Bulb flickers once
0:11-0:12 — She closes the ledger, motionless

FULL COMPOSED PROMPT (Video-model variant):
"A young woman with a dark bob haircut, wearing an oversized cardigan,
kneels in a dust-heavy back room lined floor to ceiling with old leather
ledgers, a single warm bulb providing the only dim light. She opens the
newest ledger to its final page and goes still — her own name is written
there, dated today. She flips backward through the pages, finding her name
repeated at earlier and earlier dates. A low three-beat hum sounds once,
distant. The bulb flickers a single time and steadies. She closes the
ledger slowly and does not move, the camera holding perfectly still before
beginning an almost imperceptible push toward her."

CONTINUITY VERIFICATION: Cardigan state matches Scene 13 (clean, unchanged)
✓. Night setting matches prior scene end state ✓. No creature token
required for this scene ✓.
