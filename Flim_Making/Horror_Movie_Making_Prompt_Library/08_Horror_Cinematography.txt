1. TITLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HORROR CINEMATOGRAPHY
Category: Visual Style & Color Science
Compatible Engines: Sora, Veo, Kling, Runway, Pika, Luma, Hailuo (video
generation) + GPT/Claude/Gemini/Grok/DeepSeek (style reasoning)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Defines the overarching VISUAL STYLE SYSTEM of a horror project — color
grading philosophy, film stock/digital character emulation, contrast
handling, and grain/texture treatment — distinct from Template 07's shot-
by-shot camera mechanics. Where Camera Master answers "how is this shot
composed," Cinematography answers "what does this entire film look and
feel like as a consistent visual object," ensuring every generated scene
shares the same photographic DNA regardless of which AI platform produced
it.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. PROMPT FORMULA
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[VISUAL REFERENCE ANCHOR] + [COLOR PALETTE PHILOSOPHY] + [CONTRAST/DYNAMIC
RANGE PROFILE] + [TEXTURE/GRAIN TREATMENT] + [SATURATION LOGIC BY SCENE
TYPE] + [PRACTICAL VS. STYLIZED BALANCE] + [CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN] +
[PLATFORM-SPECIFIC SYNTAX WRAPPER]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. VARIABLES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
{VISUAL_REFERENCE_ANCHOR} — 2-3 named cinematographic reference points
  (described by their qualities, not just named, e.g., "the desaturated
  naturalism of Roger Deakins' night exteriors")
{COLOR_PALETTE_PHILOSOPHY} — the core palette logic (desaturated with one
  intruding color, monochromatic with warm/cool split, high-saturation
  wrongness against muted reality)
{CONTRAST_PROFILE} — high-contrast/crushed-blacks vs. low-contrast/flat
  vs. selective-contrast (deep blacks with one carefully lit subject)
{TEXTURE_GRAIN_TREATMENT} — clean digital vs. film-grain emulation vs.
  degraded/found-footage texture, and consistency requirements across
  scenes
{SATURATION_LOGIC_BY_SCENE_TYPE} — how saturation should shift between
  "normal world" scenes and "wrongness" scenes (a specific, repeatable
  rule, not a vague mood shift)
{PRACTICAL_VS_STYLIZED_BALANCE} — how naturalistic vs. how expressionistic
  the visual treatment should be overall (a 1-10 scale is useful here)
{SIGNATURE_COLOR} — one specific color reserved exclusively for
  wrongness/danger moments, never used elsewhere in the palette
{TIME_OF_DAY_TREATMENT} — how day/night/magic-hour should each be rendered
  within the established palette philosophy
{CONSISTENCY_LOCK_TOKEN} — reusable phrase capturing the overall visual
  signature for insertion into every scene prompt
{NEGATIVE_TRAITS} — exclude default AI-generation tendencies (no default
  overly-saturated "cinematic" look, no unmotivated lens flare, no generic
  teal-and-orange grading unless deliberately chosen)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. OUTPUT STRUCTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. CINEMATOGRAPHY BIBLE SUMMARY (paragraph describing the complete visual
   philosophy and how it serves the story's themes)
2. CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN (isolated, bolded — the style fragment for every
   scene prompt)
3. SCENE-TYPE VARIANTS (specific palette/contrast adjustments for: normal-
   world scenes, build/tension scenes, wrongness/reveal scenes, climax)
4. PLATFORM ADAPTATIONS (how to phrase this style system for image-model
   syntax vs. video-model natural language vs. LLM reasoning blocks)
5. CONTINUITY CHECKLIST (fixed visual facts that must never drift: grain
   level, signature color usage rule, contrast baseline)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Establish the visual style BEFORE generating any scenes, not after — style
  drift across a project is one of the most common failure modes when
  multiple AI platforms or multiple generation sessions are involved.
- Reserve SIGNATURE_COLOR with absolute discipline — a color that appears
  everywhere loses its power to signal danger; audiences learn visual
  grammar quickly and will read the pattern within a few appearances.
- Avoid defaulting to generic "cinematic" AI-generation aesthetics (overly
  saturated, excessive lens flare, glossy digital sheen) unless
  specifically serving the story — these defaults actively work against
  horror's need for naturalistic dread.
- Describe grain/texture treatment with enough specificity that it survives
  translation across platforms — vague terms like "moody" or "cinematic"
  are interpreted wildly differently by different video models.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. BEST PRACTICES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Build 3-4 reference frames (even as rough test generations) early in
  production to validate that the described style actually renders
  consistently on your target platform(s) before committing to full-scene
  generation.
- Keep the CONSISTENCY_LOCK_TOKEN concise (15-20 words) — style tokens
  compete for descriptive weight with character/location/action tokens in
  the same prompt, so brevity here preserves fidelity elsewhere.
- Document exactly which scenes are permitted to break the established
  palette rules (if any) as deliberate, planned exceptions — unplanned
  exceptions read as inconsistency, planned ones read as intentional
  visual punctuation.
- When working across multiple video platforms for a single project,
  test the same lock token on each and note necessary micro-adjustments
  per platform in the continuity checklist.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. HORROR CINEMATIC TECHNIQUES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- THE CREEPING DESATURATION: gradually reduce saturation across the film's
  runtime as dread mounts, so the "normal world" at the start looks
  noticeably more vivid in retrospect than the washed-out climax.
- SIGNATURE COLOR INTRUSION: introduce SIGNATURE_COLOR in small, easy-to-
  miss doses early (a single object) before it dominates a frame at the
  climax, training audience recognition subliminally.
- CONTRAST AS THREAT INDICATOR: use a measurable contrast increase (deeper
  blacks, harder falloff) specifically in scenes where the antagonist is
  present or imminent, even if the audience can't consciously name why the
  scene "feels different."
- GRAIN AS TEXTURE OF DREAD: increase grain/texture noise subtly during
  high-tension sequences — a slightly "dirtier" image reads as more
  vulnerable and analog, less controlled.
- THE FALSE-WARMTH SCENE: occasionally light a dangerous scene with
  the "safe" warm palette reserved for normal-world scenes, creating
  unconscious unease through mismatched visual grammar.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. CAMERA & LIGHTING GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- This template governs the "paint," Template 09 governs the "light
  sources" — they must be developed together, since contrast profile and
  practical lighting choices are deeply interdependent.
- CONTRAST_PROFILE should specify exact black/shadow behavior (crushed to
  near-void vs. detail-retaining) since this single choice affects every
  night/dark scene generated across the entire project.
- Cross-reference Template 07's lens choices — wide lenses and shallow
  depth of field interact differently with different grain/texture
  treatments, and should be validated together on sample generations.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
11. SOUND & ATMOSPHERE GUIDE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Note whether SIGNATURE_COLOR intrusions should be paired with a
  corresponding signature sound cue (from Template 13) for cross-sensory
  conditioning of the audience.
- Match the overall PRACTICAL_VS_STYLIZED_BALANCE to a corresponding sound
  design philosophy (naturalistic sound design for low stylization,
  processed/synthetic sound design for high stylization) for tonal
  cohesion between image and audio.

12. EXAMPLE INPUT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
VISUAL_REFERENCE_ANCHOR: The patient, naturalistic dread of "The Witch"
  combined with the creeping desaturation of "The Road"
COLOR_PALETTE_PHILOSOPHY: Muted earth tones (ochre, faded green, bone white)
  for the "normal" commune world, with a single reserved deep amber-gold
  for anything tied to the entity's "invitations"
CONTRAST_PROFILE: Low-to-moderate contrast for daylight commune scenes
  (soft, overcast naturalism); high contrast with crushed shadow detail for
  all night/interior scenes
TEXTURE_GRAIN_TREATMENT: Fine, consistent 35mm-emulation grain throughout;
  no clean digital look permitted at any point
SATURATION_LOGIC_BY_SCENE_TYPE: Normal-world scenes start at moderate
  saturation and decrease by a visible increment at each of the 5 major
  escalation beats; wrongness/invitation scenes always carry the reserved
  amber-gold at full saturation regardless of surrounding desaturation
PRACTICAL_VS_STYLIZED_BALANCE: 3/10 (highly naturalistic, minimal overt
  stylization)
SIGNATURE_COLOR: Deep amber-gold, reserved exclusively for entity-related
  "invitation" moments
TIME_OF_DAY_TREATMENT: Daylight scenes overcast/soft; night scenes deep,
  near-black shadow with minimal practical illumination
NEGATIVE_TRAITS: No teal-and-orange default grading, no glossy digital
  sheen, no unmotivated lens flare

13. EXAMPLE OUTPUT (Condensed)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CINEMATOGRAPHY BIBLE SUMMARY: A naturalistic, 35mm-grain-emulated visual
world built on muted earth tones that grow quieter and more desaturated
with each escalation beat, punctuated only by a single reserved amber-gold
that marks the entity's presence — the palette itself tells the story of
a world slowly losing its color to something that offers only one.

CONSISTENCY LOCK TOKEN:
"Naturalistic 35mm grain, muted earth-tone palette, overcast soft daylight,
crushed-shadow night interiors, reserved deep amber-gold for entity
moments only"

SCENE-TYPE VARIANT — Wrongness/Invitation Scene:
[Lock Token] + "this scene carries the full reserved amber-gold at maximum
saturation against the otherwise desaturated surroundings, contrast
elevated, shadows crushed to near-black outside the amber's reach"

PLATFORM ADAPTATIONS:
- Video-model syntax: "Shot on naturalistic 35mm-style film grain, muted
  earth-tone color palette throughout — ochre, faded green, bone white.
  Daylight scenes are soft and overcast. Night interiors carry high
  contrast with deep crushed shadow. In this moment, a single warm
  amber-gold glow dominates the frame, more saturated than anything else
  in the world around it."

CONTINUITY CHECKLIST: Amber-gold NEVER appears outside entity/invitation
moments ✓. Grain level remains constant across all scenes regardless of
platform ✓. Saturation decrease is measured per escalation beat, not
randomly applied ✓.
