ULTIMATE HOLLYWOOD AI FILMMAKING PROMPT LIBRARY
FILE 13: SOUND & MUSIC
================================================================================

TITLE
-----
The Sonic Architecture Engine — Diegetic Sound, Score & Sound Design
Specification System

PURPOSE
-------
Design the complete audio layer for a scene — diegetic sound (sound that
exists within the story world), non-diegetic score (music the audience
hears but characters don't), and sound design (constructed sonic textures
that heighten mood/tension) — all working together to serve the story
established across every prior template. Sound is frequently treated as
an afterthought in AI filmmaking workflows; this template ensures it's
specified with the same rigor as visual elements.

PROMPT FORMULA
---------------
[SCENE/BEAT CONTEXT] + [DIEGETIC SOUND LAYER] + [SCORE/MUSIC LAYER] +
[SOUND DESIGN LAYER] + [SILENCE AS TOOL] + [MIX PRIORITY] + [EMOTIONAL
FUNCTION] + [TRANSITION BEHAVIOR]

Master formula string:

"Design the audio for {SCENE_BEAT_CONTEXT}. Diegetic sound layer:
{DIEGETIC_SOUNDS} at {DIEGETIC_LEVEL}, sourced from
{ENVIRONMENT_CONSISTENCY_TOKEN}. Score/music layer: {MUSICAL_APPROACH},
instrumentation {INSTRUMENTATION}, tempo/energy {TEMPO_ENERGY}, entering
at {SCORE_ENTRY_POINT} and {SCORE_BEHAVIOR_ACROSS_BEAT}. Sound design
layer: {CONSTRUCTED_SOUND_ELEMENTS} serving {SOUND_DESIGN_FUNCTION}.
Silence: {SILENCE_USAGE} at {SILENCE_MOMENT}, serving
{SILENCE_FUNCTION}. Mix priority: {MIX_HIERARCHY} — ensure
{PRIORITY_ELEMENT} remains audible above all other layers when present.
Emotional function: this audio must make the audience feel
{TARGET_EMOTION} without the visual alone achieving it. Transition:
{TRANSITION_TYPE} into/out of adjacent scenes."

VARIABLES
---------
SCENE_BEAT_CONTEXT       = pulled from File 04
DIEGETIC_SOUNDS          = actual in-world sounds (footsteps, dialogue,
                            ambient room tone, mechanical sounds) —
                            cross-reference File 09's AMBIENT_SOUND_
                            DEFAULT for the location
DIEGETIC_LEVEL           = relative volume/presence in the mix
ENVIRONMENT_CONSISTENCY_TOKEN = pulled from File 09, ensures sound matches
                            established location
MUSICAL_APPROACH         = "score present, subtle/underscoring" | "score
                            present, prominent/driving" | "no score,
                            diegetic-only" | "source music (music that
                            exists within the story world, e.g. a radio)"
INSTRUMENTATION          = specific instrument palette (e.g. "sparse
                            piano and low string drone" not just "sad
                            music")
TEMPO_ENERGY             = pace and intensity of the musical approach
SCORE_ENTRY_POINT        = the specific moment within the beat where
                            score begins (never arbitrary — should be
                            motivated by a story beat)
SCORE_BEHAVIOR_ACROSS_BEAT = how the score evolves during the beat
                            (builds, sustains, drops out, resolves)
CONSTRUCTED_SOUND_ELEMENTS = non-naturalistic sound design choices that
                            heighten mood (a subtle low-frequency rumble
                            under tension, a hyper-real amplified detail
                            sound) — distinct from pure diegetic realism
SOUND_DESIGN_FUNCTION    = what psychological/narrative job the
                            constructed sound elements do
SILENCE_USAGE            = "hard silence (complete audio drop)" | "near-
                            silence (minimal room tone only)" | "not used
                            this beat"
SILENCE_MOMENT           = the specific point where silence is deployed,
                            if used
SILENCE_FUNCTION         = why silence is being used here (emphasis,
                            dread, arrested attention)
MIX_HIERARCHY            = which audio layer takes priority when multiple
                            elements compete (typically dialogue > key
                            sound design element > score > ambient
                            diegetic, but can be deliberately inverted for
                            effect)
PRIORITY_ELEMENT         = the single most important audio element in
                            this specific beat
TARGET_EMOTION           = pulled from File 04's beat-level emotional
                            temperature and File 10's performance
                            direction
TRANSITION_TYPE          = "hard cut" | "cross-fade" | "score bridges the
                            cut" | "sound effect bridges the cut (a sound
                            that starts in one scene and completes in the
                            next)"

INPUT STRUCTURE
----------------
SCENE_BEAT_CONTEXT: <required, from File 04>
ENVIRONMENT_CONSISTENCY_TOKEN: <required, from File 09>
TARGET_EMOTION: <required, from File 04/10>
MUSICAL_APPROACH: <required>
MIX_HIERARCHY: <optional, default = "dialogue priority, all else
  subordinate">

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
-----------------
1. DIEGETIC SOUND LAYER (itemized in-world sounds, sourced from
   established environment)
2. SCORE/MUSIC LAYER (approach, instrumentation, entry point, evolution
   across the beat)
3. SOUND DESIGN LAYER (constructed elements and their psychological
   function)
4. SILENCE STRATEGY (if used — placement and function)
5. MIX HIERARCHY (explicit priority ordering for this beat)
6. EMOTIONAL FUNCTION STATEMENT (what feeling this audio design achieves
   that visuals alone don't)
7. TRANSITION SPECIFICATION (how audio bridges into/out of adjacent
   scenes)
8. COMPILED AUDIO PROMPT LINE (dense, generation-ready description for
   audio generation tools or as direction accompanying video generation)

PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
---------------------------
- Score entry must always be motivated by a specific story beat, never
  arbitrary — music that starts "because the scene needs music" rather
  than at a specific dramatic trigger point reads as generic and
  undermines the emotional precision the rest of this pipeline has built.
- Diegetic sound must be sourced from and consistent with the established
  Environment (File 09) — never invent ambient sounds that contradict a
  location's established sonic signature.
- Silence is an active tool, not the absence of design — every deployment
  of silence must have a stated function (dread, emphasis, arrested
  attention) exactly like any other sound choice; unmotivated silence is
  as much an error as unmotivated sound.
- Mix hierarchy must be explicit for every beat with multiple competing
  audio elements — never leave it ambiguous which element the audience's
  attention should be drawn to.
- Sound design elements should generally be felt rather than consciously
  noticed by the audience — favor subtle, texturally integrated
  constructed sound over obvious, attention-drawing effects unless the
  moment specifically calls for an audible sonic event.
- Avoid "mickey-mousing" (score that literally mimics on-screen action
  beat-for-beat in an obvious, cartoonish way) unless the genre
  specifically calls for that heightened style — Hollywood-grade scoring
  generally works more subtly, supporting emotional truth rather than
  illustrating physical action.

BEST PRACTICES
----------------
- Generate Sound & Music after Emotion & Acting (File 10) and Dialogue
  (File 11) are locked for a given beat — audio design should respond to
  and enhance the performance/dialogue choices already made, not be
  generated in isolation and risk working against them.
- Build a project-wide "sonic identity" document (similar to File 06's
  Cinematography identity) early in a project — establish a consistent
  scoring palette/instrumentation approach that can flex per scene while
  remaining recognizably unified across the whole piece.
- Use the Mix Hierarchy specification as a discipline tool even for
  simple scenes — explicitly stating "dialogue priority" even when
  obvious prevents the habit of leaving audio relationships unspecified
  in more complex scenes later.
- Cross-reference every SCORE_ENTRY_POINT against File 04's beat
  breakdown to confirm it aligns with an actual emotional turn in the
  scene, not just a scene boundary.
- For AI generation pipelines that separate visual and audio generation,
  ensure the COMPILED AUDIO PROMPT LINE explicitly references timing
  relative to the visual beat structure so the two can be synchronized
  in post.

EXAMPLE INPUT
---------------
SCENE_BEAT_CONTEXT: Scene 2, Beats 1-3 — Marcus reading the trace results
and the aborted photo-frame reach
ENVIRONMENT_CONSISTENCY_TOKEN: [Bullpen token from File 09]
TARGET_EMOTION: Controlled shock cracking into suppressed vulnerability,
then forced composure
MUSICAL_APPROACH: Score present, subtle/underscoring — this is a
restrained, internal moment, not one that should be musically overstated

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
-----------------
DIEGETIC SOUND LAYER: Per File 09's established ambient signature —
keyboard clatter from distant desks (reduced/receded per File 04 Beat 1's
"recession" note), HVAC hum, the electronic hum of Marcus's own dual
monitors (closer, more present than the distant bullpen sounds). No
dialogue in this sequence.

SCORE/MUSIC LAYER: Sparse, subtle underscoring — a single sustained low
string note (cello, close to its lowest register) enters precisely at
Beat 1's midpoint, the exact moment Marcus's eyes register the resolved
address. Instrumentation: solo cello only, no other instruments, no
percussion, no harmonic movement — a static, held tone rather than a
melodic phrase. Tempo/energy: near-static, barely perceptible bow
pressure variation. Behavior across the beat: the note sustains
continuously through Beats 1 and 2, developing a barely perceptible
tremolo (bow shake) precisely as his hand begins its aborted reach in
Beat 2, then resolves — a single subtle downward pitch bend, not a full
release — as composure reasserts in Beat 3.

SOUND DESIGN LAYER: The "recession" of ambient bullpen noise established
in File 04 Beat 1 should be achieved partly through sound design rather
than pure diegetic realism — a subtle low-pass filter effect making
distant sounds feel muffled/far away, as if Marcus's own perception is
narrowing. This serves the function of putting the audience inside his
subjective experience without any visual point-of-view shot being
necessary.

SILENCE STRATEGY: Not full silence, but the described "recession" creates
a near-silence quality relative to Scene 1's fuller ambient bullpen sound.
Function: isolates Marcus psychologically from his environment at the
exact moment his composure is tested.

MIX HIERARCHY: Priority order: (1) the sustained cello note — this is the
emotional throughline of the sequence and must remain clearly audible
throughout, (2) Marcus's own monitor hum (close, personal, isolating),
(3) the receded/filtered distant bullpen ambience (present but
subordinate).

EMOTIONAL FUNCTION STATEMENT: The sustained cello note does the emotional
work that Marcus's restrained performance (File 10) can't fully carry
alone — it gives the audience access to the internal intensity beneath
his controlled exterior, without requiring him to visibly "act" more than
his established restraint allows.

TRANSITION SPECIFICATION: The cello note's final subtle pitch-bend
resolution should occur just before the cut to the next scene, functioning
as a score bridge — the emotional "settling" the note performs should
feel connected to, not abruptly cut off from, whatever follows.

COMPILED AUDIO PROMPT LINE: "Sparse ambient office soundscape with
distant keyboard clatter and HVAC hum, filtered/muffled to suggest
narrowing perception, close electronic monitor hum. Single sustained low
cello note enters at the moment of recognition, develops subtle tremolo
during the hand's aborted movement, resolves with a gentle downward pitch
bend as composure returns. No dialogue, no percussion, minimal harmonic
movement — restrained, internal scoring."

ADVANCED VERSION
-------------------
For complex multi-scene scoring or full sequence sound design, extend
with:

"Additionally generate: (a) a PROJECT-WIDE MOTIF NOTE if this musical
material should recur/develop as a leitmotif across later scenes tied to
this character or theme, (b) a FULL FREQUENCY SPECTRUM MAP noting which
frequency ranges each audio layer occupies to prevent mix conflicts
(especially important when dialogue, score, and sound design compete for
similar frequency space), (c) a TEMP-TRACK REFERENCE NOTE describing the
general sonic tradition/tonal reference this score draws from (for
calibration only, never for direct copying, and never naming specific
copyrighted compositions to replicate), (d) explicit AUDIO GENERATION
PLATFORM NOTES for whichever AI audio/music generation tool is being
used, phrased in that tool's known effective prompt conventions as of
current best practice, including how to specify the precise timing
synchronization needed against the visual beat structure from File 05."
