TITLE
-----
The Rhythm & Cut Engine — Editorial Pacing & Transition Specification
System

PURPOSE
-------
Specify how individual generated shots (from Files 04-14) are assembled
into a coherent sequence — cut timing, rhythm, transition type, and the
editorial logic connecting one shot to the next. This template exists
because even perfectly generated individual shots will fail to create a
coherent viewing experience without deliberate editorial architecture;
editing is where pacing, tension, and narrative momentum are ultimately
made or broken.

PROMPT FORMULA
---------------
[SEQUENCE CONTEXT] + [SHOT-TO-SHOT CUT LOGIC] + [PACING RHYTHM] +
[TRANSITION TYPE PER CUT] + [CONTINUITY RULES] + [MATCH CUTS/MOTIVATED
CUTS] + [MONTAGE LOGIC IF APPLICABLE] + [OVERALL SEQUENCE ARC]

Master formula string:

"Edit the sequence spanning {SHOT_LIST}, part of {SCENE_CONTEXT}. Cut
logic for each transition: cut FROM {SHOT_A} TO {SHOT_B} is motivated by
{CUT_MOTIVATION} — {CUT_TYPE}. Pacing rhythm: overall sequence tempo is
{TEMPO_DESCRIPTION}, with cut frequency {CUTS_PER_MINUTE_EQUIVALENT}.
Transition type: {TRANSITION_STYLE} at this specific cut point.
Continuity: verify {CONTINUITY_CHECKPOINTS} remain consistent across the
cut (screen direction, eyeline, prop/costume state, lighting
continuity). Match cuts/motivated cuts: {MATCH_CUT_OPPORTUNITIES} if
present. Montage logic: {MONTAGE_STRUCTURE if applicable}. Overall
sequence arc: pacing should {PACING_ARC_DIRECTION} across the full
sequence, serving {SEQUENCE_EMOTIONAL_FUNCTION}."

VARIABLES
---------
SHOT_LIST                = the ordered list of shots being assembled,
                            pulled from File 05's beat-by-beat camera
                            specifications
SCENE_CONTEXT             = pulled from File 04
SHOT_A / SHOT_B           = the two shots being joined at each cut point
CUT_MOTIVATION            = why the cut happens HERE specifically (a
                            look, a sound, a completed action, a line of
                            dialogue ending) — cuts should almost always
                            be motivated by something within the shot,
                            not arbitrary timing
CUT_TYPE                  = "straight cut (standard)" | "match cut
                            (visual/conceptual continuity across the
                            cut)" | "jump cut (deliberate discontinuity
                            for effect)" | "cutaway (brief shot to
                            secondary subject before returning)" | "L-cut
                            /J-cut (audio from one shot bleeds into the
                            next before/after the visual cut)"
TEMPO_DESCRIPTION         = "slow, contemplative, long holds" |
                            "moderate, standard dramatic pacing" |
                            "fast, urgent, quick cuts" | "escalating
                            (starts slow, accelerates)" | "variable/
                            syncopated for effect"
CUTS_PER_MINUTE_EQUIVALENT = rough guide to cut frequency appropriate to
                            the tempo (helps calibrate — contemplative
                            drama might be 4-8 cuts/minute, action might
                            be 30+)
TRANSITION_STYLE          = "hard cut" | "cross-dissolve (specify
                            duration)" | "fade to black/from black" |
                            "wipe (rare in modern Hollywood work, use
                            sparingly)" | "match cut transition"
CONTINUITY_CHECKPOINTS    = specific elements that must remain consistent
                            across the cut (drawn from Files 03, 08, 09
                            consistency tokens)
MATCH_CUT_OPPORTUNITIES   = specific visual or conceptual rhymes between
                            shots that could be exploited as a deliberate
                            match cut
MONTAGE_STRUCTURE         = if this sequence is a montage (compressed
                            time/multiple locations), the organizing
                            principle connecting the shots (thematic,
                            chronological, parallel action)
PACING_ARC_DIRECTION      = "accelerate" | "decelerate" | "sustain" |
                            "build then release" — how tempo should
                            evolve across the sequence
SEQUENCE_EMOTIONAL_FUNCTION = what feeling this sequence's editorial
                            rhythm should create in the audience

INPUT STRUCTURE
----------------
SHOT_LIST: <required, from File 05's beat breakdown for the relevant
  scene/sequence>
SCENE_CONTEXT: <required, from File 04>
TEMPO_DESCRIPTION: <required, should align with File 01's structural
  pacing needs at this point in the story>
CONTINUITY_CHECKPOINTS: <required, pull from relevant Character/
  Environment consistency tokens>
SEQUENCE_EMOTIONAL_FUNCTION: <required>

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
-----------------
1. SEQUENCE OVERVIEW (shot list restated, overall sequence function)
2. CUT-BY-CUT BREAKDOWN, for each transition:
   - Shots being joined
   - Cut motivation (what specifically triggers this cut)
   - Cut type and transition style
   - Continuity verification (screen direction, eyeline, prop/costume/
     lighting state)
3. PACING RHYTHM MAP (tempo across the full sequence, cut frequency
   pattern)
4. MATCH CUT / MOTIVATED CUT HIGHLIGHTS (any particularly deliberate
   editorial choices worth flagging)
5. MONTAGE STRUCTURE (if applicable)
6. SEQUENCE ARC SUMMARY (how pacing serves the sequence's emotional
   function overall)
7. EDITORIAL ASSEMBLY NOTES (practical guidance for actually assembling
   the AI-generated clips in sequence, including any needed overlap/
   trim guidance)

PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
---------------------------
- Every cut must be motivated — a hard rule. Cuts should occur at natural
  attention-shift points (end of an action, a look toward something off-
  screen, a completed line of dialogue, a sound cue) rather than at
  arbitrary time intervals. If a cut point can't be justified by
  something happening in the shot, reconsider its placement.
- Screen direction (the 180-degree rule) must be explicitly verified at
  every cut involving character movement or eyeline — characters and
  objects must maintain consistent left-right spatial relationships
  across cuts unless a deliberate direction-crossing shot is used to
  signal disorientation (and even then, this should be a rare, marked
  choice, not an accident).
- Continuity checkpoints must be actively verified, not assumed —
  explicitly confirm that costume state, prop position, and established
  lighting logic remain consistent shot-to-shot, especially in AI-
  generated pipelines where each shot may be a separate generation call
  vulnerable to unprompted drift.
- Pacing rhythm must serve the STORY's needs (from File 01's structural
  breakdown), not be applied uniformly — a midpoint revelation beat
  likely needs different cut rhythm than a climactic action sequence, and
  this template should reflect that story-driven variation.
- Match cuts and other highly deliberate editorial techniques should be
  used sparingly and only when they genuinely serve meaning — overuse
  dilutes their impact and can read as showing off technique rather than
  serving story.
- L-cuts and J-cuts (audio bleeding across a visual cut) are a powerful,
  underused tool for creating smoother, more sophisticated-feeling
  editorial flow — actively look for opportunities to use them rather than
  defaulting to hard cuts throughout.

BEST PRACTICES
----------------
- Generate this template only after the full shot list for a
  scene/sequence exists (Files 04, 05, and ideally 07-14 for full
  specification) — editing decisions should respond to what's actually
  been specified for each shot, not be planned in isolation beforehand.
- Cross-reference File 13 (Sound & Music) for any planned score
  entry/transition points — audio and editorial rhythm should be designed
  together, since a cut landing exactly on a musical beat (or deliberately
  NOT landing on one) is a powerful and commonly used technique.
- For AI-generated footage specifically, build in slightly more overlap/
  handle at the start and end of each generated clip than you think
  you'll need — this gives editorial flexibility to adjust exact cut
  timing without needing to regenerate footage.
- Map the full sequence's Pacing Rhythm against File 01's structural
  beats before finalizing — verify that editorial tempo actually
  escalates/decelerates in alignment with the story's stakes ladder,
  rather than being disconnected from narrative structure.
- When working with multiple AI generation tools across a single
  sequence, pay special attention to Continuity Checkpoints since
  different platforms may render the "same" specified detail with subtle
  variation — flag any point where cross-platform consistency is
  especially at risk.

EXAMPLE INPUT
---------------
SHOT_LIST: Scene 2's three beats — MCU eye-level static (Beat 1), ECU
insert high-angle dolly-in (Beat 2), MCU eye-level static bookend (Beat 3)
— per File 05
SCENE_CONTEXT: Marcus reading the trace results, the aborted photo-frame
reach
TEMPO_DESCRIPTION: Slow, contemplative, long holds — this is an internal,
restrained moment
CONTINUITY_CHECKPOINTS: Marcus's consistency token (File 03), desk/prop
state (photo frame remains face-down throughout, per File 04's continuity
handoff), established cool-blue lighting (File 07)
SEQUENCE_EMOTIONAL_FUNCTION: The audience should feel the weight of a
single held breath — restraint and internal pressure, not urgency

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
-----------------
SEQUENCE OVERVIEW: A three-shot sequence (Beats 1-3 of Scene 2) depicting
Marcus's controlled reaction to a devastating discovery, cut with maximum
restraint to mirror his own suppressed emotional state.

CUT-BY-CUT BREAKDOWN:

Cut 1 (Beat 1 → Beat 2): Shots joined — MCU eye-level static (reading the
address) → ECU insert high-angle dolly-in (the hand's aborted reach). Cut
motivation: triggered by the completion of his reading — a beat of
stillness after his eyes stop moving across the screen, then cut,
mirroring the exact moment his conscious control gives way to
involuntary impulse. Cut type: straight cut, but held slightly longer
than a standard reaction cut (approximately half a second longer than
tempo would typically dictate) to let the stillness register before the
cut occurs. Transition style: hard cut, no dissolve — this moment
requires the abruptness of a straight cut, not the softness of a
dissolve. Continuity verification: Marcus's position at the desk remains
consistent (File 03 token, File 09 environment token); no screen-
direction conflict since both shots are effectively axis-neutral
(camera positioned directly at/above the subject).

Cut 2 (Beat 2 → Beat 3): Shots joined — ECU insert high-angle dolly-in
(hand stopping) → MCU eye-level static bookend (composure reasserting).
Cut motivation: triggered by the hand's dead-stop — the cut occurs the
instant the movement ceases completely, not before or after, emphasizing
the abruptness of the self-interruption. Cut type: straight cut. Transition
style: hard cut. Continuity verification: hand position at end of Beat 2
(withdrawn, per File 04's handoff) must match hand position visible (if
any) at start of Beat 3; lighting continuity (File 07's cool baseline,
with the barely perceptible warm shift resolving) must read consistently
across the cut.

PACING RHYTHM MAP: Two cuts across an 8-second sequence — deliberately
sparse cutting for a moment this internal. Cut frequency equivalent to
roughly 15 cuts/minute if sustained, but functionally the sequence reads
as even slower due to the held stillness before Cut 1 specifically.

MATCH CUT / MOTIVATED CUT HIGHLIGHTS: Beat 1 and Beat 3 use matching
shot specifications (MCU, eye-level, static) as a deliberate bookend —
this isn't a match cut in the traditional visual-rhyme sense, but a
compositional echo that reinforces the "return to baseline" function
of Beat 3, letting the audience register the return to composure partly
through the camera's own return to its earlier position.

MONTAGE STRUCTURE: Not applicable — this is a continuous real-time
sequence, not a montage.

SEQUENCE ARC SUMMARY: The pacing sustains a deliberately slow, restrained
rhythm throughout — no acceleration, no release — mirroring Marcus's own
suppression of his reaction. The two cuts that do occur are both
tightly motivated by physical action completion (the end of reading, the
end of the hand's movement) rather than arbitrary timing, reinforcing
that even the editorial rhythm itself is "controlled" rather than
free-flowing.

EDITORIAL ASSEMBLY NOTES: When assembling the AI-generated clips for
these three beats, ensure at least 0.5-1 second of handle/overlap at each
clip's start and end beyond the stated beat duration, to allow precise
cut-point adjustment without needing regeneration. Beat 1's ending
stillness (the half-second hold before the cut) should be preserved in
full when trimming — do not cut this moment short even if the generated
clip runs slightly longer than the original 3-second target.
