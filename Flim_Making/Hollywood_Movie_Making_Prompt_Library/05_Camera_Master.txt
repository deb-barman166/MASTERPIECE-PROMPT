================================================================================
ULTIMATE HOLLYWOOD AI FILMMAKING PROMPT LIBRARY
FILE 05: CAMERA MASTER (SHOTS + ANGLES + MOVEMENTS + LENS)
================================================================================

TITLE
-----
The Complete Camera Grammar Engine — Shot Type, Angle, Movement & Lens
Specification System

PURPOSE
-------
Translate a beat-by-beat scene (from File 04) into precise, technically
correct camera language that any AI video model can parse consistently.
This is the single most consequential template in the library for visual
quality — vague camera direction is the number one cause of generic-looking
AI video output. This template forces explicit selection across four
locked categories (Shot Type, Angle, Movement, Lens) for every single beat,
with no ambiguity permitted.

PROMPT FORMULA
---------------
[BEAT INPUT] + [SHOT TYPE] + [CAMERA ANGLE] + [CAMERA MOVEMENT] + [LENS
SPECIFICATION] + [FOCAL BEHAVIOR] + [FRAME RATE/SHUTTER FEEL] + [RATIONALE]

Master formula string, applied per beat:

"For beat {BEAT_NUMBER} ({BEAT_DESCRIPTION}), specify: Shot type —
{SHOT_TYPE}. Camera angle — {CAMERA_ANGLE}. Camera movement —
{CAMERA_MOVEMENT} at {MOVEMENT_SPEED}. Lens — {FOCAL_LENGTH}mm equivalent,
{APERTURE_FEEL} depth of field. Focal behavior — {FOCUS_BEHAVIOR}.
Frame rate/shutter feel — {MOTION_QUALITY}. State the dramatic rationale
for every choice in one sentence: why this shot type, this angle, this
movement, and this lens serve the beat's emotional/narrative function."

VARIABLES
---------
SHOT_TYPE       = "Extreme Wide Shot (EWS)" | "Wide Shot (WS)" | "Full Shot
                   (FS)" | "Medium Wide/Cowboy Shot" | "Medium Shot (MS)" |
                   "Medium Close-Up (MCU)" | "Close-Up (CU)" | "Extreme
                   Close-Up (ECU)" | "Two-Shot" | "Over-the-Shoulder (OTS)"
                   | "Point of View (POV)" | "Insert Shot"
CAMERA_ANGLE    = "Eye Level" | "High Angle" | "Low Angle" | "Bird's Eye/
                   Overhead" | "Worm's Eye" | "Dutch/Canted Angle
                   (specify degrees)" | "Over-the-Shoulder Angle"
CAMERA_MOVEMENT = "Static/Locked" | "Pan (specify direction, L-R or R-L)" |
                   "Tilt (up/down)" | "Dolly In/Out" | "Truck L/R" |
                   "Tracking Shot (follow subject)" | "Crane/Jib
                   (up/down/arc)" | "Handheld (specify shake intensity:
                   subtle/moderate/aggressive)" | "Steadicam (smooth
                   float)" | "Whip Pan" | "Zoom (specify in/out, and
                   whether combined with dolly for a 'dolly zoom/vertigo
                   effect')" | "Orbit/Arc around subject"
MOVEMENT_SPEED  = "slow/deliberate" | "moderate" | "fast/urgent" |
                   descriptive real-world pacing comparison if helpful
FOCAL_LENGTH    = specify actual mm equivalent (e.g. "24mm wide,"
                   "50mm normal/natural perspective," "85mm portrait
                   compression," "135mm+ telephoto compression")
APERTURE_FEEL   = "shallow depth of field (heavy background blur, f/1.4-
                   2.8 equivalent)" | "moderate depth (f/4-5.6
                   equivalent)" | "deep focus (everything sharp, f/8+
                   equivalent)"
FOCUS_BEHAVIOR  = "static focus on subject" | "rack focus from
                   {SUBJECT_A} to {SUBJECT_B}" | "soft focus pull
                   maintaining subject throughout movement" | "focus
                   breathing intentional for effect"
MOTION_QUALITY  = "standard cinematic 24fps motion blur" | "crisp
                   high-shutter clarity (sports/action feel)" |
                   "slow-motion (specify speed ramp, e.g. 2x, 4x)" |
                   "time-lapse/hyperlapse quality"
BEAT_NUMBER/DESCRIPTION = pulled directly from File 04 output

INPUT STRUCTURE
----------------
SCENE_BEATS: <required, full beat-by-beat breakdown from File 04>
VISUAL_STYLE_REFERENCE: <optional but recommended — director/DP tonal
  reference for calibration, e.g. "Emmanuel Lubezki-style unbroken takes"
  or "Deakins-style precise, motivated composition">
GENRE: <required, inherited from File 01, affects default camera language —
  horror favors different angle logic than romance>
TARGET_AI_MODEL: <optional, affects how explicit movement descriptions need
  to be, since different platforms parse camera language with varying
  precision>

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
-----------------
For EACH beat in the scene:
1. BEAT REFERENCE (number + one-line description, restated from File 04)
2. SHOT SPECIFICATION (type, angle, movement + speed, lens + aperture,
   focus behavior, motion quality — every field filled, no defaults left
   implicit)
3. DRAMATIC RATIONALE (one sentence justifying the combined choice)
4. COMPILED GENERATION-READY PROMPT LINE (a single dense sentence
   combining all technical specs into natural language ready to paste into
   an AI video generator, e.g. "Medium close-up, slight low angle, slow
   dolly-in, 50mm lens, shallow depth of field with soft background blur,
   focus locked on subject's eyes, standard cinematic motion")

After all beats:
5. SHOT VARIETY CHECK (confirms the scene doesn't overuse any single shot
   type/angle combination — flags repetition that would read as visually
   monotonous)
6. COVERAGE NOTE (identifies whether any beat is missing a shot that
   editorial (File 15) would need for proper cutting — e.g. reaction shots,
   establishing shots)

PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
---------------------------
- Every camera choice must be MOTIVATED, never arbitrary. A low angle isn't
  just "cool" — it must serve a specific purpose (power dynamic, scale,
  disorientation). If a choice can't be justified in the Dramatic Rationale
  sentence, it's the wrong choice — pick again.
- Never combine an EXTREME CLOSE-UP with FAST HANDHELD unless the beat
  specifically calls for chaos/disorientation — this combination is
  physically nauseating on screen without narrative justification.
- Match lens choice to emotional intimacy: wider lenses (24-35mm) for
  environmental/establishing context and alienation effects; longer lenses
  (85mm+) for intimacy, compression, and emotional close focus. This is not
  an absolute rule but must be deliberately broken, not defaulted past.
- Movement must have a clear START and END state — "dolly in" is
  incomplete; specify what the shot begins on and what it ends on.
- For dialogue-heavy beats, favor stable, motivated camera work (locked,
  slow push, or classical OTS shot-reverse-shot) — excessive movement
  during dialogue reads as amateurish unless the scene's emotional
  register specifically calls for instability.
- Dutch/canted angles must always specify exact degree of tilt (typically
  5-15 degrees for subtle unease, 15-45 for extreme disorientation) — never
  leave this vague.
- The COMPILED GENERATION-READY PROMPT LINE is the actual deliverable most
  users will copy-paste — it must stand alone as a complete, unambiguous
  instruction without requiring the reader to cross-reference the itemized
  breakdown above it.

BEST PRACTICES
----------------
- Run the SHOT VARIETY CHECK as a hard gate — if 4+ consecutive beats use
  the same shot type/angle combination, force a revision even if each
  individual choice was individually justified, because monotony itself
  becomes a viewing problem.
- For AI video generation specifically, understand that different models
  interpret camera movement language with different fidelity as of current
  tooling — simpler, more explicit movement descriptions (e.g. "slow dolly
  in" rather than "creeping insidiously toward") generally produce more
  reliable results. When in doubt, favor mechanical precision over poetic
  language in the COMPILED GENERATION-READY PROMPT LINE specifically (poetic
  language is fine in the Dramatic Rationale, which is for human reference
  only).
- Build a per-project "camera language document" by compiling all Camera
  Master outputs in sequence — this becomes your shot list and reveals
  pacing/rhythm patterns across the whole piece before a single frame is
  generated.
- Cross-reference every shot against File 08 (Composition & Framing) — 
  Camera Master determines shot TYPE and MOVEMENT, Composition determines
  what's placed WHERE within that shot; the two must be generated as a
  pair for full specification.
- When uncertain between two shot choices, default toward the choice that
  gives editorial (File 15) more flexibility — a wider shot can always be
  punched in during editing conceptually, but a close-up that turns out
  too tight limits options.

EXAMPLE INPUT
---------------
SCENE_BEATS: [Beat-by-beat breakdown from File 04 example, Scene 2 — the
photo frame reach]
VISUAL_STYLE_REFERENCE: Deakins-style precise, motivated composition;
stillness holds weight
GENRE: Techno-thriller with family drama undertones
TARGET_AI_MODEL: Runway Gen and Kling (dialogue/performance-focused)

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
-----------------
BEAT 1 (0:00-0:03, controlled shock reading the address):
Shot Specification — Medium Close-Up, Eye Level angle, Static/Locked
camera, 50mm lens, moderate depth of field (f/4 equivalent, background
softly present but not eliminated), static focus on Marcus's eyes,
standard cinematic 24fps motion.
Dramatic Rationale: A locked eye-level MCU denies the audience any
directorial "comment" on the moment — the stillness of the frame mirrors
Marcus's own frozen control, letting his micro-expression carry the entire
beat without camera interference.
Compiled Prompt Line: "Medium close-up, eye level, static locked camera,
50mm lens, moderate depth of field with softly present background, sharp
focus on subject's eyes, standard cinematic motion, no camera movement."

BEAT 2 (0:03-0:06, aborted reach toward photo frame):
Shot Specification — Extreme Close-Up (Insert), slight High Angle
(looking down at the desk surface), slow Dolly In combined with a subtle
Rack Focus from Marcus's face to his hand, 85mm lens, shallow depth of
field (f/2 equivalent, heavy blur outside focal plane), focus racks
precisely as the hand enters frame, standard cinematic motion with slight
motion blur on the hand's movement.
Dramatic Rationale: The high angle and rack focus physically enact the
audience's attention being pulled toward the involuntary gesture, exactly
as Marcus's own control is being pulled toward it — camera behavior mirrors
psychological behavior.
Compiled Prompt Line: "Extreme close-up insert shot, slight high angle
looking down, slow dolly in with rack focus shifting from face to hand,
85mm lens, shallow depth of field with heavy background blur, focus pulls
sharply onto hand as it enters frame, subtle natural motion blur."

BEAT 3 (0:06-0:08, forced composure reasserting):
Shot Specification — Medium Close-Up, Eye Level (return to Beat 1's
framing for bookend effect), Static/Locked, 50mm lens, moderate depth of
field, static focus, standard cinematic motion.
Dramatic Rationale: Returning to Beat 1's exact framing visually confirms
the "reset" — Marcus's composure reasserting itself is mirrored by the
camera itself returning to its earlier, controlled position.
Compiled Prompt Line: "Medium close-up, eye level, static locked camera,
50mm lens, moderate depth of field, sharp focus on subject's face, standard
cinematic motion, matching earlier framing exactly."

SHOT VARIETY CHECK: Passed — three beats use three distinct shot type/angle
combinations (MCU-eyelevel-static, ECU-highangle-dolly, MCU-eyelevel-
static-bookend), with the bookend repetition being intentional and
motivated rather than accidental monotony.

COVERAGE NOTE: Scene would benefit from one additional insert shot of the
photo frame itself (face-down, untouched) at the end of Beat 3 for
editorial flexibility in File 15 — flagged as optional B-roll.
