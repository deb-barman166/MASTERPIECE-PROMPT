================================================================================
ULTIMATE HOLLYWOOD AI FILMMAKING PROMPT LIBRARY
FILE 01: STORY GENERATOR
================================================================================

TITLE
-----
The Hollywood Story Architect — Feature-Grade Narrative Generation Engine

PURPOSE
-------
Generate a complete, structurally sound story premise and outline suitable as
the foundation for a screenplay, short film, trailer, or AI-generated video
narrative. This template forces three-act (or chosen alternative) structure,
character want/need clarity, thematic coherence, and genre-appropriate stakes
BEFORE any scene or dialogue writing begins. It is the root node all other 19
templates in this library build on — Screenplay, Scene, and Character
templates all assume a story generated here already exists.

Use this when you have: a genre, a rough idea, a theme, or nothing but a mood
— and need Hollywood-level narrative architecture out the other end.

PROMPT FORMULA
---------------
[ROLE ANCHOR] + [STORY SEED] + [STRUCTURAL FRAMEWORK] + [GENRE CONSTRAINTS] +
[CHARACTER CORE] + [THEME] + [TONE/COMP TITLES] + [OUTPUT FORMAT] +
[LENGTH TARGET] + [CONSTRAINTS/EXCLUSIONS]

Master formula string (fill every {VARIABLE}):

"Act as a {ROLE} with credits comparable to {COMP_WRITERS}. Generate a
{STRUCTURE_TYPE} story in the {GENRE} genre, tonally similar to
{COMP_TITLES}. The core seed is: {STORY_SEED}. The protagonist is defined by
an external WANT of {EXTERNAL_WANT} and an internal NEED of {INTERNAL_NEED},
currently obstructed by {FLAW_OR_WOUND}. The central theme to dramatize
(never state directly) is {THEME}. Antagonistic force: {ANTAGONIST_TYPE}.
World/setting: {SETTING}. Target format: {TARGET_FORMAT} at approximately
{LENGTH_TARGET}. Structure the output using {STRUCTURAL_FRAMEWORK} with
explicit act/beat labels. Exclude: {EXCLUSIONS}. Rating/content ceiling:
{CONTENT_RATING}."

VARIABLES
---------
ROLE                    = e.g. "veteran Hollywood story consultant / studio
                           development executive / Pixar story artist"
COMP_WRITERS            = 2-3 real or archetypal writer styles for calibration
                           (e.g. "Aaron Sorkin's dialogue-driven tension,
                           Christopher Nolan's structural ambition")
STORY_SEED              = the raw idea, one to three sentences, can be as
                           vague as a single image or as specific as a logline
STRUCTURE_TYPE           = "short film" | "feature film" | "trailer concept" |
                           "single AI-generated video sequence" | "TV pilot"
GENRE                   = primary genre + optional subgenre
                           (e.g. "sci-fi thriller with noir elements")
COMP_TITLES             = 2-4 tonal reference films (for calibration only,
                           never for copying plot/IP)
EXTERNAL_WANT           = concrete, visible goal the protagonist pursues
INTERNAL_NEED           = the true psychological resolution required
FLAW_OR_WOUND           = the internal obstacle causing the want/need gap
THEME                   = the thesis statement the story argues for/against
ANTAGONIST_TYPE         = "person" | "system" | "self" | "nature" |
                           "concept/force" — with one line of specificity
SETTING                 = time period, location, world rules if speculative
TARGET_FORMAT           = "feature outline" | "short film beat sheet" |
                           "60-second trailer narrative" | "3-scene sequence"
LENGTH_TARGET           = word count or runtime target
STRUCTURAL_FRAMEWORK    = "Three-Act Structure" | "Save the Cat 15-beat" |
                           "Hero's Journey (12 stage)" | "Kishōtenketsu" |
                           "Freytag's Pyramid" | "Non-linear/fractured"
EXCLUSIONS              = tropes, plot devices, or content to avoid
CONTENT_RATING          = "G" | "PG-13 equivalent" | "R equivalent" |
                           "platform-safe (no violence/gore/sexual content)"

INPUT STRUCTURE
----------------
Provide as a structured block. Minimum viable input is STORY_SEED + GENRE +
TARGET_FORMAT — everything else can be auto-inferred, but explicit input
always outranks inference.

STORY_SEED: <required>
GENRE: <required>
STRUCTURE_TYPE: <required>
TARGET_FORMAT: <required>
LENGTH_TARGET: <optional, default = format-appropriate standard>
COMP_TITLES: <optional, improves tonal accuracy>
THEME: <optional, will be inferred from seed if omitted>
CONTENT_RATING: <optional, default = PG-13 equivalent>
EXCLUSIONS: <optional>

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
-----------------
The generator must return output in this exact shape, every time:

1. LOGLINE (1-2 sentences, industry-standard format: protagonist + want +
   obstacle + stakes)
2. THEME STATEMENT (1 sentence, the argument the story makes)
3. PROTAGONIST CORE
   - Want (external)
   - Need (internal)
   - Flaw/Wound
   - Arc direction (positive/negative/flat)
4. ANTAGONISTIC FORCE (what opposes the want, and why it's thematically
   necessary, not arbitrary)
5. STRUCTURAL BREAKDOWN (formatted per chosen STRUCTURAL_FRAMEWORK, with
   every beat labeled and given 1-3 sentences — e.g. for 3-Act: Setup,
   Inciting Incident, Break into Two, Midpoint, Break into Three, Climax,
   Resolution)
6. STAKES LADDER (what happens if protagonist fails — personal, then
   relational, then world-level if applicable)
7. TONAL NOTES (2-3 sentences on how this should FEEL on screen —
   pacing, mood, visual register)
8. ONE-PARAGRAPH PROSE SYNOPSIS (200-400 words, readable standalone)

PROFESSIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
---------------------------
- Never let theme be stated as dialogue ("the theme is family") — it must be
  DRAMATIZED through plot choices only. Flag this constraint explicitly in
  the generated output as a rule the writer (human or AI) must follow
  downstream.
- Want and Need must be in tension, not alignment. If they're the same thing,
  reject the input and request clarification — that's a structural error,
  not a valid premise.
- Every antagonistic force must be the SPECIFIC pressure that exposes the
  protagonist's flaw. Generic obstacles ("a villain who is evil") must be
  rejected and reworked into something thematically load-bearing.
- Stakes must escalate across the structural framework, never plateau.
- Do not resolve the story with unearned coincidence, deus ex machina, or an
  antagonist that simply gives up. The resolution must be a direct
  consequence of the protagonist's arc (they change and that change causes
  the outcome).
- If STRUCTURE_TYPE is "short film" or a single AI-video sequence, compress
  aggressively — cut all subplots, keep exactly one A-story, and make the
  inciting incident occur in the first 10% of runtime.
- Genre conventions must be honored (a thriller needs escalating tension
  beats; comedy needs a "dark night of the soul" that's still funny in tone)
  but never at the expense of the specific story's internal logic.

BEST PRACTICES
----------------
- Feed COMP_TITLES even when uncertain — even two loosely-related films sharp-
  ens tonal calibration enormously versus leaving it blank.
- For AI video generation downstream (Sora/Veo/Kling etc.), keep
  TARGET_FORMAT tight (3-7 scenes max) — these models generate short clips,
  so an overloaded story structure will get truncated badly at the Scene
  Generator stage. Match story scope to eventual output medium NOW.
- Run the STAKES LADDER check manually: if you can't articulate what's lost
  at each level, the story isn't ready to move to Screenplay Generator (File
  02) yet — regenerate with a sharper FLAW_OR_WOUND.
- Reuse THEME and PROTAGONIST CORE outputs verbatim as inputs into every
  downstream template (Character Design, Dialogue Generator, Emotion &
  Acting) to guarantee narrative consistency across the full pipeline.
- When exploring options, generate 3 LOGLINE variants at low cost before
  committing to a full structural breakdown — cheaper to iterate at the
  logline stage than after full generation.

EXAMPLE INPUT
---------------
STORY_SEED: A retired bomb-disposal veteran now defuses viral disinformation
for a living, and discovers the next "bomb" was built by his own daughter.
GENRE: Techno-thriller with family drama undertones
STRUCTURE_TYPE: Short film
TARGET_FORMAT: 8-scene short film beat sheet
LENGTH_TARGET: ~12 minute runtime
COMP_TITLES: The Hurt Locker (tension craft), Ordinary People (family rupture)
CONTENT_RATING: PG-13 equivalent

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
-----------------
LOGLINE: A disinformation-defusal expert with a lethal talent for detecting
lies must decide whether to expose the viral conspiracy his estranged
daughter engineered, or protect her from the consequences of her own genius.

THEME STATEMENT: The truth you're trained to protect can't save the people
you love if you never learned to actually listen to them.

PROTAGONIST CORE:
- Want: Identify and neutralize the disinformation source before it triggers
  real-world violence.
- Need: Reconnect with his daughter as a person, not a threat to be managed.
- Flaw/Wound: He treats every relationship like ordnance — defuse, contain,
  move on — since surviving his last deployment.
- Arc: Positive — moves from tactical detachment to emotional presence.

ANTAGONISTIC FORCE: His daughter's ideology isn't villainy — it's a mirror
of his own binary, threat-assessment worldview, radicalized. Opposing her
means confronting the parenting philosophy that made her this way.

STRUCTURAL BREAKDOWN (3-Act, 8 beats):
1. SETUP — He defuses a minor disinfo campaign with clinical precision;
   we see the isolation his skill has cost him.
2. INCITING INCIDENT — Forensic trace on the new viral conspiracy leads to
   his daughter's IP address.
3. BREAK INTO TWO — He goes to confront her instead of reporting her.
4. RISING ACTION — Their conversation reveals she built it to expose HIS
   employer's own disinformation operation — she thinks she's the hero.
5. MIDPOINT — He realizes she's right about the target, wrong about the
   method; the "threat" reframes as a plea for his attention.
6. BREAK INTO THREE — He must choose: report her (protect the mission) or
   help her do it right (protect her).
7. CLIMAX — He walks into his own agency and blows the whistle himself,
   using her research but taking the fall.
8. RESOLUTION — She visits him after, first real conversation in years —
   not about ordnance, about her.

STAKES LADDER: Personal (his career, his freedom) → Relational (his last
chance with his daughter) → Systemic (implied: the disinformation operation
his own agency runs continues unless someone exposes it).

TONAL NOTES: Coiled, procedural tension in the first half (handheld,
clinical framing) dissolving into something warmer and more vulnerable by
the resolution — the camera itself should "exhale."

PROSE SYNOPSIS: [200-400 word version follows the beats above in flowing
prose — omitted here for brevity, generator produces this in full.]

ADVANCED VERSION
-------------------
For multi-model pipelines and studio-grade development, extend the base
formula with:

"Additionally generate: (a) THREE ALTERNATE MIDPOINT TWISTS ranked by
thematic strength, (b) a SUBTEXT MAP showing what each major scene is
'really about' beneath its surface action, (c) a COMPARABLE BOX
OFFICE/AUDIENCE POSITIONING paragraph for pitch purposes, (d) a
FRANCHISE/SEQUEL POTENTIAL note if relevant, (e) explicit AI-VIDEO-MODEL
SCENE COUNT RECOMMENDATION — given {TARGET_PLATFORM: Sora/Veo/Kling/Runway/
Pika/Luma/Hailuo}, recommend the maximum number of distinct generated clips
this story should be broken into to remain coherent and character-
consistent across generations, accounting for that platform's known clip-
length and continuity constraints as of your knowledge."

This Advanced Version output feeds directly and losslessly into File 04
(Scene Generator) and File 16 (Video Generation Master) without requiring
re-derivation of story fundamentals.
