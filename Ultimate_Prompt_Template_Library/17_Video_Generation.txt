================================================================================
05 — VIDEO GENERATOR
================================================================================

TITLE
AI Video Generation Master Template

PURPOSE
Direct text-to-video models with film-production-level specificity
(shot, motion, camera, timing) to produce coherent short clips instead of
generic or physically inconsistent motion.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Sora, Runway Gen-3/Frames, Veo, Kling, Pika, Luma Dream Machine, Hailuo

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Intermediate → Expert

WHEN TO USE
- B-roll generation, concept previsualization, social content, ad
  creative, music video segments, establishing shots

EXPECTED OUTPUT
A short video clip (typically 4-20 seconds depending on platform) with
coherent motion, consistent subject appearance across frames, and
intended camera behavior.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUBJECT + APPEARANCE
  ↓
ACTION (specific, single continuous motion)
  ↓
ENVIRONMENT
  ↓
CAMERA MOVEMENT (static / pan / dolly / orbit / handheld)
  ↓
SHOT TYPE (wide / medium / close-up)
  ↓
LIGHTING & TIME OF DAY
  ↓
STYLE (cinematic / documentary / animated / hyperreal)
  ↓
PACING (slow-motion / real-time / timelapse)
  ↓
DURATION TARGET
  ↓
NEGATIVE PROMPT (artifacts, morphing, extra limbs)

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Action must be singular and specific: video models struggle with
  compound actions ("runs then jumps then waves") — one clear motion per
  clip produces far more coherent results.
- Camera Movement: unspecified camera behavior often results in
  unintended drift or an unstable "breathing" frame; explicit instruction
  anchors it.
- Pacing: without pacing instructions, models default to real-time,
  which may not match creative intent (e.g. product reveals often want
  slow-motion).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{SUBJECT} — main subject with appearance detail
{ACTION} — one specific continuous motion
{ENVIRONMENT} — setting
{CAMERA_MOVE} — static / slow pan left / dolly in / orbit / handheld shake
{SHOT_TYPE} — wide / medium / close-up / macro
{LIGHTING} — time of day, light quality
{STYLE} — cinematic / documentary / anime / claymation / hyperreal
{PACING} — real-time / slow-motion 2x / timelapse
{DURATION} — target clip length in seconds

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"{SHOT_TYPE} of {SUBJECT} {ACTION} in {ENVIRONMENT}. Camera: {CAMERA_MOVE}.
Lighting: {LIGHTING}. Style: {STYLE}, {PACING}. Duration: ~{DURATION}
seconds. Avoid: morphing, flickering, extra limbs, unnatural motion blur,
text artifacts."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Video clip matching specified duration, camera behavior, and style
- Consistent subject identity across all frames (no morphing)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Is the requested action physically simple enough for current video
   model capability (single continuous motion, not a multi-step sequence)?
2. Does the camera movement serve the shot's purpose (e.g. slow dolly-in
   for tension, orbit for product reveal)?
3. Is the environment description simple enough to remain stable across
   frames, or does its complexity risk background morphing?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
If output shows morphing, flickering, or physically impossible motion:
- Simplify the action to a smaller, more contained motion
- Reduce camera movement complexity (static often outperforms complex
  moves on lower-tier models)
- Shorten requested duration, since coherence degrades over longer
  generated spans

REFLECTION LAYER
"Does this clip look like it was filmed, or does something about the
physics/continuity immediately signal 'AI-generated' in a way that
undermines the intended use?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Not typically applicable for pure creative video generation; relevant
only when depicting real, identifiable people, events, or branded
products, in which case verify use rights and platform policy compliance.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Single, specific, continuous action (not compound)
[ ] Camera movement explicitly stated
[ ] Shot type specified
[ ] Lighting/time of day specified
[ ] Pacing specified
[ ] Negative prompt addresses known model failure modes
[ ] Duration realistic for platform capability

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
- Shorter, simpler prompts often outperform overloaded ones for current-
  generation video models — describe one clear moment, not a whole scene.
- For subject consistency across multiple clips, use an identical
  appearance-description block in every prompt in the sequence.
- Test with static camera first to validate subject/action fidelity
  before adding camera movement complexity.

COMMON MISTAKES
- Requesting multi-step narrative action in one clip ("walks in, sits
  down, opens laptop, starts typing")
- Over-specifying camera moves the model can't yet handle smoothly
  (complex compound moves, e.g. "dolly in while orbiting")
- Ignoring negative prompts, resulting in recurring limb/hand artifacts
- Requesting on-screen legible text (still unreliable across most models)

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
- Storyboard multi-clip sequences with consistent character/environment
  description blocks before generating, to maintain visual continuity.
- Generate multiple takes of the same prompt and select the most
  physically coherent result rather than accepting the first output.
- Pair AI video with traditional editing (color grade, sound design,
  cuts) — raw AI clips rarely work "as-is" without post-production polish.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Medium shot of a barista in a green apron steaming milk in a stainless
steel pitcher at a modern coffee bar. Camera: slow dolly in. Lighting:
warm morning light through a large window, soft shadows. Style:
cinematic, real-time pacing. Duration: ~6 seconds. Avoid: morphing,
flickering, extra limbs, unnatural motion blur, text artifacts.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[Video: A 6-second clip where the camera slowly moves closer to a barista
steaming milk, steam visibly rising, warm window light catching the
pitcher's surface, background softly out of focus, motion physically
coherent throughout with no visible artifacting on hands or pitcher.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add: multi-shot sequence planning (3-5 clips forming a coherent short
scene with matched lighting/color continuity across shots), explicit
frame-rate/shutter-style description (e.g. "24fps film look with subtle
motion blur" vs. "60fps hyperreal"), and a fallback: "If subject identity
drifts mid-clip, regenerate with a tighter, more specific appearance
description and reduce total motion complexity."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in: first-frame/last-frame conditioning instructions (for models
supporting keyframe-to-keyframe generation), camera-lens simulation
(focal length, depth-of-field behavior over the clip), and audio-
sync-ready pacing notes (marking beats in the motion for later music/SFX
alignment in edit).

ENTERPRISE VERSION
Add: brand-safe content constraints (no incidental logos, no
unintentional trademarked elements in background), batch-generation
specification for producing consistent B-roll libraries across a
campaign, and a production pipeline note on required human review/
editing pass before any AI-generated video reaches a public-facing
deliverable.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"[SHOT_TYPE] of [SUBJECT] [ACTION] in [ENVIRONMENT]. [CAMERA_MOVE] camera,
[LIGHTING], [STYLE] style, [PACING]."
Works as a base across Sora, Runway, Veo, and Kling; each platform has
unique syntax extensions (Runway supports explicit camera-control
sliders, Kling supports motion-brush region control) worth layering on
top of this universal core once a platform is selected.
