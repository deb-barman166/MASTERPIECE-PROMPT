================================================================================
06 — CINEMATIC VIDEO
================================================================================

TITLE
Cinematic AI Video Master Template

PURPOSE
Produce film-grade AI video sequences with deliberate cinematography
language (lens choice, color grade, composition rules) for narrative or
advertising work that must feel directed rather than generated.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Sora, Runway Gen-3, Veo, Kling (Pro tier)

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Expert

WHEN TO USE
- Film previsualization, high-end ad concepts, narrative short scenes,
  music video sequences, trailer-style content

EXPECTED OUTPUT
A clip with intentional cinematographic choices (specific lens behavior,
color grade, framing) that reads as directed footage.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SCENE CONTEXT (what story beat this is)
  ↓
SUBJECT + WARDROBE/APPEARANCE
  ↓
BLOCKING (where subject is/moves within frame)
  ↓
LENS CHOICE (focal length + aperture behavior)
  ↓
CAMERA MOVEMENT (motivated, not arbitrary)
  ↓
COMPOSITION RULE (rule of thirds, leading lines, symmetry)
  ↓
LIGHTING SETUP (key/fill/rim, practical vs. motivated)
  ↓
COLOR GRADE REFERENCE (film stock or grading style)
  ↓
EMOTIONAL SUBTEXT (what the shot should make viewer feel)
  ↓
SOUND DESIGN IMPLICATION (for post-production alignment)

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Scene Context: cinematography choices should serve story, not exist
  decoratively — this section forces every technical choice to be
  justified by narrative purpose.
- Motivated Camera Movement: arbitrary camera moves read as "generated";
  movement tied to a story reason (revealing information, following
  action, building tension) reads as directed.
- Color Grade Reference: without this, models default to a flat,
  slightly oversaturated "AI look" — anchoring to a known film stock or
  grading style produces more intentional results.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{SCENE_BEAT} — the narrative moment this shot represents
{SUBJECT_APPEARANCE} — detailed subject/wardrobe description
{BLOCKING} — subject's position/movement in frame
{LENS} — e.g. 35mm deep focus, 85mm shallow DOF, wide 24mm
{CAMERA_MOVE} — motivated movement description
{COMPOSITION} — rule of thirds / centered symmetry / leading lines
{LIGHTING_SETUP} — key light direction + fill ratio + practical sources
{COLOR_GRADE} — reference style (e.g. "Roger Deakins cool desaturated,"
  "Wes Anderson pastel symmetry," "Fincher teal-black high contrast")
{EMOTION} — the intended felt response

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Scene: {SCENE_BEAT}. {SUBJECT_APPEARANCE}, {BLOCKING}. Shot on {LENS}.
Camera: {CAMERA_MOVE}, composed using {COMPOSITION}. Lighting:
{LIGHTING_SETUP}. Color grade: {COLOR_GRADE}. The shot should evoke
{EMOTION}. Cinematic film quality, 24fps motion characteristics."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Clip with deliberate framing, motivated camera behavior, and consistent
  color grade throughout

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Does every technical choice (lens, movement, light) serve the
   emotional subtext, or is any element arbitrary/decorative?
2. Is the color grade reference specific enough to be reproducible, or
   is it a vague term like "cinematic" that the model will interpret
   generically?
3. Does the blocking make sense within the frame given the chosen lens
   and composition rule?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
If the result reads as generic rather than directed:
- Replace vague style words ("cinematic," "epic") with specific,
  concrete technical/reference language
- Tie the camera movement explicitly to what it reveals or follows
- Increase color grade specificity (name actual film stocks, colorists,
  or directors' known visual signatures)

REFLECTION LAYER
"If I removed the text prompt and just watched this clip, would the
emotional intent be clear from the visual choices alone?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Not applicable for original narrative content; if referencing a specific
director/cinematographer's style by name, this is used as a stylistic
descriptor, not a claim of affiliation or endorsement.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Every technical choice ties to narrative/emotional purpose
[ ] Lens choice specified with focal length
[ ] Camera movement is motivated, not arbitrary
[ ] Composition rule explicitly named
[ ] Lighting setup includes key + fill + practical logic
[ ] Color grade reference is specific and reproducible
[ ] Emotional subtext is clearly stated

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
- Use real cinematography vocabulary (dolly, crane, Steadicam, rack
  focus) rather than generic terms — models trained on film data respond
  more precisely to correct terminology.
- Anchor color grade to a specific, well-documented visual style rather
  than an adjective.
- Keep blocking simple; complex multi-mark blocking rarely survives
  current video-generation coherence limits.

COMMON MISTAKES
- Using "cinematic" as a catch-all without specifying what that means
  technically
- Choosing camera movement for spectacle rather than narrative purpose
- Neglecting color grade, resulting in default flat AI-video color
- Overcomplicating blocking beyond current model coherence capability

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
- Build a "look book" of 5-10 reference color grades/compositions you
  return to, refined over many generations, for consistent visual
  authorship across a project.
- Sequence shots using shot-reverse-shot or establishing-to-detail logic
  even in short-form AI content — it reads as more intentional than
  isolated single shots.
- Always pair the final AI output with a proper color grade pass in
  post — even a well-prompted clip benefits from finishing work.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scene: the protagonist realizes she's been betrayed by her closest ally.
A woman in a rain-damp trench coat stands motionless in a dim hallway,
her expression shifting from confusion to quiet devastation. Shot on
50mm, shallow depth of field. Camera: slow push-in, motivated by the
emotional realization landing. Composed using centered symmetry with the
hallway's vanishing point behind her. Lighting: single practical
overhead bulb as key, deep shadow fill, cool blue ambient. Color grade:
Fincher-style desaturated teal-black high contrast. The shot should evoke
quiet devastation and isolation. Cinematic film quality, 24fps motion.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[Video: A slow, steady push-in on a woman standing centered in a long,
shadow-heavy hallway, single overhead bulb casting a hard pool of light
around her as the background falls into cool blue-black shadow. Her
expression subtly shifts across the clip's duration. The symmetrical
hallway framing and desaturated teal-black grade create a controlled,
isolating atmosphere consistent throughout.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add: multi-shot sequence with shot-to-shot continuity rules (consistent
wardrobe, lighting logic, and color grade across a 3-5 shot scene),
explicit aspect ratio choice tied to genre convention (2.39:1 for epic/
drama, 16:9 for commercial), and a fallback: "If emotional read is
unclear, simplify blocking and increase reliance on lighting contrast to
carry the subtext."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in: full shot-list structure (wide establishing → medium →
close-up → insert, each with individual technical specs), sound-design
notes embedded per shot (diegetic vs. score cues) for editor handoff, and
explicit reference to real film grain/halation characteristics for
texture authenticity.

ENTERPRISE VERSION
Add: brand/campaign visual-consistency rules across multiple cinematic
spots, legal-safe casting/wardrobe description standards (avoiding
implied real-person likeness), and a formal creative-brief-to-shot-list
pipeline document for agency/client sign-off before generation begins.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"Cinematic shot: [SUBJECT_APPEARANCE], [BLOCKING], shot on [LENS],
[CAMERA_MOVE], [LIGHTING_SETUP], [COLOR_GRADE] color grade, evoking
[EMOTION]."
Works across Sora, Runway, and Veo; Runway currently offers the most
granular camera-control parameters worth layering on top for precise
motivated movement.
