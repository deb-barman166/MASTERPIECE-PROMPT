================================================================================
48 — INTERVIEW
================================================================================

TITLE
Interview Preparation Master Template

PURPOSE
Prepare specific, structured interview answers (using STAR method for
behavioral questions, and genuine problem-solving reasoning for
technical questions) grounded in real experience — rather than generic,
memorized-sounding responses that fail to differentiate a candidate.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Claude, GPT-4/5, Gemini

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Beginner → Advanced

WHEN TO USE
- Behavioral interview prep, technical interview prep, mock interview
  practice, answer structuring, post-interview reflection

EXPECTED OUTPUT
Structured, specific interview answers grounded in real personal
experience (behavioral) or clear reasoning process (technical), tailored
to the specific role/company context.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INTERVIEW TYPE (behavioral / technical / case) → TARGET ROLE/COMPANY →
QUESTION → RAW EXPERIENCE (what actually happened, for behavioral) →
STRUCTURE (STAR for behavioral; reasoning process for technical) →
RELEVANCE TO ROLE → PRACTICE FEEDBACK LOOP

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Raw Experience first: asking for a polished answer before capturing
  what actually happened produces generic, forgettable responses even
  when the underlying experience was strong — specifics must be
  extracted first, then structured.
- STAR Structure (Situation-Task-Action-Result): behavioral answers
  without this structure tend to be either too vague (no concrete
  situation) or too long (no clear result) — the structure forces both
  specificity and conciseness.
- Reasoning Process for technical: interviewers evaluate HOW a candidate
  thinks through a problem, not just whether they reach the right
  answer — technical prep must practice narrating reasoning aloud, not
  just solving silently.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{INTERVIEW_TYPE} {TARGET_ROLE} {QUESTION} {RAW_EXPERIENCE}

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Help me prepare for this {INTERVIEW_TYPE} interview question for a
{TARGET_ROLE} role: '{QUESTION}'. Here's what actually happened (raw):
{RAW_EXPERIENCE}. Structure this into a {STAR method for behavioral /
clear step-by-step reasoning for technical} answer. Keep it specific and
concise — not generic. Tailor the framing toward what matters for this
specific role. After the answer, give me feedback on where it could be
stronger."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Structured answer (STAR or reasoning-process format)
- Specific, not generic
- Brief feedback on strengthening it further

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Is this answer specific to the candidate's real experience, or does
   it read as a generic template that could apply to almost anyone?
2. For behavioral answers, is the Result quantified/concrete, or does it
   trail off vaguely without a clear outcome?
3. For technical answers, does the reasoning process demonstrate actual
   problem-solving thinking (considering tradeoffs, edge cases), or does
   it jump straight to a memorized-sounding solution?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
Push for more specific detail anywhere the answer feels generic; ensure
the Result section has a concrete, ideally quantified outcome; for
technical answers, add explicit reasoning narration ("I'd first
consider X because...") if the answer currently jumps straight to a
conclusion.

REFLECTION LAYER
"Would this answer sound distinctly like MY experience if I said it
aloud in an interview, or does it sound like it could be anyone's
generic answer to this question?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Not typically applicable beyond ensuring the candidate isn't
inadvertently encouraged to embellish or fabricate experience — answers
should be grounded in what genuinely happened.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Answer is specific to real experience, not generic
[ ] Behavioral: clear STAR structure with a concrete Result
[ ] Technical: reasoning process narrated, not just final answer
[ ] Tailored to the specific target role's actual priorities
[ ] Concise enough to deliver naturally in an actual interview (not an
    overlong monologue)
[ ] Feedback provided on how to strengthen further

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
Practice answers aloud, not just in writing — written answers often
don't translate naturally to spoken delivery. For technical interviews,
practice explicitly narrating your thought process even when solving
alone, since this is the skill actually being evaluated. Prepare 5-7
strong behavioral stories in advance that can flex to answer multiple
different question types, rather than one story per possible question.

COMMON MISTAKES
Generic, memorized-sounding answers with no specific detail; vague or
missing Result in behavioral answers; jumping straight to a technical
answer without narrating reasoning; answers that are too long/rambling
for natural interview delivery; not tailoring answers to what the
specific role actually values.

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
Do at least one full mock interview (timed, spoken aloud) before the
real one — writing answers and speaking them under mild pressure are
different skills. Research the specific company/role's actual priorities
beforehand so answers can be genuinely tailored, not just generically
strong.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Help me prepare for this behavioral interview question for an AI/ML
internship: 'Tell me about a time you had to debug something difficult.'
Here's what actually happened: I built a RAG system and the retrieval
was returning irrelevant results even though the embeddings looked
correct; spent a while checking the embedding model before realizing the
actual issue was that my chunking strategy was splitting text mid-
sentence, destroying semantic coherence of the chunks; fixed it by
switching to sentence-aware chunking and retrieval accuracy improved
significantly.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[STAR-structured answer:

Situation: "While building a RAG retrieval system for a personal
project, I noticed the retriever was consistently returning irrelevant
document chunks for queries I expected it to handle well."

Task: "I needed to figure out why retrieval quality was poor despite the
underlying embedding model being a well-established, capable one — my
first assumption was that something was wrong with the model itself."

Action: "I spent time verifying the embedding model was working
correctly — checking that similar texts produced similar vectors, which
they did. That ruled out the model as the issue, which pushed me to look
earlier in the pipeline. I found that my chunking logic was splitting
documents at fixed character counts without respecting sentence
boundaries, which meant many chunks contained fragments of two unrelated
sentences — destroying the semantic coherence the embedding model needed
to work with."

Result: "I switched to sentence-aware chunking, and retrieval relevance
improved significantly in my testing. The bigger lesson was that I'd
initially assumed the most sophisticated component (the embedding model)
was the likely culprit, when the actual bug was in a much simpler,
earlier stage of the pipeline — which now shapes how I debug: verify the
simplest upstream steps before assuming a complex component is at
fault."

Feedback: this is strong because it's specific and technical, but for
extra impact, if you have even an approximate before/after retrieval-
accuracy number from your testing, add it to the Result — a concrete
number would make this even more memorable than "significantly
improved" alone.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add multi-question story-mapping (identifying which 5-7 core experiences
can flexibly answer 15+ likely behavioral questions), technical whiteboard-
style practice format (narrating code reasoning without running it), and
a fallback: "If the raw experience doesn't have a strong quantified
result, help identify the most credible qualitative evidence of impact
instead of forcing a fabricated number."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in company-specific interview-style calibration (different
companies weight behavioral vs. technical differently, and specific
companies have known interview format patterns worth preparing for),
and stress-testing with likely follow-up/probing questions an
interviewer might ask after the initial answer.

ENTERPRISE VERSION
(For career coaches/university career centers) Add cohort-level mock-
interview feedback rubric for consistent evaluation across many
students, industry-specific question-bank curation, and structured
progress-tracking across multiple practice sessions.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"Help me prepare a [STAR/technical-reasoning] answer for: '[QUESTION]'
for a [TARGET_ROLE] interview. Raw experience: [RAW_EXPERIENCE]. Keep it
specific, concise, and tailored to this role. Give feedback."
