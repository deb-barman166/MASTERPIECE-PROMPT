================================================================================
46 — EMAIL
================================================================================

TITLE
Email Writing Master Template

PURPOSE
Write emails (professional, sales, or personal) that get read and acted
upon by respecting the recipient's time — clear purpose stated
immediately, appropriate length, and one clear ask — rather than
rambling messages that bury the point.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Claude, GPT-4/5, Gemini

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Beginner → Intermediate

WHEN TO USE
- Professional correspondence, cold outreach, follow-ups, difficult/
  sensitive emails, newsletters, internal team communication

EXPECTED OUTPUT
A clear, appropriately-concise email with the purpose stated early, one
specific ask, and a tone matched to the relationship and context.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RECIPIENT + RELATIONSHIP → PURPOSE (one clear thing) → CONTEXT NEEDED
(minimum necessary) → TONE (matched to relationship/context) → SPECIFIC
ASK → SUBJECT LINE (states the purpose, not vague)

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Purpose stated as ONE clear thing: emails trying to accomplish
  multiple unrelated things often result in the recipient acting on
  none of them — reducing to one primary purpose per email dramatically
  increases response/action rate.
- Specific Ask: emails that describe a situation without a clear
  request leave the recipient unsure what to do, often resulting in no
  response at all — the ask must be explicit and specific ("can you
  approve X by Friday" not "let me know your thoughts").

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{RECIPIENT} {RELATIONSHIP} {PURPOSE} {TONE} {ASK} {DEADLINE}

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Write an email to {RECIPIENT} ({RELATIONSHIP}). Purpose (one clear
thing): {PURPOSE}. Tone: {TONE}. Include only the minimum context needed
to understand the ask. Specific ask: {ASK}, {DEADLINE if applicable}.
Write a subject line that states the purpose clearly, not vague. Keep
length appropriate — as short as possible while still being complete and
appropriately warm for the relationship."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Clear subject line
- Email with purpose stated early, minimum necessary context, specific
  ask, appropriate tone

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Is there genuinely one clear purpose, or is this email trying to
   accomplish multiple unrelated things that would be better as
   separate emails?
2. Would the recipient know exactly what's being asked of them and by
   when, without needing to ask a clarifying question back?
3. Is the tone appropriately matched to the actual relationship (not
   overly formal for a close colleague, not overly casual for a first
   cold outreach)?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
Split any email trying to accomplish multiple unrelated purposes; make
any vague or implied ask explicit and specific; cut any context that
isn't strictly necessary for understanding the ask; adjust tone if
mismatched to the stated relationship.

REFLECTION LAYER
"If the recipient only read the first two sentences and the last
sentence, would they still understand what's being asked?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Not typically applicable beyond ensuring any factual claims/details
referenced in the email are accurate.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] One clear purpose, not multiple bundled asks
[ ] Purpose/ask stated early, not buried
[ ] Specific, actionable ask with deadline if relevant
[ ] Minimum necessary context only
[ ] Tone matched to actual relationship
[ ] Subject line states the purpose clearly

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
Put the ask in the first 1-2 sentences for busy recipients, with
supporting context after rather than before. Use a specific, scannable
subject line ("Approval needed: Q3 budget by Fri" beats "Quick
question"). For sensitive/difficult emails specifically, write a draft,
then reread it as if you were the recipient before sending.

COMMON MISTAKES
Burying the actual ask several paragraphs in; bundling multiple
unrelated requests into one email; vague subject lines; mismatched tone
(too casual or too formal for the relationship); unnecessarily long
emails that could be half the length without losing clarity.

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
For any email requesting action, always include an explicit deadline or
timeframe — "whenever you get a chance" often means never. Before
sending a sensitive/difficult email, wait at least a few minutes and
reread it once more, ideally after a short break, to catch tone issues.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Write an email to a potential mentor (a senior AI engineer I met briefly
at a meetup, relationship: new/cold, not close). Purpose: ask if they'd
be willing to do a 20-minute call to give feedback on my project
portfolio. Tone: respectful, appreciative of their time, not
presumptuous. Include only minimum context. Specific ask: a 20-minute
call sometime in the next 2 weeks, at their convenience. Deadline: n/a,
flexible on timing.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
Subject: 20 min for portfolio feedback? (met at [meetup name])

Hi [Name],

We spoke briefly at [meetup] a couple weeks ago about RAG systems — I
mentioned I was building out a portfolio of AI projects, and you were
kind enough to offer some thoughts if I ever wanted feedback.

I've put together a small portfolio now and would really value your
perspective, if you have 20 minutes sometime in the next couple of
weeks. Completely understand if you're too busy — no pressure either
way, and thank you for even considering it.

If you're up for it, I'm happy to work around your schedule — just let
me know what works.

Best,
[Name]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add follow-up-sequence planning (what a polite follow-up looks like if
no response after a reasonable period), and a fallback: "If the purpose
genuinely requires covering two related items, structure clearly with
one primary ask and one secondary note explicitly marked as secondary,
rather than presenting both with equal weight."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in relationship-stage-specific tone calibration (cold outreach vs.
warm introduction vs. ongoing relationship require different opening
conventions), and cultural/regional email-convention awareness for
cross-cultural professional correspondence.

ENTERPRISE VERSION
Add brand-voice compliance for customer-facing email templates, legal
review requirements for any binding commitments referenced, and
template-system design for recurring email types (onboarding sequences,
support responses) at scale.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"Write an email to [RECIPIENT] ([RELATIONSHIP]). Purpose: [PURPOSE].
Tone: [TONE]. Specific ask: [ASK]. Minimum necessary context, clear
subject line."
