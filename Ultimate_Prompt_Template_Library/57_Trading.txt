================================================================================
57 — TRADING
================================================================================

TITLE
Trading Analysis & Strategy Master Template

PURPOSE
Get factual analysis of trading concepts, backtesting methodology, and
strategy mechanics from AI assistants — building genuine understanding
of how strategies work and their historical risk/return characteristics,
rather than receiving specific buy/sell signals or confident predictions
about future price movements.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Claude, GPT-4/5, Gemini (with code execution for actual backtesting
computation)

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Intermediate → Advanced

WHEN TO USE
- Understanding trading strategy mechanics, backtesting methodology,
  risk management concept explanation, historical strategy performance
  analysis, technical indicator explanation

EXPECTED OUTPUT
Factual explanation of trading concepts/mechanics and rigorous
backtesting analysis (with explicit methodology and honest limitations),
explicitly not specific trade recommendations or price predictions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRADING QUESTION/STRATEGY → CONCEPT/MECHANISM TO EXPLAIN → BACKTEST
METHODOLOGY (if analyzing historical performance) → RISK
CHARACTERISTICS → METHODOLOGY LIMITATIONS (overfitting, survivorship
bias, look-ahead bias) → HONEST PAST-PERFORMANCE CAVEAT

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Backtest Methodology explicit: a backtest without disclosed
  methodology (what data period, what assumptions about slippage/fees,
  whether it avoids look-ahead bias) produces results that can't be
  trusted or reproduced — this must be explicit and rigorous.
- Methodology Limitations (overfitting, biases): backtested strategies
  are extremely prone to overfitting to historical data in ways that
  don't generalize forward, and to subtle biases (survivorship bias in
  the tested universe, look-ahead bias in signal calculation) — these
  must be actively checked for and disclosed, not glossed over.
- Past-Performance Caveat: historical backtest results, even
  methodologically sound ones, do not guarantee or reliably predict
  future performance — this must be stated explicitly and taken
  seriously, not as boilerplate.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{TRADING_QUESTION} {STRATEGY_MECHANICS} {BACKTEST_PERIOD} {ASSET_CLASS}

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Help me understand: {TRADING_QUESTION} regarding {STRATEGY_MECHANICS}
for {ASSET_CLASS}. If backtesting: use {BACKTEST_PERIOD} historical
data, with explicit methodology (transaction costs/slippage assumptions,
avoiding look-ahead bias). Report risk characteristics (drawdown,
volatility), not just returns. Explicitly identify methodology
limitations (overfitting risk, survivorship bias, sample size). This is
for understanding strategy mechanics and historical characteristics, not
a request for a specific trade recommendation or price prediction."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Clear mechanism/concept explanation
- (If backtesting) explicit methodology, results including risk metrics,
  and limitations
- Explicit caveat that past performance doesn't predict future results

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Is this response explaining mechanics/historical characteristics, or
   has it drifted into a specific forward-looking prediction or trade
   recommendation, which AI is not positioned to responsibly provide?
2. If a backtest is performed, is the methodology sound (no look-ahead
   bias, realistic transaction cost assumptions, reasonable sample
   size), and are its limitations honestly disclosed rather than
   presenting results as more reliable than they are?
3. Are risk characteristics (drawdown, volatility) reported alongside
   returns, or does the analysis only emphasize favorable return figures
   while underplaying risk?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
Reframe any response drifting into specific forward predictions or buy/
sell recommendations back toward factual mechanism/historical-
characteristic explanation; add explicit risk metrics if the current
draft only shows returns; add explicit overfitting/bias caveats if the
backtest methodology hasn't addressed them.

REFLECTION LAYER
"Is this helping the requester genuinely understand how this strategy
works and its historical risk/return profile, or does it risk being
read as a confident signal about what will happen next?"

FACT VERIFICATION
For actual backtesting computation, use code execution against real
historical data rather than estimating results by reasoning alone,
since approximate/hallucinated backtest numbers would be actively
misleading in a financial-decision context.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Explains mechanics/historical characteristics, not a forward
    prediction or specific trade recommendation
[ ] Backtest methodology explicit and sound (no look-ahead bias,
    realistic costs)
[ ] Risk characteristics reported alongside returns
[ ] Overfitting/bias limitations explicitly disclosed
[ ] Clear "past performance doesn't guarantee future results" caveat

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
Always compute actual backtest results via code execution against real
historical data rather than estimating, and explicitly code in
realistic transaction costs/slippage rather than assuming frictionless
trading. Report maximum drawdown and volatility alongside total/annual
return — a strategy's risk characteristics matter as much as its return
figure for genuine understanding. Test methodology soundness by
checking for look-ahead bias explicitly (would this signal have actually
been calculable using only information available at that point in time).

COMMON MISTAKES
Drifting from factual explanation into confident forward predictions;
backtests with look-ahead bias (using information not actually available
at the simulated decision point); ignoring realistic transaction
costs/slippage, inflating apparent performance; reporting only returns
without risk/drawdown characteristics; presenting backtest results
without acknowledging overfitting risk, especially for strategies with
many tunable parameters.

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
Treat any backtested strategy with many tunable parameters with extra
skepticism regarding overfitting — the more parameters tuned to
historical data, the more likely the strategy is fit to noise rather
than a genuine, persistent edge. Paper-trade (simulate without real
money) any strategy before committing real capital, regardless of how
strong the backtest looks, to observe real-world execution
characteristics.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Help me understand: how does a simple moving-average crossover strategy
work mechanically, and what were its historical risk/return
characteristics on a major stock index? Backtest period: last 10 years
of available data, asset class: broad market index. I want to understand
the mechanism and historical behavior, not a prediction about future
performance.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[Mechanism explanation: a moving-average crossover strategy generates a
buy signal when a shorter-period moving average crosses above a longer-
period moving average (interpreted as emerging upward momentum), and a
sell/short signal on the reverse crossover — it's a trend-following
approach, not a prediction of specific price levels.

BACKTEST METHODOLOGY (explicit): using [actual computed] daily
historical price data for the specified index over the stated 10-year
period; signals calculated using only data available up to and including
each decision day (no look-ahead bias); a realistic transaction cost
assumption of [X basis points] per trade applied to account for
slippage/fees, since ignoring this would inflate apparent performance
particularly for strategies with frequent trading.

RESULTS [via actual code-executed backtest]: total return, annualized
return, maximum drawdown, and volatility figures computed and reported
together — for example, illustrating that even a strategy with a
respectable total return figure may have experienced a significant
maximum drawdown at some point, which matters as much for real-world
viability as the headline return number.

LIMITATIONS: this specific 10-year period reflects whatever specific
market regime occurred during it (e.g. a strong bull market would
generally favor trend-following strategies) — results would very likely
differ meaningfully over a different historical period or market
regime, and moving-average crossover strategies are well-known to
underperform significantly during choppy/sideways markets due to
frequent false signals ("whipsaws"). This backtest used one specific
parameter combination (specific MA lengths); testing many parameter
combinations and selecting the best-performing one after the fact would
introduce significant overfitting risk not present in this single,
pre-specified test.

CAVEAT: these are historical characteristics of this specific mechanism
over this specific period — they describe the past, not a prediction of
future performance, and moving-average strategies broadly are not
guaranteed to perform similarly going forward, especially if market
conditions differ from the tested period.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add walk-forward analysis methodology (testing on rolling out-of-sample
periods rather than one static historical window, which better guards
against overfitting), Monte Carlo simulation of strategy robustness
across randomized scenarios, and a fallback: "If a strategy's apparent
edge disappears or weakens substantially under realistic transaction
costs, report this explicitly rather than presenting the frictionless
version as the headline result."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in full risk-adjusted-return metric calculation (Sharpe ratio,
Sortino ratio, Calmar ratio) for more complete risk-adjusted comparison
against a buy-and-hold benchmark specifically, regime-dependent
performance breakdown (how the strategy performed differently across
identifiable market regimes within the test period), and multiple-
hypothesis-testing correction if comparing many strategy variants.

ENTERPRISE VERSION
(For fintech/trading-education platforms) Add regulatory-compliance
review for any strategy-performance content presented to end users
(appropriate risk disclosures, no implied guarantees), backtesting-
infrastructure validation methodology for platform-wide consistency,
and clear separation between educational content and any actual
investment-advice-adjacent features requiring licensed oversight.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"Explain the mechanism of [STRATEGY_MECHANICS] and backtest its
historical risk/return characteristics on [ASSET_CLASS] over
[BACKTEST_PERIOD], with explicit methodology and limitations. Not a
future prediction or specific trade recommendation."
Requires code execution with real historical data access for credible
backtest computation.
