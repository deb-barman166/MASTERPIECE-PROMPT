================================================================================
13 — PRESENTATION
================================================================================

TITLE
AI Presentation/Slide Deck Master Template

PURPOSE
Structure and generate presentation decks that follow persuasive
narrative arcs (not just bullet-point dumps per slide), suitable for
pitches, reports, or educational talks.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Claude/GPT (content + structure), Gamma, Beautiful.ai, Tome, Canva AI
(visual slide generation)

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Beginner → Expert

WHEN TO USE
- Investor pitch decks, sales presentations, internal reports, keynote
  talks, educational lectures, project proposals

EXPECTED OUTPUT
A slide-by-slide outline (or full visual deck, depending on tool) with a
clear narrative arc, one idea per slide, and a defined call-to-action.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRESENTATION GOAL (persuade / inform / report / teach)
  ↓
AUDIENCE (who + what they already know/care about)
  ↓
CORE MESSAGE (the one thing they must remember)
  ↓
NARRATIVE ARC (problem→solution, before→after, chronological)
  ↓
SLIDE-BY-SLIDE STRUCTURE (one idea per slide)
  ↓
DATA/PROOF POINTS (what evidence supports each claim)
  ↓
VISUAL STYLE DIRECTION
  ↓
TIME CONSTRAINT (total talk length)
  ↓
CALL TO ACTION (what happens after this presentation)

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Narrative Arc: presentations that are just "slide 1: overview, slide 2:
  more info, slide 3: even more info" have no persuasive structure —
  anchoring to a known arc (problem→solution, status quo→change→benefit)
  gives the deck a reason to hold attention.
- One Idea Per Slide: the most common presentation failure is cramming
  multiple ideas onto one slide, which splits audience attention between
  listening and reading — this constraint forces proper pacing.
- Call to Action: every presentation should end with a specific,
  actionable next step, not a vague "thank you" — this section makes
  that explicit and required.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{GOAL} — persuade / inform / report / teach
{AUDIENCE} — who's watching + their existing context
{CORE_MESSAGE} — the single memorable takeaway
{ARC_TYPE} — problem-solution / before-after / chronological / data-story
{SLIDE_COUNT} — target number of slides
{TIME_LIMIT} — total presentation time
{CTA} — the specific next step requested

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Create a {SLIDE_COUNT}-slide presentation to {GOAL} for {AUDIENCE}, to
be delivered in {TIME_LIMIT}. Core message: '{CORE_MESSAGE}'. Use a
{ARC_TYPE} narrative arc. Structure one idea per slide, with a clear
headline per slide stating the takeaway (not a topic label). Include
proof points/data where claims are made. End with a specific call to
action: {CTA}."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Slide-by-slide outline: headline + key content + visual note per slide
- Clear narrative progression across the deck
- Defined opening hook and closing CTA slide

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Does each slide headline state a conclusion/takeaway, or just a
   topic label (e.g. "Revenue Grew 40% Last Quarter" vs. "Revenue")?
2. Does the arc build logically, with each slide setting up the next,
   or could slides be reordered without losing coherence (a sign of weak
   structure)?
3. Is the deck paced appropriately for the time limit (roughly 1-2
   minutes per slide for spoken presentations)?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
If the deck feels like a list rather than a story:
- Rewrite every slide headline as a complete, conclusion-stating
  sentence rather than a noun-phrase label
- Check for a clear "turn" in the narrative (where the problem shifts to
  solution, or where-status-quo shifts to change) and ensure it's
  visually/structurally marked
- Cut slides that repeat information already established, tightening
  toward the time limit

REFLECTION LAYER
"If someone only saw the slide headlines in sequence, with no spoken
narration, would they follow a coherent argument?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Any data/statistics cited in the deck must be verified against a
reliable source before presenting, especially in investor/business
contexts where inaccuracy has real credibility consequences.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Every slide headline states a takeaway, not just a topic
[ ] One idea per slide, no overcrowding
[ ] Clear narrative arc with a discernible turn/progression
[ ] Data claims backed by cited proof points
[ ] Paced appropriately for stated time limit
[ ] Ends with specific, actionable CTA

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
- Write the closing CTA slide first, then work backward — knowing the
  destination clarifies what the arc needs to build toward.
- Limit text per slide to what can be read in under 6 seconds; anything
  denser should be spoken, not displayed.
- Use one strong visual/data point per slide rather than multiple
  competing charts or images.

COMMON MISTAKES
- Topic-label headlines instead of takeaway headlines
- Overcrowding slides with paragraphs of text
- No clear narrative arc — just sequential information dump
- Vague or missing call to action at the end

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
- Design for the "board meeting test" — could someone who missed the
  talk understand the argument just from reading the slides afterward?
- Rehearse against the stated time limit; decks are almost always
  over-scoped on the first draft.
- Keep a separate, denser "appendix" section for anticipated Q&A data
  rather than cramming it into the core narrative slides.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Create a 10-slide presentation to persuade a technical audience of
fellow developers, to be delivered in 8 minutes. Core message: 'Most
RAG systems fail not because of the LLM, but because of bad retrieval —
and fixing retrieval first is the highest-leverage improvement.' Use a
problem-solution narrative arc. Structure one idea per slide, with a
clear headline per slide stating the takeaway. Include proof points/data
where claims are made. End with a specific call to action: try the
speaker's open-source hybrid-retrieval benchmark repo.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[Outline:
1. "Everyone Blames the LLM. It's Usually the Retriever." (hook)
2. "What RAG Actually Promises vs. What It Delivers" (problem framing)
3. "Case Study: A Support Bot That 'Hallucinated' — But Didn't" (proof)
4. "The Real Failure: Retrieval Returned the Wrong Chunks" (diagnosis)
5. "Why Naive Vector Search Misses Obvious Answers" (technical root
   cause)
6. "Fix #1: Hybrid Search (Vector + Keyword) Recovers 30% of Misses"
   (data point)
7. "Fix #2: Re-Ranking Closes the Rest of the Gap" (data point)
8. "Before/After: Same LLM, Same Prompt, Different Retrieval" (proof)
9. "The Takeaway: Debug Retrieval Before You Touch the Prompt"
   (reinforced core message)
10. "Try It Yourself: [repo link], Benchmark Included" (CTA)]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add: audience-objection preemption slides (anticipating and addressing
the top 2-3 skeptical questions within the flow), speaker-notes
generation per slide (what to say, distinct from what's displayed), and
a fallback: "If a slide requires more than 3 supporting points, split
into two slides rather than compressing."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in: full rhetorical-device annotation (where the deck uses
contrast, repetition, or a rule-of-three for memorability), pacing/
energy-curve mapping across the talk (where to build intensity, where to
let the audience breathe), and appendix-slide generation for anticipated
deep-dive Q&A.

ENTERPRISE VERSION
Add: brand-template compliance (locked master slide layouts, approved
chart styles, required legal disclaimer slides), multi-stakeholder
review structure (executive summary version vs. full-detail version of
the same content), and localization notes for multi-region delivery.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"A [SLIDE_COUNT]-slide deck to [GOAL] for [AUDIENCE]. Core message:
'[CORE_MESSAGE]'. [ARC_TYPE] structure, one takeaway-headline per slide,
ending with CTA: [CTA]."
Works as a content/structure brief across Claude/GPT for outlining; feed
the resulting outline into Gamma, Tome, or Beautiful.ai for automated
visual slide generation.
