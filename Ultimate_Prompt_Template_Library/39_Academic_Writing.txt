================================================================================
39 — ACADEMIC WRITING
================================================================================

TITLE
Academic Writing Master Template

PURPOSE
Produce academically rigorous writing (essays, literature reviews,
theses sections) with proper argumentation structure, appropriate
academic register, and correct citation discipline — while using AI as a
structuring/drafting aid rather than a substitute for the student's own
understanding and original analysis.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Claude, GPT-4/5, Gemini

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Intermediate → Expert

WHEN TO USE
- Essay drafting/structuring, literature review organization, thesis
  chapter drafting, argument development, academic editing/feedback

EXPECTED OUTPUT
Well-structured academic prose with clear thesis, logical argument
progression, appropriate register, and a citation framework — always
used in accordance with the student's institution's academic integrity
policy.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ASSIGNMENT CONTEXT (question/prompt, discipline, level)
  ↓
THESIS/ARGUMENT (the specific claim being argued)
  ↓
STRUCTURE (introduction → body arguments → counterargument → conclusion)
  ↓
EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS (what sources/data support each point)
  ↓
ACADEMIC REGISTER (discipline-appropriate tone/conventions)
  ↓
CITATION STYLE (APA/MLA/Chicago/etc.)
  ↓
COUNTERARGUMENT HANDLING (acknowledging and addressing opposing views)
  ↓
INTEGRITY BOUNDARY (AI as structuring aid, not ghostwriter — student's
  own analysis and understanding must be central)

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Thesis/Argument specificity: academic writing is fundamentally
  argumentative — a vague or purely descriptive "thesis" produces weak
  papers regardless of how well-structured the prose is; this must be a
  specific, defensible, arguable claim.
- Counterargument Handling: strong academic arguments engage seriously
  with opposing positions rather than ignoring them — this section
  ensures the writing doesn't just assert one side but demonstrates
  critical engagement with the topic's actual complexity.
- Integrity Boundary: this is the most important section for this
  template category — AI assistance should help a student structure,
  clarify, and strengthen their OWN thinking and research, not generate
  submitted work wholesale in violation of academic integrity policies;
  this must be explicit and non-negotiable.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{ASSIGNMENT} {DISCIPLINE} {THESIS} {CITATION_STYLE} {SOURCES_AVAILABLE}
{WORD_COUNT}

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"I'm working on {ASSIGNMENT} for a {DISCIPLINE} course, target
{WORD_COUNT}. My thesis/argument: {THESIS}. Sources I'm drawing on:
{SOURCES_AVAILABLE}. Help me structure this into a strong academic
argument: introduction with clear thesis statement, body sections each
developing one supporting point with evidence, explicit engagement with
at least one counterargument, and a conclusion that synthesizes (not
just repeats) the argument. Use {CITATION_STYLE} citation format. This
should help me organize and strengthen MY OWN analysis and argument —
flag if any part of what I'm asking for would cross into work that
should be my own original writing rather than AI-generated."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Structured outline or draft with clear thesis, body logic, and
  counterargument engagement
- Citation format applied consistently
- Explicit notes distinguishing structural/organizational help from
  content the student should develop themselves

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Is the thesis a specific, arguable claim (something a reasonable
   person could disagree with), or is it a purely descriptive statement
   that doesn't actually require defending?
2. Does the argument structure build logically, with each section's
   evidence genuinely supporting the specific claim it's attached to?
3. Does this response help the student think more clearly and develop
   their own argument, or does it risk substituting for their own
   required academic work in a way that would violate integrity
   policies?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
If the thesis is too descriptive or the structure weak:
- Sharpen a descriptive statement into an arguable claim by asking "so
  what — why does this matter, and what would someone who disagrees
  say?"
- Check that each body section's evidence directly supports its
  specific claim, not just the general topic
- If a request risks crossing into "write my essay for me" territory,
  redirect toward structuring/feedback/clarification support instead

REFLECTION LAYER
"Does this response help the student produce stronger work through
their own understanding, or does it risk them submitting AI-generated
content as their own original academic work?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Any factual claims, statistics, or citations referenced must be
accurate; fabricated citations (a known AI failure mode) are especially
serious in academic contexts and must never be presented — if a specific
citation isn't verifiably known, this should be flagged rather than
invented.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Thesis is a specific, arguable claim, not merely descriptive
[ ] Each body section's evidence directly supports its specific point
[ ] At least one counterargument seriously engaged, not strawmanned
[ ] Citation format applied consistently and correctly
[ ] No fabricated citations or sources
[ ] Response supports the student's own thinking/authorship, doesn't
    substitute for required original work

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
- Bring your own source material and rough ideas to the conversation
  rather than asking for a thesis from scratch — the strongest use of
  AI here is strengthening and organizing your existing thinking, not
  generating it wholesale.
- Ask for feedback/critique on your own draft paragraphs specifically,
  which builds your own skill, rather than only requesting new AI-
  generated prose.
- Always independently verify any citation before including it in
  submitted work — AI models can produce plausible-sounding but
  incorrect or fabricated citations.

COMMON MISTAKES
- Purely descriptive "thesis" statements that don't require defending
- Ignoring counterarguments entirely, weakening the paper's rigor
- Inconsistent citation formatting
- Accepting AI-suggested citations without independently verifying they
  exist and say what's claimed
- Using AI output as final submitted text rather than as a drafting/
  structuring aid, risking academic integrity violations

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
- Check your institution's specific academic integrity policy on AI use
  before starting — policies vary significantly by institution and even
  by individual course/instructor.
- Use AI most effectively for the "cold start" problem (organizing
  scattered notes into a coherent outline) and for critique of your own
  drafts, both of which build your skills rather than replacing them.
- Independently verify every citation in the final version against the
  actual source before submission, without exception.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I'm working on a 2000-word essay for a Class XI economics course on
whether AI-driven automation will increase or decrease income inequality
in developing economies. Target 2000 words. My thesis/argument:
automation's effect on inequality depends more on a country's existing
labor-market flexibility and education infrastructure than on the
technology itself, so the same automation wave could increase inequality
in one country and decrease it in another. Sources I'm drawing on: two
assigned readings on automation and labor markets, plus I want to bring
in a recent example I read about. Help me structure this into a strong
academic argument. Use APA citation format. Flag if any part crosses
into work that should be my own.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[Structural guidance:

Your thesis is genuinely arguable — good starting point, since it
pushes back against the simpler "automation increases/decreases
inequality" framing with a conditional claim. Here's a structure to
develop it:

I. Introduction: frame the simple binary question (does automation
increase or decrease inequality), then introduce your more nuanced
conditional thesis as the paper's contribution.

II. Body 1 — Labor-market flexibility as a mediating factor: this is
where you'd draw on your first assigned reading. [Note: you'll need to
develop the specific mechanism/evidence from that reading yourself —
I can help you organize and clarify it once you've drafted it, but the
substantive analysis of your assigned reading should be your own work.]

III. Body 2 — Education infrastructure as a mediating factor: similarly
structured using your second reading.

IV. Counterargument: engage seriously with the view that automation's
effects are primarily driven by the technology's nature (e.g. skill-
biased technical change) regardless of local context — this is the
strongest objection to your conditional thesis, and addressing it will
strengthen your paper significantly.

V. Conclusion: synthesize — don't just restate — by returning to your
conditional claim with the mediating factors established.

On your "recent example" — bring me the specific facts once you've
found and read the source, and I can help you integrate it into this
structure with correct APA in-text citation, but I can't generate or
verify a specific real-world example/citation for you from a vague
memory, since that risks introducing an inaccurate or fabricated
source into your paper.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add explicit rubric-alignment checking (structuring the argument against
a provided grading rubric's specific criteria), peer-review-style
feedback simulation (critiquing a draft the way a peer reviewer or
professor might), and a fallback: "If a requested section would require
substantive original analysis the student hasn't yet done, ask
clarifying questions to help them develop that analysis themselves
rather than generating it on their behalf."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in full literature-synthesis methodology (organizing multiple
sources into a coherent review structure that identifies genuine
scholarly debates/gaps), discipline-specific argumentation convention
coaching (how argument structure differs between, say, humanities and
STEM academic writing), and iterative-draft-improvement tracking (before/
after comparison showing specifically what feedback improved).

ENTERPRISE VERSION
(Institutional/educator use) Add rubric-based automated feedback
generation for instructor use, plagiarism/originality-adjacent
integrity-check integration notes, and cohort-level common-misconception
pattern identification across multiple student submissions for
instructional improvement.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"Help me structure an academic argument for [ASSIGNMENT]. Thesis:
[THESIS]. Body sections with evidence, counterargument engagement,
[CITATION_STYLE] format. This should strengthen my own analysis, not
replace it — flag anything that crosses that line."
