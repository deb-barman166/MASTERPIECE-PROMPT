================================================================================
42 — STARTUP
================================================================================

TITLE
Startup Strategy Master Template

PURPOSE
Reason through startup-stage-specific decisions (MVP scope, positioning,
fundraising narrative) using the lens appropriate to the company's
actual stage, rather than generic business advice mismatched to
pre-product-market-fit reality.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Claude, GPT-4/5, Gemini

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Intermediate → Expert

WHEN TO USE
- MVP scoping, pre-PMF positioning, pitch narrative development,
  early hiring decisions, fundraising strategy, pivot evaluation

EXPECTED OUTPUT
Stage-appropriate startup strategy reasoning — advice calibrated to
whether the company is pre-product, pre-PMF, or scaling, not generic
"grow the business" advice.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
COMPANY STAGE (idea / MVP / pre-PMF / post-PMF / scaling) → CORE
PROBLEM/QUESTION → CONSTRAINTS (runway, team size) → RISK BEING TESTED
(the single riskiest assumption) → OPTIONS CONSIDERED → STAGE-
APPROPRIATE REASONING → RECOMMENDED NEXT STEP (small, testable)

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Company Stage: advice appropriate for a scaling Series B company
  (org structure, process) is actively harmful if applied to a 2-person
  pre-product team — stage must anchor every subsequent recommendation.
- Riskiest Assumption identification: startups fail most often from
  building the wrong thing well, not building the right thing poorly —
  identifying and prioritizing the single riskiest unvalidated
  assumption focuses effort on what actually matters at this stage.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{STAGE} {CORE_QUESTION} {CONSTRAINTS} {RISKIEST_ASSUMPTION}

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"My startup is at {STAGE} stage. Question: {CORE_QUESTION}. Constraints:
{CONSTRAINTS}. I believe the riskiest unvalidated assumption right now
is: {RISKIEST_ASSUMPTION} — confirm or challenge this. Reason through
options appropriate specifically to {STAGE} stage (not generic growth-
company advice). Recommend a small, fast, testable next step rather than
a large committed plan."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Stage-calibrated reasoning
- Confirmed/challenged riskiest assumption
- Small, specific, testable recommended next step

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Is this advice genuinely calibrated to the stated stage, or is it
   generic startup advice that would apply equally at any stage?
2. Is the identified "riskiest assumption" actually the thing most
   likely to kill the company if wrong, or a secondary concern?
3. Is the recommended next step small and fast enough to test cheaply,
   or does it commit significant resources before validating the
   assumption?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
Re-examine whether advice assumes resources/team size the company
doesn't have at this stage; check the "riskiest assumption" against the
classic pre-PMF question ("will anyone actually pay for/use this") if
that hasn't been validated yet; shrink any recommended next step that
looks like a multi-month commitment into a smaller test.

REFLECTION LAYER
"Would a stage-appropriate startup advisor who has seen many companies
at exactly this stage recognize this as sound, or as advice from a
later-stage playbook applied too early?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Any cited startup benchmarks (typical runway, typical time-to-PMF)
should be verified against current, credible sources rather than
outdated conventional wisdom.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Advice genuinely calibrated to stated company stage
[ ] Riskiest assumption correctly identified/challenged
[ ] Recommended next step is small, fast, and cheaply testable
[ ] Constraints (runway, team) respected in the recommendation
[ ] Avoids premature scaling advice for pre-PMF stage

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
Before any stage-specific advice, explicitly confirm what's actually
been validated so far (do customers exist, has anyone paid) since this
often reveals the stage is earlier than assumed. Bias every
recommendation toward the fastest, cheapest possible test of the
riskiest assumption over a comprehensive plan.

COMMON MISTAKES
Applying scaling-stage advice (org design, process) to pre-PMF
companies; treating a secondary risk as the primary one while the real
existential risk goes unaddressed; recommending large committed plans
before validating the core assumption cheaply first.

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
Revisit "what's our riskiest assumption right now" explicitly at every
major milestone — it changes as the company progresses, and yesterday's
answer may no longer be today's biggest risk. Resist the pull toward
comprehensive planning at early stages; speed of learning matters more
than plan completeness pre-PMF.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
My startup is at pre-PMF stage (have an MVP, ~40 free users, 0 paying).
Question: should we spend the next month building more features
users have requested, or focus on converting existing users to paid?
Constraints: 4 months runway, 2-person team. I believe the riskiest
unvalidated assumption right now is: whether anyone will actually pay
for this at all — confirm or challenge this.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[Your instinct is correct and this is exactly the right question to
prioritize: with 0 paying users despite 40 free users, "will anyone pay"
is unambiguously the riskiest unvalidated assumption — more features for
free users doesn't test this and actively burns runway you can't afford
to spend on the wrong risk. Recommended next step: before building
anything new, directly ask your most engaged free users to pay (even a
manual, unscalable "here's a Stripe link" ask to your top 5-10 most
active users this week) — this tests the core assumption in days, not a
month, and costs no build time. If several genuinely convert, that
validates willingness-to-pay and justifies investing the next month in
building requested features for paying customers specifically. If
none convert even among your most engaged users, that's critical
information suggesting either pricing, packaging, or the core value
proposition needs rethinking before any further feature investment —
information worth having now, with 4 months of runway left, rather
than discovering it after a month of building.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add explicit kill-criteria definition (what result from this test would
signal a pivot is needed) and a fallback: "If the riskiest assumption
can't be tested within 2 weeks given current constraints, find a
smaller/cheaper proxy test that approximates it."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in cohort-based PMF-signal analysis (retention curves, not just
raw conversion), and competitive-positioning stress-testing against the
riskiest assumption specifically.

ENTERPRISE VERSION
(For accelerators/incubators advising portfolio companies) Add
portfolio-pattern-matching notes (how similar-stage companies commonly
succeed/fail at this decision point) and structured advisor-session
prep documentation.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"Startup at [STAGE] stage. Question: [CORE_QUESTION]. Constraints:
[CONSTRAINTS]. Riskiest assumption: [RISKIEST_ASSUMPTION] — confirm/
challenge and recommend a small, fast test."
