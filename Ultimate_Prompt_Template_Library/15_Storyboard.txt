================================================================================
15 — STORYBOARD
================================================================================

TITLE
AI Storyboard Master Template

PURPOSE
Plan a video/film/animation sequence shot-by-shot before production,
ensuring visual continuity, pacing, and narrative clarity are solved on
paper (cheaply) rather than discovered expensively during filming/
generation.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Midjourney v6+ (frame visualization), Claude/GPT (shot-list reasoning),
Runway/Kling (turning storyboard frames into motion)

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Intermediate → Expert

WHEN TO USE
- Pre-production for video/film/animation, ad concept development,
  AI-video sequence planning, explainer video planning

EXPECTED OUTPUT
A shot-by-shot storyboard: numbered frames with visual description,
camera direction, dialogue/action notes, and duration estimate per shot.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STORY SUMMARY (what happens overall)
  ↓
SHOT LIST (numbered sequence of shots)
  ↓
PER-SHOT: VISUAL DESCRIPTION
  ↓
PER-SHOT: CAMERA (angle, movement, shot type)
  ↓
PER-SHOT: ACTION/DIALOGUE
  ↓
PER-SHOT: DURATION ESTIMATE
  ↓
TRANSITION LOGIC (cut / dissolve / match-cut between shots)
  ↓
CONTINUITY NOTES (wardrobe, prop, lighting consistency across shots)
  ↓
TOTAL RUNTIME TARGET

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Per-Shot breakdown: a storyboard's entire value is in decomposing a
  scene into individually plannable, filmable/generatable units — a
  vague overall description doesn't achieve this.
- Transition Logic: how shots connect is as important as what's within
  them; unplanned transitions create jarring or confusing sequences.
- Continuity Notes: this is where most amateur storyboards (and AI-
  generated video sequences) break down — a character's shirt color or
  the room's lighting shifting between shots destroys believability.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{STORY_SUMMARY} — the overall scene/sequence being boarded
{SHOT_COUNT} — target number of shots
{TOTAL_RUNTIME} — target total duration
{CHARACTERS} — who appears, with consistent appearance descriptions
{SETTING} — where this takes place
{TONE} — the emotional register of the sequence

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Storyboard this sequence: {STORY_SUMMARY}, set in {SETTING}, featuring
{CHARACTERS} (maintain consistent appearance description across all
shots), with a {TONE} tone. Target {SHOT_COUNT} shots totaling
{TOTAL_RUNTIME}. For each shot provide: visual description, camera
(angle/movement/shot type), action/dialogue, duration estimate, and
transition to next shot. Include a continuity note listing anything
that must remain visually consistent across all shots (wardrobe, key
props, lighting direction)."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Numbered shot list, each with the 5 per-shot elements above
- Continuity checklist covering the full sequence
- Total runtime tally

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Does each shot serve a specific narrative or visual purpose, or could
   it be cut without losing anything (tightening pacing)?
2. Do consecutive shots vary appropriately in framing (avoiding, for
   example, five consecutive medium shots which reads as visually flat)?
3. Is the continuity information (appearance, setting, lighting)
   specific and consistent enough to actually be reused verbatim across
   every shot's generation prompt?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
If the sequence feels visually monotonous or narratively unclear:
- Vary shot type/angle between consecutive shots for visual rhythm
  (e.g. wide → medium → close-up → wide, not all medium)
- Check each shot's purpose against the story summary; cut shots that
  don't advance action, emotion, or information
- Tighten continuity descriptions into copy-pasteable blocks to prevent
  drift when each shot is generated separately

REFLECTION LAYER
"If I only saw the storyboard frames in sequence with no other context,
would the story/message be clear?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Not typically applicable to fictional storyboarding; for documentary/
factual sequences, verify any depicted facts/events for accuracy before
finalizing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Every shot serves a clear narrative/visual purpose
[ ] Shot type/angle varies for visual rhythm
[ ] Character/setting appearance descriptions are consistent and reusable
[ ] Transitions between shots are intentional, not default cuts only
[ ] Duration estimates sum to the target runtime
[ ] Continuity notes are specific enough to prevent drift across shots

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
- Write one master character/setting appearance description block and
  literally copy-paste it into every relevant shot's visual description
  to guarantee consistency when generating with AI video tools.
- Storyboard in beats, not just shots — identify where the emotional or
  informational "turn" happens and ensure the shot list serves it.
- Keep individual shot durations realistic for current AI video model
  capability (4-10 second clips) if this storyboard will be generated,
  not filmed live-action.

COMMON MISTAKES
- Inconsistent character appearance descriptions across shots, causing
  visual drift when each is generated independently
- All shots using the same framing (no visual rhythm)
- Storyboarding shots that don't serve the story (padding)
- Ignoring transitions, leaving connective tissue between shots
  unplanned

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
- For AI-video-generation pipelines specifically, maintain a single
  "continuity bible" text block (character appearance, environment,
  lighting) referenced in every individual shot prompt.
- Storyboard the emotional arc first (where does tension rise/resolve)
  before locking specific camera choices.
- Review the boarded sequence read purely as thumbnails/frames — if the
  story doesn't track without dialogue, the shot selection needs work.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Storyboard this sequence: a solo developer discovers their AI agent has
been running successfully in production overnight, set in a small home
office at dawn, featuring one character (mid-20s developer, tousled
hair, wearing a gray hoodie, sitting at a desk with two monitors), with a
quietly triumphant tone. Target 5 shots totaling 20 seconds. For each
shot provide: visual description, camera, action/dialogue, duration
estimate, and transition to next shot. Include a continuity note listing
anything that must remain visually consistent across all shots.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[Shot list:
1. Wide shot, static camera, dim room lit only by monitor glow. Developer
   asleep at desk, head on arms. (3s) → Cut to
2. Close-up, static, on a monitor showing a dashboard: "247 successful
   runs, 0 errors" ticking upward. (3s) → Cut to
3. Medium shot, slow push-in. Developer stirs, lifts head, blinking at
   the screen. (4s) → Cut to
4. Close-up on developer's face — slow dawning smile as they realize
   what they're seeing. (4s) → Cut to
5. Wide shot, static, warm dawn light now entering through a window
   behind them as they lean back, satisfied. (6s) → End.
Continuity note: gray hoodie, tousled dark hair, and the two-monitor
desk setup must remain identical across all 5 shots; monitor glow
transitions naturally to warm dawn light only in shot 5.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add: sound-design cue notes per shot (what audio/music beat aligns with
each visual beat), match-cut opportunity identification (visual elements
that could connect consecutive shots more elegantly than a hard cut),
and a fallback: "If a shot's described action is too complex for a
single AI-video generation pass, split it into two simpler consecutive
shots."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in: full animatic-style pacing notes (exact frame-to-frame timing
suitable for a rough edit), alternate-take shot options (a secondary
framing choice per key shot for editorial flexibility), and coverage
planning (which shots need multiple angles for edit-room flexibility).

ENTERPRISE VERSION
Add: production-budget implications per shot (complexity/cost tier),
client-approval checkpoint structure (which storyboard frames require
sign-off before proceeding to production), and brand/legal compliance
notes for any depicted products, locations, or trademarked elements.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"Storyboard [STORY_SUMMARY] in [SHOT_COUNT] shots, [TOTAL_RUNTIME] total.
Each shot: visual description, camera, action, duration, transition.
Maintain consistent [CHARACTERS] appearance across all shots."
This is a pure reasoning/planning template — the resulting shot list
feeds directly into the Video Generator or Cinematic Video templates for
per-shot AI generation.
