================================================================================
60 — CAREER PLANNING
================================================================================

TITLE
Career Planning Master Template

PURPOSE
Develop a career plan grounded in an honest assessment of current
skills/gaps relative to a specific target role, with a concrete,
sequenced action plan — rather than generic career advice disconnected
from the person's actual starting point and specific goal.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Claude, GPT-4/5, Gemini

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Beginner → Advanced

WHEN TO USE
- Career path planning, skill-gap analysis, career transition planning,
  long-term learning roadmap design, evaluating career options

EXPECTED OUTPUT
A career plan with a specific target role, an honest gap analysis
between current and required skills, and a concrete, time-sequenced
action plan to close that gap.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TARGET ROLE (specific) → CURRENT STATE (skills, experience, honestly
assessed) → GAP ANALYSIS (what's actually required vs. what's currently
present) → PRIORITIZATION (which gaps matter most for this specific
role) → SEQUENCED ACTION PLAN (what to do first, second, third) →
MILESTONES (checkpoints to assess progress) → REALISTIC TIMEFRAME

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Specific Target Role: generic career advice ("build your skills,
  network more") is nearly useless without a specific target to plan
  toward — the role must be specific enough to identify concrete actual
  requirements.
- Honest Gap Analysis: a plan built on an inflated self-assessment of
  current skills will misallocate effort toward the wrong things —
  genuine honesty about current gaps (not falsely modest, not falsely
  confident) is what makes the resulting plan actually useful.
- Prioritization: not every gap matters equally for a specific role —
  identifying which 2-3 gaps are actually most consequential prevents
  spreading limited time/effort too thin across everything at once.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{TARGET_ROLE} {CURRENT_SKILLS} {CURRENT_EXPERIENCE} {TIME_AVAILABLE}
{TIMEFRAME_GOAL}

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Help me plan toward this target role: {TARGET_ROLE}. My current skills/
experience, honestly: {CURRENT_SKILLS}, {CURRENT_EXPERIENCE}. Time I can
realistically dedicate: {TIME_AVAILABLE}. Goal timeframe: {TIMEFRAME_
GOAL}. Identify what this role actually requires, compare honestly
against my current state, and prioritize the 2-3 gaps that matter most.
Build a sequenced action plan (what to focus on first, second, third)
with milestones to check progress along the way."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Specific role requirements identified
- Honest gap analysis
- Prioritized top gaps
- Sequenced action plan with milestones
- Realistic timeframe assessment

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Is the target role specific enough to identify concrete, actual
   requirements, or is it too vague ("something in AI") to plan against
   meaningfully?
2. Is the gap analysis genuinely honest (not inflating current skills to
   make the gap look smaller, not being falsely modest about real
   existing strengths), grounded in what the stated current experience
   actually demonstrates?
3. Are the prioritized gaps genuinely the ones that matter most for THIS
   specific role, or a generic "improve everything" list that doesn't
   help focus limited time?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
If the target role is too vague, ask for it to be narrowed to something
specific enough to research actual requirements against; recalibrate the
gap analysis if it seems to over- or under-state current skill level
relative to what's actually described; narrow an overly broad
prioritization list down to the genuinely highest-leverage 2-3 items.

REFLECTION LAYER
"If someone followed this exact action plan for the stated timeframe,
would they genuinely be a credible candidate for this specific target
role by the end, or does the plan not actually close the gaps that
matter most?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Any claims about what a specific role/industry actually requires should
be checked against current, real job postings/industry information
rather than assumed from general/possibly outdated knowledge, since
skill requirements for specific roles evolve.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Target role specific enough to identify concrete requirements
[ ] Gap analysis is genuinely honest, not inflated or falsely modest
[ ] Top gaps prioritized (2-3), not an unfocused long list
[ ] Action plan is sequenced (what first, second, third), not a flat
    list
[ ] Milestones included to check progress along the way
[ ] Timeframe is realistic given stated available time

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
Research actual current job postings for the target role (or roles one
level below it, which are more attainable near-term) to ground the gap
analysis in real, current requirements rather than assumption. Focus
the action plan on building demonstrable evidence (projects, portfolio
pieces) of the prioritized gap-closing skills, not just passive
learning, since evidence is what actually matters to evaluators.
Revisit and adjust the plan at each milestone rather than treating it as
fixed once created.

COMMON MISTAKES
Planning toward a target role too vague to identify real requirements;
inflated self-assessment leading to a plan that doesn't address real
gaps; an unfocused list trying to improve everything at once instead of
prioritizing; passive-learning-only plans with no demonstrable evidence-
building; unrealistic timeframes given actually available time.

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
Revisit the plan every few months against real progress and any new
information (e.g. actual interview feedback, evolving role requirements)
rather than following an initial plan rigidly regardless of new
evidence. Prioritize building a small number of genuinely strong,
demonstrable projects/proof-points over broad but shallow exposure to
many things — depth in the areas that matter most for the target role
tends to matter more than breadth.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Help me plan toward this target role: AI/ML engineering internship at a
mid-size tech company. My current skills, honestly: strong self-taught
Python, have built several personal projects (RAG systems, multi-agent
orchestration, a deep learning framework), but no formal internship/work
experience, no published/peer-reviewed work, still in Class XI (high
school). Time I can realistically dedicate: several hours most days
after school. Goal timeframe: applying for internships in the next
12-18 months.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[Role requirements (based on typical current AI/ML internship
postings): most postings at this level look for demonstrated practical
project experience, foundational ML/DL understanding (not necessarily
formal credentials at internship level), some evidence of collaborative/
version-controlled development practice, and increasingly, evidence of
genuine understanding (not just AI-assisted output) through ability to
explain design decisions.

HONEST GAP ANALYSIS: your existing project portfolio (RAG systems,
multi-agent orchestration) is a genuine, unusually strong asset for this
stage — many internship candidates have far less hands-on build
experience. Real gaps: (1) no public, well-documented portfolio that a
recruiter/interviewer could actually review (project work may currently
be scattered/undocumented) — this is likely your highest-leverage gap to
close, since it's the most direct evidence a recruiter sees; (2) no
demonstrated collaborative development practice (open-source
contribution, working with others' code via PRs/reviews) — internship
roles specifically often value this since interns join existing teams;
(3) formal age/timeline eligibility for internships needs early
research, since some programs have specific eligibility windows relative
to graduation timing.

PRIORITIZED TOP GAPS: (1) public portfolio documentation, (2)
collaborative/open-source experience, (3) early research into specific
target programs' actual eligibility requirements.

SEQUENCED ACTION PLAN:
Months 1-3: consolidate and properly document 2-3 of your strongest
existing projects into a genuinely portfolio-ready state (clean READMEs,
clear explanation of design decisions and tradeoffs, not just code
dumps) — this directly addresses gap #1 using work you've largely
already done, making it high-leverage relative to effort.
Months 2-4 (overlapping): make small, genuine contributions to 1-2
existing open-source AI/ML-adjacent projects — start small
(documentation fixes, small bug fixes) to build real collaborative
experience and a visible contribution history, addressing gap #2.
Months 3-6: research specific target companies'/programs' actual
internship eligibility and application timelines directly (gap #3),
adjusting the plan's later timeline to match real application windows
rather than an assumed generic schedule.
Months 6-12: apply, using the now-documented portfolio and
contribution history; use any interview experience gained (even
unsuccessful applications) as a feedback milestone to refine the plan
further.

MILESTONE CHECK: by month 3, you should have at least one fully
portfolio-ready project live and reviewable — if not, that's a signal to
reassess whether the documentation approach needs simplifying rather
than continuing to add more projects at lower quality.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add specific target-company research methodology (how to identify which
2-3 companies/programs are the best realistic fit given the stated
profile), networking/informational-interview strategy layered into the
timeline, and a fallback: "If a milestone isn't met on schedule,
diagnose whether the issue is time-availability, skill-gap
underestimation, or plan-focus before simply extending the timeline
uniformly."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in personal-brand/visibility strategy (technical writing, talks,
or community engagement that builds recognition alongside the direct
skill-building), competitive-positioning analysis (how this specific
profile compares to a typical competitive applicant pool for these
specific target roles), and interview-pipeline preparation integrated
into the later-stage timeline.

ENTERPRISE VERSION
(For career centers/bootcamps advising many students) Add cohort-level
outcome tracking across similar career-plan profiles, employer-
partnership-informed requirement calibration (direct current
information from hiring partners rather than general research), and
structured mentor/advisor-check-in scheduling aligned to the plan's
milestones.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"Plan toward [TARGET_ROLE]. Current state: [CURRENT_SKILLS],
[CURRENT_EXPERIENCE]. Time available: [TIME_AVAILABLE]. Goal timeframe:
[TIMEFRAME_GOAL]. Honest gap analysis, prioritize top gaps, sequenced
action plan with milestones."
