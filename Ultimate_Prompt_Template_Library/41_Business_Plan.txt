================================================================================
41 — BUSINESS PLAN
================================================================================

TITLE
Business Plan Development Master Template

PURPOSE
Develop a business plan that stress-tests assumptions and quantifies
unit economics realistically, rather than producing an optimistic
narrative document that reads well but wouldn't survive investor or
lender scrutiny.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Claude, GPT-4/5, Gemini

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Intermediate → Expert

WHEN TO USE
- New business planning, pivot evaluation, lender/investor plan
  preparation, internal strategic planning, business model validation

EXPECTED OUTPUT
A structured business plan with realistic (not just optimistic) unit
economics, explicit assumption-testing, and honest risk assessment
alongside the opportunity case.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BUSINESS CONCEPT → TARGET MARKET (specific, sized) → VALUE PROPOSITION
→ REVENUE MODEL → UNIT ECONOMICS (CAC, LTV, margins, realistic
assumptions) → COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE → GO-TO-MARKET STRATEGY → RISK
ASSESSMENT (honest, not just a token section) → FINANCIAL PROJECTIONS
(with stated assumptions, not just output numbers)

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Unit Economics with realistic assumptions: the most common business
  plan failure is optimistic, unstated assumptions baked into
  projections (unrealistically low CAC, unrealistically high retention)
  — this section forces every key number to have an explicit,
  challengeable assumption behind it.
- Risk Assessment as substantive, not token: plans that only list risks
  superficially ("competition" as a one-word risk) fail to demonstrate
  the founder has actually thought through failure modes — this must be
  specific and genuinely considered.
- Financial Projections with stated assumptions: projections presented
  as bare numbers invite (correctly) skepticism; showing the assumption
  behind each number allows the reader to evaluate whether they agree
  with the underlying logic, which is far more persuasive than the
  numbers alone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{BUSINESS_CONCEPT} {TARGET_MARKET} {REVENUE_MODEL} {KEY_ASSUMPTIONS}
{TIME_HORIZON} {PLAN_PURPOSE} (internal / investor / lender)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Help me develop a business plan for {BUSINESS_CONCEPT}, targeting
{TARGET_MARKET}. Purpose: {PLAN_PURPOSE}. Revenue model: {REVENUE_MODEL}.
Build out unit economics with explicit, realistic assumptions (not
optimistic defaults) for: customer acquisition cost, lifetime value,
gross margin, payback period. State each assumption explicitly so it can
be challenged/validated. Include competitive landscape analysis. Provide
an honest risk assessment — specific risks, not generic categories, with
realistic mitigation (or honest acknowledgment where mitigation is
weak). Financial projections over {TIME_HORIZON} with every key driver's
assumption stated alongside the output number."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Structured plan sections with explicit assumptions throughout
- Unit economics table with assumption-to-output traceability
- Substantive risk section with specific, non-generic risks

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Is each key financial assumption (CAC, conversion rate, retention)
   realistic for this specific business/industry, or is it an
   optimistic default that wouldn't survive scrutiny from someone who
   knows the space?
2. Are the identified risks specific and genuinely considered (with
   real, even if imperfect, mitigation thinking), or are they generic
   placeholders ("market risk," "competition")?
3. Does the target market sizing use a credible, traceable methodology
   (bottom-up from realistic assumptions) rather than an inflated top-
   down "if we just capture 1% of a huge market" calculation?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
If projections or assumptions look unrealistic:
- Cross-check any CAC/conversion/retention assumption against known
  industry benchmarks for a similar business type and flag significant
  deviations for justification
- Replace any "if we capture X% of the total market" sizing with a
  bottom-up calculation from realistic reach/conversion assumptions
- Expand any generic one-line risk into a specific scenario with an
  honest assessment of how well (or poorly) it's currently mitigated

REFLECTION LAYER
"If a skeptical, experienced investor or lender in this specific
industry reviewed these assumptions, would they find them credible, or
would they immediately identify unrealistic optimism?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Any cited market size, industry benchmark, or competitive data should be
verified against current, reliable sources rather than estimated from
general knowledge, since these figures materially affect the plan's
credibility and decision-usefulness.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Every key financial assumption stated explicitly and realistically
[ ] Market sizing uses credible bottom-up methodology
[ ] Unit economics assumptions checked against realistic benchmarks
[ ] Risk section is specific and substantive, not generic
[ ] Financial projections traceable to stated assumptions
[ ] Competitive landscape reflects actual current competitors

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
- Build unit economics from the most conservative realistic assumption
  first, then show a sensitivity range (what happens if CAC is 20%
  higher, if retention is 10% lower) rather than presenting a single
  optimistic point estimate.
- For market sizing, always show the bottom-up calculation (realistic
  reach × realistic conversion × realistic price) alongside any top-down
  reference figure, since bottom-up is far more credible to sophisticated
  readers.
- Have the plan explicitly address "why now" and "why us" — timing and
  team advantage are often more persuasive than market size alone.

COMMON MISTAKES
- Optimistic default assumptions presented without acknowledgment
- Top-down market sizing ("just 1% of a $50B market") without bottom-up
  validation
- Generic, superficial risk sections that don't demonstrate real
  founder awareness
- Financial projections presented as bare numbers with no visible
  assumption trail
- Outdated or unverified competitive landscape information

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
- Pressure-test your own assumptions by asking "what would have to be
  true for this number to be wrong by 50%?" for every key driver before
  finalizing.
- Show the plan to someone knowledgeable in the specific industry (not
  just business-general) before finalizing — industry-specific
  unrealistic assumptions are often invisible to generalists.
- For investor/lender contexts specifically, prepare to defend every
  assumption verbally — a plan's credibility often rests more on how
  well the founder can defend the numbers in conversation than on the
  document itself.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Help me develop a business plan for a subscription-based AI prompt
template marketplace for developers and creators, targeting indie
developers and content creators who use multiple AI tools. Purpose:
internal strategic planning, not investor-facing yet. Revenue model:
monthly subscription ($15/mo) for access to a curated, updated template
library. Build out unit economics with realistic assumptions. Include
competitive landscape. Provide honest risk assessment. Financial
projections over 18 months.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[Plan excerpt:

UNIT ECONOMICS (stated assumptions):
- CAC assumption: $35/customer, based on realistic content-marketing +
  small paid-ad-spend blended cost for a developer-tool-adjacent
  audience (not the optimistic $10-15 sometimes assumed for organic-
  only growth — flagging that achieving sub-$20 CAC would require
  strong organic/community traction not yet proven)
- Monthly churn assumption: 8%/month initially (realistic for an early-
  stage subscription product without strong retention hooks yet, not
  the 2-3% assumed by mature SaaS benchmarks — this is a key risk area,
  addressed below)
- LTV (at 8% monthly churn, $15/mo): ~$187 per customer
- LTV:CAC ratio: ~5.3:1 — healthy on paper, but highly sensitive to the
  churn assumption; at 12% monthly churn (plausible for an unproven
  early product) LTV drops to ~$125, ratio to ~3.6:1 — still viable but
  meaningfully worse

COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE: [current competitor analysis with recency-
verified positioning]

RISK ASSESSMENT (specific, not generic):
1. Content commoditization risk: prompt templates are inherently easy
   to screenshot/copy and redistribute for free once purchased —
   mitigation is weak (some products in this space rely on continuous
   new-content velocity + community/tooling lock-in rather than content
   scarcity alone; this needs a real product-differentiation answer
   beyond "better prompts")
2. Retention risk: without a strong reason to keep the subscription
   beyond initial template download, churn could exceed even the
   already-conservative 8-12% assumption above — mitigation requires
   genuinely recurring value (new templates, tooling, community) not
   yet built
3. Platform dependency risk: value proposition depends on continued
   relevance of current AI tools/platforms; a major shift in how people
   prompt (e.g. more capable default AI behavior needing less template
   engineering) could reduce the core value proposition over the
   18-month horizon

FINANCIAL PROJECTION (18mo, assumption-traceable): [monthly projection
table with each row's growth/churn/CAC inputs shown alongside the
output revenue figure]]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add scenario planning (base/optimistic/pessimistic cases with explicitly
different assumption sets, not just one point estimate), cohort-based
retention modeling instead of a single blended churn rate, and a
fallback: "If a specific industry benchmark isn't confidently known,
state the uncertainty explicitly and provide a reasoned range rather
than a false-precision single number."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in full sensitivity analysis (tornado chart of which assumptions
most affect the bottom line), break-even analysis with explicit
timeline, and cap-table/funding-requirement modeling if the plan
involves raising capital.

ENTERPRISE VERSION
Add formal financial-statement projections (P&L, cash flow, balance
sheet) suitable for lender underwriting, market-research methodology
documentation for investor due diligence, and governance/organizational
structure planning for a scaling team.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"Develop a business plan for [BUSINESS_CONCEPT] targeting
[TARGET_MARKET]. Realistic unit economics with stated assumptions.
Bottom-up market sizing. Specific, substantive risk assessment.
Assumption-traceable financial projections over [TIME_HORIZON]."
