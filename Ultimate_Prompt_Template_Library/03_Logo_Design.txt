================================================================================
03 — LOGO DESIGN
================================================================================

TITLE
AI Logo Design Master Template

PURPOSE
Generate distinctive, scalable, brand-appropriate logo concepts that work
across mediums (favicon to billboard) and communicate brand identity
without relying on trendy effects that date quickly.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Midjourney v6+, Ideogram v2 (best for text-in-logo), DALL-E 3, Adobe
Firefly, Recraft

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Intermediate → Expert

WHEN TO USE
- New brand identity, rebrand, sub-brand/product mark, icon-only mark,
  wordmark, combination mark, emblem

EXPECTED OUTPUT
A logo concept (or 4 concept variations) that is legible at small sizes,
reproducible in single-color, and distinct from direct competitors.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BRAND CONTEXT
  ↓
LOGO TYPE (wordmark / lettermark / pictorial / abstract / combination / emblem)
  ↓
CORE CONCEPT / METAPHOR
  ↓
STYLE DIRECTION (minimalist, geometric, hand-drawn, luxury, playful...)
  ↓
COLOR STRATEGY (primary, with single-color fallback)
  ↓
TYPOGRAPHY DIRECTION (if wordmark/combination)
  ↓
CONSTRAINTS (must scale to 16px favicon, must work in black/white)
  ↓
COMPETITOR DIFFERENTIATION
  ↓
OUTPUT FORMAT (vector-style flat design on white background)

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Core Concept/Metaphor: logos without a conceptual anchor look like
  generic clip-art; the metaphor is what makes a mark memorable and
  defensible in a design rationale.
- Constraints (scalability): the majority of AI-generated "logos" fail in
  practice because they're actually detailed illustrations that break down
  at small sizes — this section forces simplicity.
- Competitor Differentiation: prevents the extremely common failure of
  generating a mark that looks like every other AI-generated logo in the
  same industry (e.g. generic circuit-brain icons for every AI startup).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{BRAND_NAME} — the company/product name
{INDUSTRY} — sector (fintech, food, SaaS, fashion, etc.)
{LOGO_TYPE} — wordmark / lettermark / pictorial / abstract / combination
{CORE_METAPHOR} — the concept the mark should visually represent
{STYLE} — minimalist / geometric / organic / luxury / playful / brutalist
{PRIMARY_COLOR} — main brand color (with hex if known)
{PERSONALITY_TRAITS} — 3 adjectives describing brand personality
{COMPETITORS_TO_AVOID} — visual cliches to steer away from

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Design a {LOGO_TYPE} logo for {BRAND_NAME}, a {INDUSTRY} brand with
personality traits: {PERSONALITY_TRAITS}. Core concept/metaphor:
{CORE_METAPHOR}. Style: {STYLE}. Primary color: {PRIMARY_COLOR}, must also
work as pure black on white. Must remain legible at 16px favicon size.
Avoid visual cliches common to {COMPETITORS_TO_AVOID}. Flat vector style,
centered on white background, no mockup/3D rendering."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- 4 distinct concept directions (not 4 minor variations of one idea)
- Each shown in primary color + single-color black
- Each shown at small-size simulation if possible

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Does the core metaphor connect to what the brand actually does, or is
   it a generic industry symbol (avoid: lightbulb=ideas, circuit=tech)?
2. Will this mark still look distinctive in 5 years, or does it rely on a
   currently-trendy gradient/effect?
3. Can this be described in one sentence to a sign-painter and reproduced
   accurately? If not, it's too complex.

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
If outputs look generic or cliche:
- Push the CORE_METAPHOR toward something more specific to the brand's
  actual differentiator, not just its industry category
- Explicitly instruct "avoid gradient effects, avoid 3D bevel, avoid
  generic geometric shape combos seen in stock logo packs"
- If typography-heavy, specify a less common typeface category
  (e.g. "high-contrast serif" instead of default sans-serif)

REFLECTION LAYER
"If I saw this logo on a business card with no company name attached,
would I have any sense of what this company does or feels like?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Cross-check the resulting concept isn't accidentally similar to an
existing well-known trademark in the same or adjacent industry.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Legible/recognizable at 16px
[ ] Works in single color (pure black)
[ ] Conceptually tied to brand, not generic industry symbol
[ ] No reliance on gradient/3D effects that will look dated
[ ] Distinct from 3 named competitors
[ ] Typography (if any) is deliberate, not default

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
- Generate lettermark and pictorial versions separately, then consider a
  combination mark only after both individually succeed.
- Request "flat vector illustration style" explicitly — AI models default
  to photorealistic/3D rendering unless told otherwise.
- Ask for the mark on a plain white background with no mockup context
  (no business card, no wall sign) for a clean base asset.

COMMON MISTAKES
- Requesting overly detailed marks that don't scale down
- Using industry-default metaphors (gears for engineering, leaves for
  eco-brands) that make the brand indistinguishable from competitors
- Skipping the single-color test, which is standard in real trademark/
  print use
- Over-specifying color gradients that don't reproduce in embroidery,
  engraving, or single-color print contexts

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
- Always design the icon/mark independent of the wordmark first — this
  gives you a flexible favicon/app-icon asset regardless of final lockup.
- Build a one-page rationale connecting each design choice back to a
  brand attribute; this is what separates a designed mark from a
  generated image in client presentations.
- Test the mark at actual physical sizes (mock a 1-inch pin, a favicon
  tab, a billboard) before finalizing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Design an abstract logo for "Northline," a logistics/freight tech brand
with personality traits: reliable, precise, forward-moving. Core concept:
the idea of a direct, unbroken route — visualized as a single continuous
line that subtly forms an arrow or forward motion without being a literal
arrow. Style: minimalist, geometric. Primary color: deep navy (#12253C),
must also work as pure black on white. Must remain legible at 16px
favicon size. Avoid visual cliches common to generic "logistics" logos
(literal trucks, literal globes, literal wings). Flat vector style,
centered on white background, no mockup/3D rendering.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[4 concepts: (1) A single unbroken line forming a subtle forward-leaning
chevron through negative space, (2) An "N" monogram constructed from one
continuous stroke suggesting motion, (3) A geometric mark of two
overlapping angled bars implying directional precision, (4) A minimalist
route-line icon that reads as an abstract "N" from a distance. Each shown
in navy and in pure black, each legible as a simple silhouette at small
size.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add: explicit grid/geometry construction logic (e.g. "built on a circular
grid with consistent stroke width"), secondary color palette for
sub-brand applications, and a fallback instruction: "If the abstract mark
reads as unrelated to the brand without context, shift toward a
lettermark using the same geometric language."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in: full brand system implications (how the mark extends into a
pattern/texture system), motion-logo considerations (does the mark imply
a natural animation, e.g. the line "drawing itself"), and multi-context
simulation (app icon with rounded-square mask, favicon, letterhead,
merchandise embroidery) each with format-specific simplification notes.

ENTERPRISE VERSION
Add: full trademark-clearance-conscious differentiation brief, co-brand
lockup rules (how the mark pairs with partner logos), international
legibility check (does the mark carry unintended meaning in other
cultures/markets), and a formal design rationale document structure
suitable for board/stakeholder presentation.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"A [STYLE] [LOGO_TYPE] logo for [BRAND_NAME], representing [CORE_METAPHOR].
Flat vector, [PRIMARY_COLOR] and black versions, centered on white
background, simple enough to read at favicon size."
Works across Midjourney, Ideogram, and DALL-E; Ideogram currently produces
the most reliable in-logo typography, so route wordmark-heavy requests
there specifically.
