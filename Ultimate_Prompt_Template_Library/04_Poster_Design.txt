================================================================================
04 — POSTER DESIGN
================================================================================

TITLE
AI Poster Design Master Template

PURPOSE
Generate posters (event, movie-style, promotional, educational) that
work at both close-viewing and across-the-room distances, with a clear
information hierarchy from headline down to fine print.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Midjourney v6+, Ideogram v2 (text-heavy posters), DALL-E 3, Adobe
Firefly

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Intermediate

WHEN TO USE
- Event posters, movie/album-style key art, educational/classroom
  posters, promotional campaign posters, print advertising

EXPECTED OUTPUT
A poster at standard print/digital dimensions with a clear three-tier
hierarchy (headline visible from distance, key info at mid-range, detail
at close range).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
POSTER PURPOSE (event / promotional / educational / artistic)
  ↓
VIEWING CONTEXT (across-room / eye-level close / both)
  ↓
HEADLINE (readable from distance)
  ↓
HERO VISUAL (dominant image/illustration)
  ↓
KEY INFO (date/location/details — mid-range legible)
  ↓
FINE PRINT (close-range only: credits, terms, sponsors)
  ↓
STYLE DIRECTION
  ↓
COLOR/CONTRAST STRATEGY
  ↓
DIMENSIONS (print size or digital resolution)

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Viewing Context: a poster designed only for close-up screen viewing
  often fails when actually printed and viewed from 10+ feet away — this
  section forces the design to work at its intended real-world distance.
- Three-Tier Hierarchy: posters are read in passes (glance from far,
  approach, then read details) — collapsing all information into one
  visual weight defeats this natural reading behavior.
- Hero Visual: unlike banners (peripheral attention) or infographics
  (data-first), posters are fundamentally image-led — the visual must
  carry emotional/thematic weight, not just support text.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{PURPOSE} — event / promotional / educational / artistic
{HEADLINE} — the large, distance-readable text
{HERO_VISUAL} — the dominant image concept
{KEY_INFO} — date, location, or core details
{FINE_PRINT} — credits, sponsors, terms
{STYLE} — visual direction/reference
{DIMENSIONS} — e.g. 18x24in, 24x36in, A2, digital 1080x1350

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Design a {PURPOSE} poster at {DIMENSIONS}. Headline (large, readable
from across a room): '{HEADLINE}'. Hero visual: {HERO_VISUAL} as the
dominant compositional element. Key info (mid-range legible):
{KEY_INFO}. Fine print (small, close-range only): {FINE_PRINT}. Style:
{STYLE}. Ensure clear three-tier visual hierarchy: headline visible from
distance, key info readable at normal viewing range, fine print
legible up close."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Poster with hero visual dominant, headline large and high-contrast,
  key info clearly secondary, fine print present but unobtrusive

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Would the headline and hero visual alone communicate the poster's
   purpose from 10+ feet away?
2. Does the hero visual carry the emotional/thematic tone of the event/
   subject, or is it generic stock-style imagery?
3. Is there clear visual separation between the three information tiers
   (size, weight, or placement distinguishing headline from key info
   from fine print)?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
If the poster feels flat or the hierarchy unclear:
- Increase the size differential between headline and key info (often
  the gap is too small)
- Strengthen the hero visual's compositional dominance (it should
  occupy significant visual real estate, not compete equally with text)
- Move any information competing with the headline for top visual
  weight down into the key-info tier

REFLECTION LAYER
"Standing back from this poster, does the headline and image alone tell
me what this is about, even before I read anything else?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Verify date, location, and any factual claims in the key info/fine
print are accurate, since posters are often printed in bulk and
difficult/costly to correct after production.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Headline legible from realistic viewing distance
[ ] Hero visual is compositionally dominant and thematically relevant
[ ] Clear three-tier hierarchy (headline > key info > fine print)
[ ] Key info (date/location/etc.) accurate and complete
[ ] Style matches the tone/genre of the subject
[ ] Works at intended print or digital dimensions

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
- Design and test the headline at actual/simulated viewing distance
  before finalizing — zoom out on screen to approximate real distance.
- Let the hero visual bleed to the edges where appropriate for a more
  dynamic, less "boxed-in" composition.
- Reserve pure white/negative space deliberately — cluttered posters
  read as amateur regardless of content quality.

COMMON MISTAKES
- Headline too small or low-contrast to read from a realistic distance
- Hero visual treated as decoration rather than the primary
  communicative element
- Flat hierarchy where all text is similarly sized/weighted
- Designing only for digital screen viewing when the poster will be
  printed and viewed at true scale

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
- Reference genre conventions deliberately (film posters, concert
  posters, and academic posters all have distinct, recognizable visual
  languages) — leaning into or intentionally subverting these is a
  stronger choice than ignoring them.
- For print posters, always confirm color mode (CMYK) and bleed
  requirements with the printer before finalizing the design file.
- Create a digital-crop variant (e.g. Instagram square) alongside the
  print version for promotional reuse.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Design an event poster at 18x24in. Headline (large, readable from
across a room): 'BUILD WEEKEND 2026'. Hero visual: an abstract
illustration of interconnected nodes/circuits forming a rising arrow
shape, as the dominant compositional element. Key info (mid-range
legible): 'Aug 14-16 · Siliguri Tech Hub · Free for Students'. Fine
print (small, close-range only): 'Organized by [Org] · Sponsored by
[Sponsors] · Register at [URL]'. Style: bold geometric, high-energy
tech-conference aesthetic, dark background with electric accent colors.
Ensure clear three-tier visual hierarchy.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[Image: An 18x24in vertical poster with a dark navy background. The
abstract circuit/arrow illustration dominates the upper two-thirds in
glowing cyan and magenta. "BUILD WEEKEND 2026" spans in massive bold
white type across the middle, clearly readable from a distance. Below
it, medium-sized text reads "Aug 14-16 · Siliguri Tech Hub · Free for
Students". A small footer strip at the bottom carries the fine print in
a compact, legible but visually de-emphasized style.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add: multi-format variant generation (print + Instagram story + square
social crop from one master composition), typographic system
specification (exact type pairing for headline/key-info/fine-print
tiers), and a fallback: "If the hero visual competes visually with the
headline for dominance, either enlarge the headline's contrast treatment
(outline, background panel) or simplify the visual's detail level."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in: genre-convention deconstruction (identifying and deliberately
using or subverting specific visual tropes of the poster's category),
print-production specification (CMYK color notes, bleed/trim marks,
paper stock recommendation), and series/campaign consistency rules for
posters that are part of a multi-piece set.

ENTERPRISE VERSION
Add: brand and sponsor compliance requirements (logo sizing/placement
rules, required legal/safety text), multi-market localization
(language variants maintaining the same visual system), and full
print-vendor specification handoff (file format, resolution, color
profile requirements).

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"A [PURPOSE] poster at [DIMENSIONS]: headline '[HEADLINE]' large and
distance-readable, hero visual of [HERO_VISUAL], key info
'[KEY_INFO]' mid-tier, fine print '[FINE_PRINT]' small. Style: [STYLE]."
Works across Midjourney and DALL-E for the visual concept; route to
Ideogram specifically for reliable large-headline text rendering, then
finish exact typography in a dedicated design tool for print production.
