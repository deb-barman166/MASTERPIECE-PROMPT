================================================================================
21 — YOUTUBE SCRIPT
================================================================================

TITLE
YouTube Video Script Master Template

PURPOSE
Write long-form YouTube scripts engineered for retention (the platform's
core ranking signal), with a hook that survives the first 15 seconds and
pacing that sustains watch-time through the full video.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Claude, GPT-4/5, Gemini

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Intermediate

WHEN TO USE
- Long-form YouTube videos (8+ minutes), tutorials, essays, deep-dives,
  reviews, vlogs with scripted structure

EXPECTED OUTPUT
A full script with timestamped sections, a retention-engineered hook,
open loops sustaining curiosity, and a clear CTA — written for spoken
delivery, not silent reading.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VIDEO TOPIC + CORE PROMISE
  ↓
TARGET AUDIENCE
  ↓
HOOK (0-15 seconds — the retention-critical opening)
  ↓
OPEN LOOP (curiosity gap sustained across the video)
  ↓
SECTION STRUCTURE (numbered content blocks with sub-hooks)
  ↓
PACING NOTES (where energy rises/falls, where to re-hook)
  ↓
B-ROLL/VISUAL CUES (what to show while narrating)
  ↓
CTA PLACEMENT (mid-roll + end)
  ↓
TARGET LENGTH

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Hook (0-15s): YouTube's retention curve drops most steeply in the
  first 15 seconds; a script without a specifically engineered hook
  loses the majority of potential viewers before the content even
  begins.
- Open Loop: sustained curiosity (a question raised early, answered
  late) is the primary mechanism keeping viewers through a long video —
  without it, viewers leave once their immediate question is answered.
- Section Sub-hooks: long videos need re-hooking at each section
  transition, or retention decays steadily; each section should open
  with its own mini-curiosity-gap.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{TOPIC} — the video's subject
{CORE_PROMISE} — what the viewer will get by watching
{AUDIENCE} — who this is for + their existing knowledge level
{TARGET_LENGTH} — target video duration
{TONE} — energetic / calm-educational / conversational / authoritative
{CTA} — the specific action requested (subscribe, comment, link click)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Write a {TARGET_LENGTH} YouTube script about {TOPIC} for {AUDIENCE}.
Core promise: '{CORE_PROMISE}'. Open with a hook (first 15 seconds) that
creates a specific curiosity gap or states a surprising claim — no
generic 'hey guys welcome back' opening. Establish an open loop
sustained until late in the video. Structure content into numbered
sections, each opening with its own mini-hook. Include pacing notes
(energy shifts, re-hook points) and suggested visual/B-roll cues per
section. Place a natural mid-roll CTA and a clear end-screen CTA:
'{CTA}'. Tone: {TONE}. Written for spoken delivery."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- [HOOK] section (timestamped 0:00-0:15)
- Numbered content sections with sub-hooks and visual cues
- [MID-ROLL CTA] and [END CTA] clearly marked
- Total estimated runtime

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Does the hook create genuine curiosity/stakes within the first
   sentence, or does it delay value with throat-clearing preamble?
2. Is there a clear open loop that gives viewers a reason to stay
   through less immediately-exciting middle sections?
3. Is the script written the way a person actually talks (contractions,
   natural rhythm, short sentences) rather than reading like a formal
   essay?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
If retention would likely drop:
- Cut any preamble/intro before the hook — start with the most
  compelling sentence, not channel branding or "in this video"
- Add a re-hook question or teaser at the start of any section that
  risks feeling like a lull
- Read the script aloud mentally; replace any sentence that sounds
  written rather than spoken

REFLECTION LAYER
"If I only watched the first 15 seconds, would I be curious enough to
keep watching? If I skipped to the middle, is there still a reason to
stay?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Any factual claims, statistics, or quotes included in the script must
be verified against reliable sources before recording, since spoken
misinformation in video form spreads and is harder to correct than text.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Hook lands within the first sentence, no throat-clearing preamble
[ ] Clear open loop sustained across the video
[ ] Each section has its own sub-hook/re-engagement point
[ ] Written in natural spoken rhythm, not formal written prose
[ ] Visual/B-roll cues included per section
[ ] CTA is specific and appropriately placed (not just at the very end)
[ ] Factual claims verified

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
- Write the hook last, after knowing exactly what the video delivers —
  the best hooks reference the most compelling specific payoff.
- Vary sentence length deliberately; a string of same-length sentences
  reads flat when spoken aloud.
- Front-load the core promise's value; don't make viewers wait until
  minute 5 for the actual useful content.

COMMON MISTAKES
- Generic channel-intro preamble before the hook ("hey guys, welcome
  back to my channel, today we're going to...")
- No open loop, causing viewers to leave once their initial question is
  answered
- Written in essay-style prose that sounds unnatural when spoken
  aloud
- CTA only at the very end, missing the mid-roll retention window

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
- Study your own or competitor retention graphs (YouTube Analytics) to
  identify exactly where past videos lost viewers, and specifically
  address those patterns in new scripts.
- Record a rough read-through before final scripting to catch anything
  that's awkward to actually say aloud.
- Keep a swipe file of hooks that worked well (yours or others') to
  study the underlying mechanism, not to copy verbatim.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Write a 12-minute YouTube script about why most self-taught Python
developers plateau at intermediate level for developers who already
know the basics and want to break through. Core promise: 'the specific,
unglamorous habit that separates intermediate from advanced developers.'
Establish an open loop sustained until late in the video. Structure
content into numbered sections, each opening with its own mini-hook.
Include pacing notes and suggested visual/B-roll cues per section. Place
a natural mid-roll CTA and a clear end-screen CTA: 'subscribe for more
deep technical breakdowns.' Tone: conversational, direct, no fluff.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[HOOK 0:00-0:15]: "There's one habit that separates developers who
plateau at 'I can build things' from developers who get hired to
architect systems — and almost nobody talks about it because it's not
a cool framework or a new language. It's reading other people's bad
code on purpose." [B-roll: split screen of clean vs. messy codebase]

[Section 1 — "Why Tutorials Create a Ceiling" 0:15-2:30]: sub-hook:
"Every tutorial you've ever followed has been quietly lying to you
about what real code looks like." ... [continues with full section
content, visual cues noted per beat]

[Section 2 — "The Habit Itself" 2:30-6:00]: sub-hook: "Here's exactly
what this looks like in practice, with a real example." ...

[MID-ROLL CTA ~6:00]: natural CTA woven into a transition line, not an
abrupt ad-style interruption.

[Sections 3-4 continue...] 

[END CTA 11:30-12:00]: reinforces core promise delivered, then: "If this
kind of unglamorous, real-talk breakdown is useful, subscribe — I do
this every week."]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add: pattern-interrupt placement notes (specific points to insert a
joke, visual gag, or tone shift to reset viewer attention), thumbnail/
title-to-hook alignment check (ensuring the hook delivers on exactly
what the thumbnail/title promised), and a fallback: "If a section runs
long without a natural sub-hook, insert a rhetorical question or
teaser referencing an upcoming section to bridge the gap."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in: full retention-curve prediction annotations (where in the
script attention is statistically most likely to drop, based on known
retention patterns for this content type), A/B hook variant generation
(2-3 distinct hook approaches for the same content), and SEO-optimized
title/description generation aligned with the script's actual keyword-
relevant content.

ENTERPRISE VERSION
Add: brand-voice consistency requirements across a channel's full script
library, legal/compliance review notes for any claims made (especially
in finance, health, or technical-advice content), and multi-language
localization script structure maintaining hook/pacing integrity across
translations.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"Write a [TARGET_LENGTH] YouTube script on [TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE].
Hook in first 15 seconds, open loop sustained throughout, numbered
sections with sub-hooks, natural spoken tone, CTA: [CTA]."
This is a pure text-reasoning template — works identically across
Claude, GPT, and Gemini.
