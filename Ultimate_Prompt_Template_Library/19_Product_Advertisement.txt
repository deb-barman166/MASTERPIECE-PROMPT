================================================================================
19 — PRODUCT ADVERTISEMENT
================================================================================

TITLE
AI Product Advertisement Master Template

PURPOSE
Generate product ad visuals/videos that highlight genuine product
benefits through composition and context, rather than generic "product
floating on white background" imagery that fails to communicate value.

IDEAL AI MODELS
Midjourney v6+ (product photography style), Runway/Kling (product video),
DALL-E 3, Adobe Firefly

DIFFICULTY LEVEL
Intermediate → Expert

WHEN TO USE
- E-commerce product photography style ads, social/display product ads,
  product launch video concepts, before/after demonstration content

EXPECTED OUTPUT
A product-focused visual or short video that communicates a specific
benefit/use-case through context and composition, with the product as
clear hero.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRODUCT + KEY BENEFIT
  ↓
TARGET CUSTOMER (who this benefit matters to)
  ↓
CONTEXT/USE-CASE SCENE (where/how the product is used)
  ↓
COMPOSITION (product hero placement, scale, angle)
  ↓
LIGHTING (matches product category — clean/bright vs. moody/premium)
  ↓
SUPPORTING ELEMENTS (props that reinforce the use-case, not clutter)
  ↓
EMOTIONAL TONE
  ↓
PLATFORM/FORMAT SPEC

WHY EACH SECTION EXISTS
- Key Benefit anchoring: ads that show a product without a clear benefit
  message rely entirely on aesthetic appeal, which rarely converts —
  every compositional choice should reinforce what the product does for
  the customer.
- Context/Use-Case Scene: products shown in realistic use-context
  (rather than isolated on white) let customers project themselves into
  the experience, which is a stronger persuasion mechanism than pure
  product photography.
- Lighting matched to category: a premium skincare product and a rugged
  outdoor tool require entirely different lighting languages (soft/
  glowing vs. hard/dramatic) — defaulting to generic "product lighting"
  misses category-appropriate signaling.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{PRODUCT} — the product being advertised
{KEY_BENEFIT} — the single most important customer benefit
{TARGET_CUSTOMER} — who this is for
{USE_CASE_SCENE} — realistic context of use
{LIGHTING_STYLE} — clean/bright vs. moody/premium vs. dramatic
{TONE} — the emotional register (aspirational, trustworthy, exciting)
{PLATFORM} — where this ad will run (affects format/dimensions)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INPUT STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Create a product ad visual for {PRODUCT}, highlighting the benefit:
'{KEY_BENEFIT}' for {TARGET_CUSTOMER}. Show the product in use within
{USE_CASE_SCENE}, composed with the product as clear hero (appropriate
scale/prominence, not lost in the scene). Lighting: {LIGHTING_STYLE}.
Supporting elements should reinforce the use-case without cluttering the
frame. Tone: {TONE}. Format for {PLATFORM}."

OUTPUT STRUCTURE
- Product-hero visual/video with clear use-case context
- Benefit communicated through composition, not just implied by presence

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REASONING LAYER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Does this image communicate the specific benefit, or would a viewer
   just see "a product" without understanding why it matters to them?
2. Does {TARGET_CUSTOMER} see themselves reflected in this scene (would
   they recognize this as relevant to their life/context)?
3. Is the product clearly the hero of the composition, appropriately
   scaled and lit, not visually competing with or lost in supporting
   elements?

SELF-CORRECTION LAYER
If the ad feels generic or benefit-unclear:
- Make the use-case scene more specific and concrete (a vague "someone
  using the product" is weaker than a precisely imagined moment)
- Increase the product's compositional prominence if it's getting lost
  in the scene
- Tie supporting props/elements more directly to the stated benefit
  rather than generic lifestyle staging

REFLECTION LAYER
"If {TARGET_CUSTOMER} saw this for half a second while scrolling, would
they understand what this product does for them specifically?"

FACT VERIFICATION
Any implied claims (results, comparisons, "instantly," "clinically
proven," etc.) must be substantiated and compliant with advertising
standards in the relevant jurisdiction before use in real campaigns.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Key benefit is visually communicated, not just implied
[ ] Product is clear compositional hero
[ ] Use-case scene is specific and relatable to target customer
[ ] Lighting style matches product category expectations
[ ] Supporting elements reinforce rather than clutter
[ ] Format matches intended platform dimensions

OPTIMIZATION TIPS
- Choose one benefit per ad — trying to communicate multiple benefits in
  one visual dilutes all of them.
- Use environmental storytelling (props, setting) to imply the benefit
  rather than relying on text overlay alone.
- For e-commerce specifically, always also generate a clean, isolated
  product-only version alongside the lifestyle version — both serve
  different funnel stages.

COMMON MISTAKES
- Generic "product on a table" staging with no clear use-case or benefit
- Overcrowded lifestyle scenes where the product gets lost
- Lighting mismatched to product category (harsh lighting on a luxury
  item, or overly soft lighting on a rugged/technical product)
- Making unsubstantiated benefit claims

PROFESSIONAL TIPS
- Study category-leading competitor ads not to copy but to understand
  the visual conventions customers already associate with quality in
  that category.
- Build a family of ads around the same product each highlighting a
  different specific benefit, rather than one ad trying to say
  everything.
- Pair AI-generated concept visuals with real product photography for
  final production — AI is excellent for concept/testing, but real
  product accuracy matters for actual e-commerce listings.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE INPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Create a product ad visual for a compact mechanical keyboard, highlighting
the benefit: 'quiet enough for late-night coding without waking anyone up'
for remote developers who share living space with others. Show the
product in use within a dim, cozy home-office scene at night, warm desk-
lamp light, with a faint hallway visible in the background suggesting a
sleeping household. Composed with the keyboard as clear hero, mid-close
framing, hands typing visible. Lighting: warm, moody, low-key. Supporting
elements: a steaming mug, soft desk-lamp glow, should reinforce the
late-night quiet-work use-case without cluttering the frame. Tone:
calm, considerate, cozy. Format for Instagram square 1080x1080.

EXAMPLE OUTPUT
[Image: A warmly lit, low-key nighttime home-office scene at 1080x1080.
A pair of hands types on the compact keyboard, sharply in focus and
clearly the hero of the frame, lit by a single warm desk lamp. A steaming
mug sits softly out of focus nearby. In the deep background, a dim
hallway suggests the rest of the household is asleep, reinforcing the
"quiet enough not to wake anyone" benefit purely through visual
storytelling, with no text needed to convey the message.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add: multi-benefit ad family generation (3 distinct scenes each
highlighting a different product benefit, maintaining consistent product
appearance/branding across all), before/after or problem/solution
compositional structure, and a fallback: "If the use-case scene
overshadows the product, tighten the crop/framing to restore product
prominence while keeping just enough context to convey the benefit."

EXPERT VERSION
Layer in: funnel-stage-specific ad variants (top-of-funnel emotional/
lifestyle version vs. bottom-of-funnel feature/spec-focused version),
platform-specific creative-format requirements (Meta vs. Google vs.
Amazon each have different image/text ratio and content rules), and
performance-psychology annotations (why this specific benefit-
visualization approach tends to drive conversion in this product
category).

ENTERPRISE VERSION
Add: full regulatory-compliance review notes for the product category
(health/wellness, financial, children's products all have specific
advertising restrictions), brand-guideline adherence across a product
line's entire ad family, and localization/market-adaptation requirements
for global campaigns.

UNIVERSAL VERSION (model-agnostic core)
"A product ad for [PRODUCT] showing [KEY_BENEFIT] for [TARGET_CUSTOMER],
in [USE_CASE_SCENE], product as hero, [LIGHTING_STYLE] lighting,
[TONE] tone."
Works across Midjourney and DALL-E for still imagery; for video product
ads specifically, feed this same structure into the Video Generator
template with an added camera/motion layer.
