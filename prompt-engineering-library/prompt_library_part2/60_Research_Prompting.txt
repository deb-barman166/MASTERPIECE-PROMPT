-----------------------------
1. TITLE
-----------------------------
Research Prompting — Conducting Thorough, Multi-Source Investigation

-----------------------------
2. DEFINITION
-----------------------------
Research prompting is a technique for instructing an LLM to conduct a 
THOROUGH, MULTI-FACETED investigation into a topic or question — 
typically involving multiple searches/sources (via Search-Augmented 
Prompting or provided documents via RAG), systematic coverage of 
different angles or sub-questions, and synthesis into a comprehensive, 
well-organized answer. This differs from a simple factual question in 
scope and depth: Research Prompting is for genuinely open-ended, multi-
part, or nuanced topics where a single quick lookup wouldn't produce a 
sufficiently complete picture.

Think of it like the difference between asking a librarian "what year 
was X founded" (a quick factual lookup) versus commissioning an actual 
research report on a topic — requiring the librarian to consult 
multiple sources, cross-reference conflicting information, organize 
findings by sub-topic, and synthesize everything into a coherent, 
comprehensive overview rather than a single fact.

-----------------------------
3. PURPOSE
-----------------------------
Research prompting exists to:
- Provide COMPREHENSIVE coverage of complex, multi-faceted topics that 
  a single quick answer couldn't adequately address.
- Systematically explore DIFFERENT ANGLES or sub-questions within a 
  broader topic, ensuring balanced and thorough coverage rather than an 
  accidentally narrow focus.
- Synthesize information across MULTIPLE SOURCES, identifying 
  agreement, disagreement, and gaps in available information.
- Support informed DECISION-MAKING on complex questions by providing 
  the depth of understanding needed, rather than a superficial summary.
- Combine naturally with Search-Augmented Prompting (for current 
  information) and RAG Prompting (for provided document corpora) as the 
  underlying information-gathering mechanisms.

-----------------------------
4. WHEN TO USE AND BEST MODEL OR LLM
-----------------------------
WHEN TO USE:
- Open-ended questions requiring investigation across multiple sources 
  or sub-topics (market research, technology comparisons, background 
  investigation before a decision).
- Topics with genuine complexity or nuance that a single search/answer 
  wouldn't adequately capture.
- Preparing for significant decisions (career choices, technical 
  architecture decisions, purchasing decisions) that benefit from 
  thorough investigation.
- Academic or professional contexts requiring demonstrated, well-
  sourced understanding of a topic.

WHEN NOT TO USE:
- Simple, single-fact questions with a quick, direct answer (use 
  standard Instruction Prompting or Search-Augmented Prompting for a 
  single search instead).
- Topics you already have sufficient understanding of, where additional 
  research would be redundant effort.
- Time-sensitive situations where a "good enough" quick answer is more 
  valuable than comprehensive depth.

BEST MODELS FOR RESEARCH PROMPTING:
- Claude (with web search and extended thinking, or dedicated Research/
  deep research features) — excellent for thorough, well-organized 
  multi-source research with good synthesis and honest handling of 
  conflicting information.
- GPT-4o/4.1/5-class (with browsing/Deep Research features) — strong 
  research capability, particularly with dedicated deep-research 
  product features designed for exactly this extended investigation use 
  case.
- Gemini Pro/Ultra (with Search grounding and Deep Research features) — 
  strong research capability, well-integrated with Google's search 
  infrastructure.
- Perplexity — purpose-built specifically around thorough, cited, 
  multi-source research as its core product function.
- For VERY extensive research (30+ searches/sources), dedicated 
  "Deep Research" style product features (available across most major 
  providers) are specifically designed for this use case and generally 
  outperform manually orchestrating dozens of individual search calls 
  yourself.

-----------------------------
5. PROMPT FORMULA
-----------------------------
    [RESEARCH QUESTION/TOPIC] + [SPECIFIC SUB-QUESTIONS/ANGLES TO 
    COVER] + "Research this thoroughly across multiple sources, note 
    any conflicting information, and synthesize a comprehensive, well-
    organized answer with citations."

-----------------------------
6. PROMPT STRUCTURE
-----------------------------
    1. Research Question/Topic — the overall subject requiring 
       investigation.
    2. Sub-Questions/Angles — specific facets that need to be covered 
       for a complete picture (breaking a broad topic into concrete 
       components).
    3. Source Requirements — how many/what kind of sources should 
       inform the research (recency requirements, source type 
       preferences).
    4. Synthesis Instruction — explicit request to organize findings 
       coherently, not just list raw facts.
    5. Conflict Handling — instruction to note when sources disagree 
       rather than silently picking one.
    6. Citation Requirement — proper attribution per Citation Prompting 
       (file 37) principles.

Example skeleton:
    "Research [TOPIC] thoroughly. 
    
    Cover these specific angles: 
    - [SUB-QUESTION 1] 
    - [SUB-QUESTION 2] 
    - [SUB-QUESTION 3] 
    
    Use multiple sources, prioritizing [RECENCY/AUTHORITY 
    REQUIREMENTS]. Note any conflicting information found across 
    sources. Synthesize your findings into a well-organized, 
    comprehensive answer with citations."

-----------------------------
7. BEST PRACTICES
-----------------------------
- Break the broad TOPIC into SPECIFIC SUB-QUESTIONS upfront — this 
  ensures systematic, balanced coverage rather than the research 
  accidentally focusing heavily on whatever angle happens to dominate 
  initial search results.
- Specify SOURCE QUALITY/RECENCY requirements explicitly when relevant 
  (e.g., "prioritize sources from the last 12 months" for fast-
  changing topics, or "prioritize peer-reviewed sources" for scientific 
  topics).
- Explicitly request that CONFLICTING information be noted rather than 
  silently resolved — genuine research often surfaces disagreement, and 
  papering over this reduces the value/honesty of the research.
- Request SYNTHESIS, not just a list of found facts — the value of 
  research prompting comes from organizing and connecting information 
  into genuine understanding, not just aggregating search snippets.
- For genuinely extensive research needs (many distinct sub-questions, 
  broad scope), consider using a dedicated "Deep Research" product 
  feature rather than manually requesting dozens of searches in a 
  standard conversation.
- Request CITATIONS throughout, following Citation Prompting (file 37) 
  principles, so findings remain verifiable.
- For decision-support research, explicitly ask for the research to 
  conclude with a clear SUMMARY of key takeaways relevant to the 
  decision at hand, not just raw information.

-----------------------------
8. COMMON MISTAKES
-----------------------------
- MISTAKE: Providing only a broad topic with no specific sub-questions, 
  resulting in research that covers whatever angle happens to be most 
  prominent in initial results rather than systematic coverage.
  FIX: Break the topic into specific sub-questions/angles to cover.

- MISTAKE: Not specifying source recency/quality requirements for 
  topics where this matters significantly.
  FIX: Explicitly state recency or source-type requirements when 
  relevant.

- MISTAKE: Silently resolving conflicting information found across 
  sources rather than transparently noting the disagreement.
  FIX: Explicitly request that conflicts be flagged, not silently 
  resolved.

- MISTAKE: Receiving a list of raw facts without genuine synthesis/
  organization into coherent understanding.
  FIX: Explicitly request synthesis, not just information gathering.

- MISTAKE: Manually attempting dozens of individual search requests in 
  a standard conversation when a dedicated deep-research feature would 
  be more effective for very extensive research needs.
  FIX: Use dedicated Research/Deep Research product features for large-
  scale research tasks.

-----------------------------
9. EXAMPLES (BASIC, INTERMEDIATE, ADVANCED)
-----------------------------

--- BASIC EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"Research the current landscape of open-source vector database options 
for RAG applications (ChromaDB, FAISS, Weaviate, Qdrant, Milvus). 

Cover: 
- Key differentiating features of each 
- Typical use case fit for each 
- Any notable recent (last 6-12 months) developments 

Use multiple sources, note the publication date of information where 
recency matters, and synthesize a comparative overview with 
citations."

Expected Output:
A well-organized comparative overview covering all 5 named vector 
databases across the requested dimensions, with citations for specific 
claims and appropriate flagging of recency for any time-sensitive 
information (e.g., recent version releases or benchmark results).

--- INTERMEDIATE EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"Research the trade-offs between different approaches to building AI 
agent memory systems for long-running multi-agent applications. 

Cover these specific angles: 
- Vector-database-based semantic memory approaches 
- Graph-based memory/knowledge representation approaches 
- Hybrid approaches combining multiple memory types 
- Known challenges/limitations reported by practitioners for each 
approach 
- Any notable open-source frameworks or reference implementations 

Use multiple sources including technical blogs, documentation, and 
research discussions. Note any significant disagreements or trade-off 
debates you find across sources. Synthesize into a well-organized 
comparison, concluding with a brief summary of what factors would 
determine which approach fits different use cases."

Expected Output:
A comprehensive, well-organized research synthesis covering all 5 
specified angles, citing multiple sources, honestly noting where 
practitioner opinions/approaches diverge (e.g., debates about vector-
only vs. hybrid approaches), referencing relevant open-source 
frameworks, and concluding with practical decision-relevant guidance 
about which approach might suit different use case profiles.

--- ADVANCED EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"I'm deciding on a long-term technical direction for a personal AI 
project portfolio, considering whether to specialize deeply in agentic 
multi-agent systems versus building broader full-stack AI product 
skills. Research the current job market and skill-demand landscape to 
inform this decision. 

Cover these specific angles: 
- Current demand signals for specialized 'AI agent engineering' roles 
vs. broader 'AI/ML engineer' or 'full-stack AI developer' roles 
- How major AI companies and AI-focused startups are structuring 
relevant engineering teams/roles currently 
- Any notable recent (last 6 months) shifts in what skills are being 
prioritized in AI hiring, given how fast this field moves 
- Perspectives from practitioners/industry voices on which specialization 
path offers better long-term career resilience, acknowledging this is 
a genuinely debated question without a single settled answer 

Use multiple current sources. Explicitly note where you find genuinely 
conflicting expert opinions rather than presenting one view as 
settled consensus, since this appears to be a contested question. 
Synthesize into a well-organized overview, concluding with the key 
factors that would help someone weigh this decision for their own 
specific situation, rather than a one-size-fits-all recommendation."

Expected Output:
A thorough, current, multi-source research synthesis covering all 4 
angles, appropriately treating the specialization-vs-breadth question 
as genuinely contested among practitioners (explicitly presenting 
different expert viewpoints rather than false consensus), grounded in 
recent hiring/market signals rather than outdated general assumptions, 
and concluding with a decision framework (e.g., factors like current 
skill gaps, personal interest sustainability, local vs. remote job 
market considerations) rather than a single prescriptive answer — 
appropriately calibrated to present balanced, evenhanded treatment of 
a genuinely debated career question rather than the model's own 
singular opinion.

-----------------------------
10. REUSABLE PROMPT TEMPLATE
-----------------------------
    Research [YOUR TOPIC/QUESTION] thoroughly.
    
    Cover these specific angles:
    - [SUB-QUESTION/ANGLE 1]
    - [SUB-QUESTION/ANGLE 2]
    - [SUB-QUESTION/ANGLE 3]
    
    Source requirements: [RECENCY, AUTHORITY, OR SOURCE-TYPE 
    PREFERENCES]
    
    Note any conflicting information or genuinely contested viewpoints 
    found across sources, rather than presenting one view as settled 
    consensus if it isn't.
    
    Synthesize your findings into a well-organized, comprehensive 
    answer with citations, concluding with [KEY TAKEAWAYS / DECISION-
    RELEVANT SUMMARY, if applicable].
