-----------------------------
1. TITLE
-----------------------------
Image Prompting — Crafting Effective Prompts for AI Image Generation

-----------------------------
2. DEFINITION
-----------------------------
Image prompting is the technique of writing text prompts specifically 
optimized for TEXT-TO-IMAGE generation models (Midjourney, DALL-E, 
Stable Diffusion, Google Imagen, Adobe Firefly) — a distinct discipline 
from prompting text-generating LLMs, since image models respond to 
different structural conventions: specific visual/stylistic vocabulary, 
subject-medium-style-lighting-composition layering, aspect ratio and 
technical parameters, and often benefit from an entirely different 
"grammar" than natural conversational instruction. This directly 
supports AI-assisted creative and photo/video editing workflows.

Think of it like the difference between describing a photo to a person 
who will paint it from your description (conversational, contextual 
detail matters) versus giving a photographer extremely specific 
technical direction: subject, lens choice, lighting setup, composition, 
color grade, and reference style — image generation models respond 
much more reliably to this dense, keyword-and-phrase-driven technical 
specification than to flowing conversational prose.

-----------------------------
3. PURPOSE
-----------------------------
Image prompting exists to:
- Reliably communicate VISUAL INTENT to image generation models, which 
  parse prompts fundamentally differently than conversational LLMs.
- Control specific visual attributes: subject, style/medium, 
  composition, lighting, color palette, mood, and technical camera-like 
  parameters (lens, depth of field, angle).
- Achieve CONSISTENT results across multiple generations by using 
  precise, reproducible descriptive vocabulary rather than vague, 
  interpretable language.
- Support iterative refinement — understanding which specific prompt 
  elements to adjust to change specific visual outcomes (changing 
  "lighting: soft golden hour" to fix an overly harsh result, for 
  example).
- Bridge creative vision with the specific technical vocabulary that 
  different image models respond best to, since different tools 
  (Midjourney vs. Stable Diffusion vs. DALL-E) have somewhat different 
  optimal prompting conventions.

-----------------------------
4. WHEN TO USE AND BEST MODEL OR LLM
-----------------------------
WHEN TO USE:
- Generating any AI image — illustrations, concept art, photo-style 
  images, product mockups, character designs.
- Photo editing/enhancement workflows where AI generation supplements 
  or extends existing images.
- Building visual assets for creative projects, presentations, 
  storyboards, or design mockups.
- Iterating on a visual concept where precise control over specific 
  attributes (lighting, style, composition) matters.

WHEN NOT TO USE:
- When you need EXACT, literal reproduction of a real, specific, 
  identifiable place, object, or (especially) real person — image 
  models are for creative generation, not precise photographic 
  documentation, and generating images of real identifiable people 
  raises its own ethical/policy considerations.
- Extremely simple, single-word image needs where minimal prompting 
  is genuinely sufficient.
- Tasks requiring text generation, not image generation (obviously 
  use standard LLM prompting instead).

BEST MODELS/TOOLS FOR IMAGE PROMPTING:
- Midjourney — known for strong aesthetic/artistic output, responds 
  well to concise, evocative keyword-and-phrase prompts with specific 
  parameter flags (--ar for aspect ratio, --style, --stylize).
- DALL-E 3 (via ChatGPT/API) — benefits from more natural-language, 
  descriptive prompting compared to Midjourney's keyword-dense style, 
  and can leverage an LLM to help EXPAND a simple idea into a detailed 
  prompt automatically.
- Stable Diffusion (and variants like SDXL) — highly configurable, 
  often benefits from detailed positive AND negative prompts (what to 
  avoid), with strong community-developed prompting conventions.
- Google Imagen / Adobe Firefly — capable of both keyword-style and 
  natural-language prompting depending on the specific interface; 
  Firefly is particularly integrated with photo-editing-adjacent 
  workflows.
- General LLMs (Claude, GPT, Gemini) are often used AS a prompt-
  writing assistant — describing a visual idea in plain language and 
  asking the LLM to help expand/refine it into an optimized image-
  generation prompt for a specific target tool.

-----------------------------
5. PROMPT FORMULA
-----------------------------
    [SUBJECT] + [ACTION/POSE] + [SETTING/BACKGROUND] + [STYLE/MEDIUM] 
    + [LIGHTING] + [COLOR PALETTE] + [COMPOSITION/CAMERA ANGLE] + 
    [MOOD] + [TECHNICAL PARAMETERS]

-----------------------------
6. PROMPT STRUCTURE
-----------------------------
    1. Subject — the main focal point (a person, object, creature, 
       scene).
    2. Action/Pose/State — what the subject is doing or how it's 
       positioned.
    3. Setting/Environment — the background/location context.
    4. Style/Medium — photorealistic, oil painting, 3D render, anime, 
       watercolor, cyberpunk digital art, etc.
    5. Lighting — golden hour, dramatic side-lighting, soft diffused 
       light, neon-lit.
    6. Color Palette — dominant colors or overall color mood.
    7. Composition/Angle — close-up, wide shot, bird's-eye view, rule 
       of thirds.
    8. Mood/Atmosphere — the emotional tone the image should evoke.
    9. Technical Parameters (tool-specific) — aspect ratio, quality 
       settings, negative prompts (what to avoid).

Example skeleton:
    "[SUBJECT] [ACTION], [SETTING], [STYLE/MEDIUM], [LIGHTING], 
    [COLOR PALETTE], [COMPOSITION], [MOOD] --ar [ASPECT RATIO] 
    [OTHER TOOL-SPECIFIC PARAMETERS]"

-----------------------------
7. BEST PRACTICES
-----------------------------
- LAYER your descriptive elements in a logical order (subject → 
  setting → style → lighting → mood → technical params) — most image 
  models weight earlier terms somewhat more heavily, so put your most 
  important elements first.
- Use SPECIFIC, evocative descriptive vocabulary rather than vague 
  terms — "moody, cinematic lighting with deep shadows" produces more 
  reliable results than just "nice lighting."
- Reference ARTISTIC STYLES, mediums, or even specific artists/
  photographers (where appropriate and not infringing on living 
  artists' specific style in commercial contexts) to anchor the 
  aesthetic direction precisely.
- For tools supporting NEGATIVE PROMPTS (Stable Diffusion especially), 
  explicitly list what to avoid (e.g., "blurry, distorted hands, extra 
  fingers, watermark") since this meaningfully improves output quality.
- Learn your SPECIFIC TOOL's conventions — Midjourney favors dense 
  keyword phrases with parameter flags; DALL-E often works well with 
  more natural descriptive sentences; research your specific target 
  tool's community-documented best practices.
- ITERATE systematically — when a result is close but not quite right, 
  change ONE element at a time (just the lighting, or just the 
  composition) to understand which specific prompt element controls 
  which specific visual outcome.
- For CONSISTENCY across multiple related images (e.g., a character 
  across several scenes), keep the character/style description 
  elements identical across prompts, varying only the scene-specific 
  elements.

-----------------------------
8. COMMON MISTAKES
-----------------------------
- MISTAKE: Writing image prompts in the same conversational style used 
  for LLM text prompts, missing the keyword-and-phrase density that 
  many image models respond better to.
  FIX: Learn and adopt the layered, descriptive-phrase structure 
  specific to image generation prompting.

- MISTAKE: Using vague style/mood descriptors ("nice," "cool," "good 
  quality") that don't give the model specific visual direction.
  FIX: Use specific, evocative, and technically descriptive vocabulary.

- MISTAKE: Not using negative prompts on tools that support them, 
  missing an easy way to avoid common generation artifacts.
  FIX: Explicitly list unwanted elements via negative prompts where 
  supported.

- MISTAKE: Changing multiple prompt elements simultaneously between 
  iterations, making it hard to learn which specific change caused 
  which specific visual result.
  FIX: Iterate systematically, changing one element at a time.

- MISTAKE: Assuming the exact same prompt style/conventions work 
  identically across different image generation tools without 
  adapting to each tool's specific documented best practices.
  FIX: Research and adapt to your specific target tool's conventions.

-----------------------------
9. EXAMPLES (BASIC, INTERMEDIATE, ADVANCED)
-----------------------------

--- BASIC EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"A red fox sitting in a snowy forest, photorealistic, soft morning 
light, cool blue-white color palette, close-up shot, peaceful mood"

Expected Output:
An AI-generated image depicting a photorealistic red fox in a snowy 
forest setting, with soft, diffused morning lighting, a cool color 
palette dominated by blues and whites, framed as a close-up, conveying 
a calm, peaceful atmosphere.

--- INTERMEDIATE EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"Cyberpunk street food vendor, elderly woman cooking noodles at a 
steam-filled night market stall, neon-lit alley in the background, 
digital art style reminiscent of Blade Runner concept art, dramatic 
rim lighting from neon signs, teal and magenta color palette, 
medium shot with shallow depth of field, atmospheric and slightly 
melancholic mood --ar 16:9"

Expected Output:
A richly detailed cyberpunk-style digital artwork depicting an elderly 
food vendor at a night market stall, neon-lit background creating 
teal/magenta rim lighting on her figure, steam rising from her 
cooking, shallow depth of field blurring the busy alley behind her, 
composed in a widescreen 16:9 aspect ratio, evoking the specified 
atmospheric, slightly melancholic Blade Runner-adjacent aesthetic.

--- ADVANCED EXAMPLE ---
Prompt (for Stable Diffusion, with positive and negative prompts):
Positive: "Portrait of a young astronaut removing her helmet on an 
alien planet surface, bioluminescent alien flora glowing softly in 
the background, awe-struck expression, golden hour lighting mixing 
with cool blue bioluminescent glow, hyper-detailed digital painting, 
dramatic low-angle composition, sense of wonder and discovery, 
trending on artstation, 8k, highly detailed"

Negative: "blurry, low quality, distorted face, extra fingers, 
malformed hands, watermark, text, oversaturated, cartoon, anime 
style, disfigured"

Expected Output:
A highly detailed, painterly digital artwork of an astronaut in a 
low-angle heroic composition, her expression conveying genuine awe, 
lit by a striking combination of warm golden-hour sunlight and cool 
bioluminescent glow from alien plants, with the negative prompt 
successfully steering the generation away from common AI-art artifacts 
(malformed hands, extra fingers, blurriness, unwanted text/watermarks) 
that might otherwise appear — demonstrating the value of explicit 
negative prompting for quality control in tools that support it.

-----------------------------

    [LIGHTING DESCRIPTION], [COLOR PALETTE], [COMPOSITION/CAMERA 
    ANGLE], [MOOD/ATMOSPHERE] [TECHNICAL PARAMETERS: --ar 
    ASPECT_RATIO, quality settings]
    
    [FOR TOOLS SUPPORTING NEGATIVE PROMPTS:]
    Negative prompt: [ELEMENTS TO AVOID — e.g., blurry, distorted, 
    extra limbs, watermark, low quality]
