53. BRAINSTORMING PROMPTING

Part of: The Ultimate Prompt Engineering Library (Part 2: Creative)

-----------------------------
1. TITLE
-----------------------------
Brainstorming Prompting — Generating Volume and Variety of Ideas

-----------------------------
2. DEFINITION
-----------------------------
Brainstorming prompting is a technique specifically designed to 
maximize the QUANTITY and DIVERSITY of ideas generated for a given 
problem or creative challenge, deliberately deferring judgment/
filtering until AFTER generation — following the classic human 
brainstorming principle that idea quantity and quality judgment work 
better as separate phases rather than mixed together (since premature 
judgment suppresses creative volume). This differs from most other 
techniques in this library, which typically aim for one carefully-
reasoned best answer; Brainstorming Prompting explicitly wants BREADTH 
over depth in its first pass, with refinement/selection as an 
optional, clearly separate subsequent step.

Think of it like the difference between asking one trusted advisor for 
their single best recommendation (depth-focused) versus running an 
actual brainstorming session with a whiteboard, where the explicit 
rule is "no idea is criticized during this phase, we just want volume 
and variety, we'll filter and evaluate later" — the deferred-judgment 
structure itself is what unlocks more unusual, creative options that 
premature filtering would have cut too early.

-----------------------------
3. PURPOSE
-----------------------------
Brainstorming prompting exists to:
- Maximize IDEA VOLUME and DIVERSITY, surfacing options a single 
  "best answer" approach would never generate.
- Explicitly defer JUDGMENT/evaluation to a separate phase, preventing 
  premature filtering from suppressing creative or unconventional 
  options.
- Explore a problem space BROADLY before committing to a specific 
  direction, useful at the early stages of any creative or strategic 
  process.
- Surface UNEXPECTED connections or unconventional angles by 
  deliberately pushing past the first, most obvious ideas that come to 
  mind.
- Provide raw creative material that can be filtered, combined, or 
  refined in a subsequent, distinctly separate evaluation step (which 
  can then draw on other techniques like Self-Critique or Debate 
  Prompting).

-----------------------------
4. WHEN TO USE AND BEST MODEL OR LLM
-----------------------------
WHEN TO USE:
- Early-stage creative or strategic work where you want to explore 
  possibilities broadly before committing to a direction.
- Naming/branding, product feature ideation, marketing campaign 
  concepts, story premise generation, problem-solving approaches.
- Situations where you suspect the OBVIOUS answer isn't necessarily the 
  best one, and want to push past first-instinct responses.
- Any task explicitly asking for "ideas," "options," or "possibilities" 
  rather than a single recommendation.

WHEN NOT TO USE:
- When you already know what you want and just need it executed/
  refined (brainstorming for its own sake at this stage wastes time).
- Situations requiring a single, carefully-reasoned best answer rather 
  than broad exploration (use Goal-Oriented Prompting or Deliberate 
  Prompting instead).
- Highly technical/factual tasks with a single correct answer, where 
  "brainstorming" alternatives doesn't make sense.

BEST MODELS FOR BRAINSTORMING PROMPTING:
- GPT-4o/4.1/5-class — strong idea generation volume and variety, 
  generally willing to explore more unconventional territory when 
  explicitly invited to.
- Claude — capable brainstorming, sometimes benefits from explicit 
  encouragement to push toward more unusual/bold ideas rather than 
  defaulting to safe, conventional options.
- Gemini Pro/Ultra — strong general brainstorming capability across 
  domains.
- Grok — well-suited to more unconventional, irreverent brainstorming 
  given its design philosophy toward less conventional responses.
- Mistral / Llama — capable of basic brainstorming; may benefit from 
  more explicit prompting toward diversity (e.g., "ensure these 10 
  ideas are meaningfully different from each other, not variations on 
  one theme") to avoid clustering around very similar ideas.

-----------------------------
5. PROMPT FORMULA
-----------------------------
    "Brainstorm [NUMBER] [DIVERSE/UNCONVENTIONAL] ideas for [PROBLEM/
    CHALLENGE]. Defer judgment — include unconventional options. Do 
    not evaluate or filter yet, just generate volume and variety."

-----------------------------
6. PROMPT STRUCTURE
-----------------------------
    1. Problem/Challenge Statement — what you need ideas for.
    2. Quantity Target — a specific number (e.g., "generate 20 ideas") 
       which pushes past the first few obvious responses toward more 
       creative territory.
    3. Diversity Instruction — explicit request for genuinely different 
       ideas, not variations on a single theme.
    4. Deferred Judgment Instruction — explicit permission (and 
       encouragement) to include unconventional, even seemingly 
       impractical ideas without self-filtering.
    5. (Optional) Categorization — for large idea sets, ask for loose 
       grouping/categorization to make the volume easier to navigate.
    6. (Optional Second Phase) Evaluation Instruction — a CLEARLY 
       SEPARATE follow-up step to filter/rank the generated ideas, kept 
       distinct from the generation phase.

Example skeleton:
    "Brainstorm [N] ideas for [CHALLENGE]. 
    
    Rules for this brainstorm: 
    - Include a genuine mix of conventional AND unconventional/bold 
      ideas 
    - Do not filter or judge feasibility yet — quantity and variety 
      matter more than immediate practicality at this stage 
    - Ensure ideas are meaningfully different from each other, not 
      variations on one core idea 
    
    [OPTIONAL SEPARATE FOLLOW-UP: Now that we have this list, evaluate 
    which 3-5 are most promising, and explain why.]"

-----------------------------
7. BEST PRACTICES
-----------------------------
- Request a SPECIFIC, fairly high NUMBER of ideas (15-30 for a rich 
  brainstorm) — pushing past the first 5-8 obvious ideas is often where 
  genuinely creative options start to emerge.
- Explicitly grant PERMISSION for unconventional/impractical ideas — 
  models (like people) can default to "safe" suggestions unless 
  explicitly told that bold, even seemingly impractical options are 
  welcome at this stage.
- Explicitly request DIVERSITY — ask for ideas that are meaningfully 
  different from each other, since without this instruction, 
  generated lists can cluster around minor variations of the same core 
  concept.
- Keep the EVALUATION/FILTERING phase clearly SEPARATE from the 
  generation phase — mixing "generate ideas" and "tell me which is 
  best" into one request tends to produce fewer, more conservative 
  ideas since judgment creeps into the generation process.
- Consider asking for ideas from MULTIPLE DIFFERENT ANGLES or 
  perspectives explicitly (e.g., "some ideas focused on cost savings, 
  some on user experience, some on brand differentiation") to force 
  broader exploration.
- For genuinely stuck/difficult problems, try COMBINING with SCAMPER-
  style prompts (substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to another 
  use, eliminate, reverse) as explicit lenses to generate ideas from 
  different angles.
- After brainstorming, use a SEPARATE evaluation pass (potentially 
  Self-Critique or Debate Prompting) to filter the raw list down to 
  genuinely promising options.

-----------------------------
8. COMMON MISTAKES
-----------------------------
- MISTAKE: Requesting only a small number of ideas (3-5), which tends 
  to produce only the most obvious, conventional options without 
  pushing into more creative territory.
  FIX: Request a higher number (15-30) to push past first-instinct 
  responses.

- MISTAKE: Not explicitly granting permission for unconventional ideas, 
  resulting in a list of safe, predictable suggestions.
  FIX: Explicitly invite bold/unconventional ideas as part of the 
  brainstorm's rules.

- MISTAKE: Mixing generation and evaluation into one request ("give me 
  ideas and tell me the best one"), which tends to suppress idea volume 
  since judgment creeps into the generation process.
  FIX: Keep brainstorming (generation) and evaluation (filtering) as 
  clearly separate, sequential steps.

- MISTAKE: Getting a list of ideas that are really just minor variations 
  on one core concept rather than genuinely diverse options.
  FIX: Explicitly request diversity and meaningfully different angles.

- MISTAKE: Treating the raw brainstormed list as a final deliverable 
  without any subsequent filtering/evaluation pass.
  FIX: Always follow brainstorming with a distinct evaluation step to 
  identify the genuinely promising ideas.

-----------------------------
9. EXAMPLES (BASIC, INTERMEDIATE, ADVANCED)
-----------------------------

--- BASIC EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"Brainstorm 15 name ideas for a mobile app that helps people track 
their water intake. Include a mix of playful, professional, and 
metaphorical name styles. Don't filter or judge yet — just generate a 
diverse list."

Expected Output:
A list of 15 genuinely varied name ideas spanning different styles 
(playful: e.g., something punny about hydration; professional: 
something clean and clinical-sounding; metaphorical: something evoking 
rivers, oceans, or growth) — demonstrating real stylistic diversity 
rather than 15 minor variations on one naming approach.

--- INTERMEDIATE EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"Brainstorm 20 ideas for how a small independent bookstore could 
increase foot traffic, given a limited marketing budget (under $500/
month). Include ideas across different angles: community events, 
partnerships, digital/social media, physical store experience, and at 
least a few genuinely unconventional ideas you wouldn't normally see 
suggested. Do not evaluate feasibility yet — just generate volume and 
variety across these different angles.

Once the list is complete, separately identify the 5 ideas that seem 
most promising given the stated budget constraint, and briefly explain 
why for each."

Expected Output:
A list of 20 diverse ideas spanning the requested angles (e.g., 
community events: a monthly local-author reading series; partnerships: 
cross-promotion with a nearby coffee shop; digital: a hyper-local 
social media presence; physical experience: a redesigned window 
display rotation; unconventional: turning the store into a evening 
board-game venue one night a week) — followed by a CLEARLY SEPARATE 
evaluation section identifying the 5 most promising given the budget 
constraint, with brief reasoning for each selection.

--- ADVANCED EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"I'm stuck on how to differentiate my AI/ML content channel from the 
saturated field of generic coding tutorials, given my actual strengths: 
deep hands-on experience building production-grade systems (multi-
agent architectures, RAG pipelines, cryptographic tools) as a self-
taught teenage developer. 

Brainstorm 25 content angle/format ideas, deliberately spanning these 
different lenses: 
1. SUBSTITUTE — what if I replaced the typical tutorial format 
entirely with something else? 
2. COMBINE — what if I combined my technical content with something 
seemingly unrelated (my other interests, my age/background, a 
different medium)? 
3. ADAPT — what formats work well in OTHER creator niches that could be 
adapted to AI/ML content? 
4. MODIFY/EXAGGERATE — what if I took an existing format and pushed 
it to an unusual extreme? 
5. ELIMINATE — what if I intentionally left out something every other 
AI/ML creator includes? 
6. REVERSE — what if I approached the typical content structure 
backwards? 

Generate at least 3-4 ideas per lens (25+ total). Do not filter for 
practicality yet — I want genuine breadth, including ideas that seem 
strange at first glance. 

Afterward, separately flag which 4-5 ideas feel most genuinely 
differentiated (not just different for its own sake) given my actual 
stated strengths."

Expected Output:
A rich, genuinely diverse set of 25+ ideas organized by the requested 
SCAMPER-style lenses — for example, under SUBSTITUTE: replacing 
tutorials with "audit" content (reviewing/critiquing existing open-
source AI projects); under COMBINE: merging technical build content 
with genuine philosophical/reflective writing about building complex 
systems as a self-taught teenager; under REVERSE: starting from a 
finished, complex production system and working backward to show the 
early, messy decisions that led there, rather than the typical 
"tutorial builds up to a finished thing" structure — followed by a 
clearly separate evaluation section identifying which ideas most 
genuinely leverage the stated unique strengths (real production 
experience, self-taught teenage perspective) rather than being 
different merely for novelty's sake.

-----------------------------
10. REUSABLE PROMPT TEMPLATE
-----------------------------
    Brainstorm [NUMBER, e.g., 15-25] ideas for [YOUR PROBLEM/
    CHALLENGE].
    
    Rules for this brainstorm:
    - Include a genuine mix of conventional AND unconventional/bold 
    ideas
    - Do not filter or judge feasibility yet — prioritize quantity and 
    variety at this stage
    - Ensure ideas are meaningfully different from each other
    - [OPTIONAL: generate ideas across these specific angles/lenses: 
    LIST]
    
    [OPTIONAL SEPARATE FOLLOW-UP STEP, kept distinct from generation:]
    Now, separately evaluate this list — identify the [N] most 
    promising ideas given [YOUR ACTUAL CONSTRAINTS/GOALS], and briefly 
    explain why for each.
