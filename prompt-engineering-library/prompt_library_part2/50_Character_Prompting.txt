50. CHARACTER PROMPTING
=====================================================================
Part of: The Ultimate Prompt Engineering Library (Part 2: Creative)

-----------------------------
1. TITLE
-----------------------------
Character Prompting — Developing Deep, Consistent Fictional Characters

-----------------------------
2. DEFINITION
-----------------------------
Character prompting is a technique focused specifically on developing 
and maintaining a fictional CHARACTER'S full identity for creative 
writing purposes — their backstory, personality, motivations, speech 
patterns, physical description, relationships, and internal 
contradictions — with enough depth and specificity that the character 
behaves CONSISTENTLY and believably across multiple scenes, stories, 
or an entire narrative project. This differs from Persona Prompting 
(covered earlier in this library for having the MODEL itself adopt a 
character voice for conversation) in that Character Prompting is about 
DEVELOPING a fictional character AS A CREATIVE WRITING SUBJECT — 
someone the model writes ABOUT within a story, not someone the model 
becomes.

Think of it like the difference between an actor being asked to 
improvise IN CHARACTER for an audience (Persona Prompting) versus a 
novelist's detailed character bible/notes that inform how that 
character is written across an entire book — the character exists as a 
subject of the narrative, with depth that serves the STORY rather than 
a direct conversational interaction.

-----------------------------
3. PURPOSE
-----------------------------
Character prompting exists to:
- Create characters with genuine PSYCHOLOGICAL DEPTH and internal 
  consistency, avoiding flat, one-dimensional figures who only exist to 
  serve plot mechanics.
- Maintain CONSISTENCY across a longer narrative project (a novel, a 
  series, multiple related stories) so a character's voice, values, and 
  behavior patterns don't drift or contradict themselves between 
  scenes.
- Support ENSEMBLE casts where multiple distinct characters need 
  clearly differentiated voices, motivations, and relationships to one 
  another.
- Provide the CHARACTER FOUNDATION that Story Prompting (file 46), 
  Screenplay Prompting (file 47), and Dialogue Prompting (file 52) all 
  depend on for producing believable character-driven content.
- Enable deliberate CHARACTER ARCS — meaningful change over the course 
  of a narrative — by establishing a clear starting point that can be 
  tracked as it evolves.

-----------------------------
4. WHEN TO USE AND BEST MODEL OR LLM
-----------------------------
WHEN TO USE:
- Developing protagonists, antagonists, or significant supporting 
  characters for any narrative project (fiction, screenplay, game 
  narrative).
- Building ENSEMBLE casts where distinct, differentiated character 
  voices matter.
- Long-form or serialized projects requiring consistency across many 
  scenes/chapters/episodes.
- Any creative project where character believability and depth are a 
  primary quality concern.

WHEN NOT TO USE:
- Very minor, functional characters who appear briefly with no 
  meaningful depth requirement (a background waiter with one line 
  doesn't need a full character profile).
- When you want the MODEL itself to adopt a character's voice for 
  direct conversation (use Persona Prompting instead).
- Extremely short creative pieces where full character development is 
  disproportionate to the piece's scope.

BEST MODELS FOR CHARACTER PROMPTING:
- Claude — particularly strong at maintaining nuanced, psychologically 
  consistent characters across long creative writing sessions, with 
  good instinct for avoiding one-dimensional or contradictory character 
  behavior.
- GPT-4o/4.1/5-class — strong character development capability, 
  versatile across genres and character archetypes.
- Gemini Pro/Ultra — capable character work, particularly useful when 
  combined with visual character design (character art generation 
  alongside written profiles).
- Grok — capable of distinctive, often more irreverent character voices.
- Mistral / Llama — capable character development, especially larger 
  variants; may need more explicit reinforcement of established 
  character details in very long projects to avoid gradual drift.

-----------------------------
5. PROMPT FORMULA
-----------------------------
    [NAME] + [CORE IDENTITY/ROLE IN STORY] + [BACKSTORY] + 
    [PERSONALITY TRAITS + CONTRADICTIONS] + [MOTIVATION/WANT] + [FEAR/
    FLAW] + [SPEECH PATTERN] + [PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION] + [KEY 
    RELATIONSHIPS]

-----------------------------
6. PROMPT STRUCTURE
-----------------------------
    1. Name and Role — who they are and their function in the 
       narrative (protagonist, mentor, antagonist).
    2. Backstory — relevant history that shapes who they are now 
       (only what's actually relevant, not an exhaustive life history).
    3. Personality — core traits, INCLUDING internal contradictions or 
       tensions (genuinely interesting characters aren't simply 
       "kind" or "ruthless" — they contain some friction).
    4. Motivation/Want — what they're actively pursuing in the current 
       story.
    5. Fear/Flaw — their central weakness or blind spot, often what 
       will be tested by the story's conflict.
    6. Speech Pattern — how they talk, distinct from other characters.
    7. Physical Description — relevant visual details (only what 
       matters for the story, not an exhaustive physical inventory).
    8. Key Relationships — how they relate to other significant 
       characters in the story.

Example skeleton:
    "Character: [NAME], [ROLE IN STORY]. 
    Backstory: [RELEVANT HISTORY]. 
    Personality: [TRAITS], but also [CONTRADICTION/TENSION]. 
    Wants: [ACTIVE GOAL]. 
    Fear/Flaw: [CENTRAL WEAKNESS]. 
    Speech pattern: [HOW THEY TALK]. 
    Physical description: [RELEVANT DETAILS]. 
    Relationships: [KEY CONNECTIONS TO OTHER CHARACTERS]."

-----------------------------
7. BEST PRACTICES
-----------------------------
- Give every significant character a genuine INTERNAL CONTRADICTION or 
  tension — "confident but secretly terrified of failure," "generous 
  with strangers but cold with family" — flat, single-trait characters 
  read as less believable than ones with some internal friction.
- Distinguish characters' SPEECH PATTERNS clearly from one another in 
  ensemble casts — vocabulary level, sentence length, verbal tics, 
  what topics they gravitate toward or avoid.
- Focus backstory and physical description on what's actually RELEVANT 
  to the story being told, not an exhaustive biography — over-detailing 
  irrelevant history wastes effort and can dilute focus on what 
  actually matters dramatically.
- Establish the character's WANT (external, active goal) clearly, 
  since this is what drives their behavior and choices throughout the 
  narrative.
- For longer projects, maintain a CHARACTER REFERENCE that can be re-
  provided/reinforced in later prompts to prevent gradual drift in 
  voice or established facts across many separate writing sessions.
- When developing an ENSEMBLE, explicitly define each character's 
  RELATIONSHIP to the others — characters don't exist in isolation, and 
  their dynamics with each other often reveal character better than 
  solo description.
- Consider what the character's FLAW specifically sets up for testing 
  by the story's central conflict — a well-designed flaw connects 
  directly to what the narrative will challenge.

-----------------------------
8. COMMON MISTAKES
-----------------------------
- MISTAKE: Creating a character with only positive traits and no 
  internal contradiction or flaw, resulting in a flat, less believable 
  figure.
  FIX: Always include genuine internal tension or a meaningful flaw.

- MISTAKE: Giving multiple ensemble characters similar speech patterns/
  vocabulary, making them hard to distinguish in dialogue.
  FIX: Deliberately differentiate speech patterns across characters.

- MISTAKE: Over-detailing irrelevant backstory/physical description 
  that doesn't actually serve the story being told.
  FIX: Focus character details on what's genuinely relevant to the 
  narrative.

- MISTAKE: Not establishing a clear active want/goal, leaving the 
  character reactive rather than driving their own story.
  FIX: Always define what the character actively wants/pursues.

- MISTAKE: Character voice/details gradually drifting across a long 
  project without any reference document to check consistency against.
  FIX: Maintain and periodically re-provide a character reference 
  summary for long-form projects.

-----------------------------
9. EXAMPLES (BASIC, INTERMEDIATE, ADVANCED)
-----------------------------

--- BASIC EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"Develop a supporting character for a fantasy adventure story: a 
gruff blacksmith who secretly writes poetry. Personality: outwardly 
short-tempered and no-nonsense, but privately sentimental. Wants: to 
be taken seriously as an artist, not just a tradesman. Speech pattern: 
short, clipped sentences in conversation, but his internal 
monologue/poetry is lyrical and expressive — a deliberate contrast."

Expected Output:
A developed character profile for the blacksmith capturing the 
described gruff-exterior/sentimental-interior contradiction, with 
concrete details about how this tension might manifest in scenes 
(e.g., gruffly dismissing a compliment while secretly treasuring it), 
and guidance on maintaining the clipped-speech/lyrical-inner-voice 
contrast consistently.

--- INTERMEDIATE EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"Develop the antagonist for a corporate thriller. Name: Victor Ashford, 
CEO of a tech company. 

Backstory: Built his company from nothing after a difficult childhood 
marked by financial instability; this history drives an obsessive need 
for control and security. 

Personality: Charismatic and genuinely inspiring in public, but 
privately paranoid and willing to cross ethical lines he'd publicly 
condemn. The contradiction: he genuinely believes he's protecting his 
employees by any means necessary, which is how he rationalizes his 
worst actions to himself. 

Wants: To close a merger that would secure the company's future — and 
his own legacy — permanently. 

Fear/Flaw: Terrified of losing control/security again like he did in 
childhood; this fear, not simple greed, is what drives his willingness 
to cross lines. 

Speech pattern: Polished, persuasive, uses inclusive 'we' language 
publicly; more clipped and transactional in private. 

Relationship to protagonist: Was once a mentor figure to her before 
their ethical conflict began, making the confrontation more personally 
painful for both."

Expected Output:
A rich antagonist profile that avoids simple villainy by grounding 
Victor's actions in genuine psychological motivation (childhood 
instability → control-seeking) rather than cartoonish greed, with clear 
guidance for how his public/private personality contrast should 
manifest in different scene types, and an emphasis on the mentor-
turned-antagonist relationship dynamic that adds emotional complexity 
beyond a simple good-vs-evil framing.

--- ADVANCED EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"Develop a 3-character ensemble for a psychological drama exploring a 
family reunion after 15 years of estrangement. Each character needs a 
distinct voice, a want that conflicts with at least one other 
character's want, and a flaw connected to why the estrangement 
originally happened. 

Character 1 — MAYA (52): The eldest sibling who initiated the 
estrangement. 
Character 2 — DANIEL (48): The middle sibling who has spent 15 years 
resenting Maya for the family's fracture. 
Character 3 — THEIR MOTHER, ROSE (78): Whose declining health is the 
reason for this reunion, and who has her own undisclosed reasons for 
originally supporting Maya's decision to leave. 

For each: provide backstory relevant to the estrangement, personality 
with genuine contradiction, active want in THIS reunion, central flaw, 
distinct speech pattern, and how their want creates direct friction 
with at least one other character's want."

Expected Output:
Three distinct, interconnected character profiles: Maya perhaps wanting 
genuine reconciliation but through a controlling, terms-dictating 
approach that echoes the same behavior that caused the original rift 
(contradiction: seeking connection while repeating the pattern that 
severed it); Daniel wanting acknowledgment/apology before any 
reconciliation, whose resentment has calcified into a flaw of 
withholding forgiveness even when it's offered (creating direct 
friction with Maya's approach); Rose wanting the family whole again 
before she dies, but harboring an undisclosed reason for originally 
supporting Maya's departure that, if revealed, would complicate both 
her children's narratives about what happened — each with genuinely 
distinct speech patterns (perhaps Maya's controlled/measured language, 
Daniel's more clipped and defensive, Rose's more indirect and 
diplomatically evasive) — demonstrating an ensemble where individual 
character depth and INTER-character friction are developed together, 
since the dramatic tension emerges specifically from how these three 
wants and flaws interact.

10. REUSABLE PROMPT TEMPLATE
-----------------------------
    Character: [NAME], [ROLE IN STORY — protagonist/antagonist/
    supporting]
    
    Backstory: [RELEVANT HISTORY THAT SHAPES WHO THEY ARE NOW]
    
    Personality: [CORE TRAITS], but also [GENUINE INTERNAL 
    CONTRADICTION OR TENSION]
    
    Wants (active goal): [WHAT THEY'RE PURSUING IN THIS STORY]
    
    Fear/Flaw: [CENTRAL WEAKNESS, CONNECTED TO THE STORY'S CONFLICT]
    
    Speech pattern: [HOW THEY TALK — DISTINCT FROM OTHER CHARACTERS]
    
    Physical description: [RELEVANT VISUAL DETAILS]
    
    Key relationships: [HOW THEY RELATE TO OTHER SIGNIFICANT 
    CHARACTERS, INCLUDING ANY WANT-CONFLICTS]
