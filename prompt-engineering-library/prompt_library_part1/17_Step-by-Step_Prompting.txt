17. STEP-BY-STEP PROMPTING
=====================================================================
Part of: The Ultimate Prompt Engineering Library (Part 1: Reasoning)
Author: Deb Barman
=====================================================================

-----------------------------
1. TITLE
-----------------------------
Step-by-Step Prompting — Explicitly Sequencing the Process

-----------------------------
2. DEFINITION
-----------------------------
Step-by-step prompting is a technique where YOU (the prompt writer) 
explicitly define a NUMBERED SEQUENCE OF STEPS that the model must 
follow, in order, to complete a task — as opposed to Chain-of-Thought, 
where the model GENERATES its own reasoning steps freely. Here, the 
STRUCTURE of the process is predetermined by you; the model's job is 
to execute each defined step faithfully and in the correct order.

Think of it as the difference between telling someone "figure out how 
to bake this cake" (they invent their own process — closer to CoT) 
versus handing them a numbered recipe: "Step 1: preheat oven. Step 2: 
mix dry ingredients. Step 3: mix wet ingredients..." (Step-by-Step 
Prompting) — you've already designed the process; they just need to 
execute it correctly.

-----------------------------
3. PURPOSE
-----------------------------
Step-by-step prompting exists to:
- Enforce a SPECIFIC, REPRODUCIBLE process when the exact sequence of 
  operations matters (not just the final outcome).
- Reduce the model's tendency to skip important steps or take 
  shortcuts, especially on tasks with a known correct methodology.
- Provide auditability and consistency — since the steps are fixed, 
  outputs across multiple runs follow the same structure and are 
  easier to compare/verify.
- Break down complex, multi-part tasks into manageable, sequential 
  chunks that are easier for the model (and you) to track.
- Serve as a lightweight alternative to full Workflow Prompting when 
  you need sequence but not necessarily conditional branching logic.

-----------------------------
4. WHEN TO USE AND BEST MODEL OR LLM
-----------------------------
WHEN TO USE:
- You already KNOW the correct/required process and want it followed 
  exactly (e.g., a compliance checklist, a specific analysis 
  methodology, a required editing process).
- The task has a natural, fixed sequence where doing things out of 
  order would produce incorrect or lower-quality results.
- You need CONSISTENCY across many runs of the same type of task 
  (e.g., processing 50 documents the exact same way).
- Teaching or onboarding contexts where showing the explicit process 
  itself has educational value.
- Multi-part tasks where you want to verify each step's output before 
  the model proceeds to the next (can be done turn-by-turn).

WHEN NOT TO USE:
- The best process ISN'T known in advance and you want the model to 
  figure out its own approach (use Chain-of-Thought or Goal-Oriented 
  Prompting instead).
- The task is simple enough that an explicit process would be 
  overkill (a single-step task doesn't need "Step 1: do the thing").
- You want creative flexibility rather than a fixed procedure.

BEST MODELS FOR STEP-BY-STEP PROMPTING:
- ALL modern instruction-tuned models handle explicit step-by-step 
  instructions well, since faithfully following numbered/sequential 
  instructions is a core instruction-following capability: GPT-4o/
  4.1/5-class, Claude, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Qwen, Mistral-Instruct, 
  Llama-Instruct, Copilot, Cursor.
- Claude and GPT-4-class models are particularly reliable at not 
  skipping or reordering steps even in long, complex sequences.
- For VERY long step sequences (10+ steps), models with strong long-
  context handling (Claude, Gemini) maintain step fidelity better 
  than shorter-context models.
- Smaller/open-source models (Mistral 7B, Llama 8B) handle step-by-
  step instructions reasonably well but may benefit from processing 
  fewer steps at once (break very long sequences into smaller batches 
  of steps if you notice step-skipping).

-----------------------------
5. PROMPT FORMULA
-----------------------------
    [TASK OVERVIEW] + "Follow these steps in order:" + 
    "Step 1: [...]" + "Step 2: [...]" + "Step 3: [...]" + 
    [OUTPUT FORMAT/EXPECTATION FOR EACH OR FINAL STEP]

-----------------------------
6. PROMPT STRUCTURE
-----------------------------
    1. Task Overview — a one-sentence summary of the overall goal.
    2. Explicit Step List — each step clearly numbered, in the exact 
       required order, each step being a single clear action.
    3. (Optional) Step-Specific Output Instructions — if you want 
       output shown after EACH step (not just the end), specify this.
    4. Final Output Instruction — what the completed result should 
       look like after all steps are done.

Example skeleton:
    "Goal: [OVERALL TASK]. 
    Follow these steps in order, showing your work for each: 
    Step 1: [FIRST ACTION] 
    Step 2: [SECOND ACTION, building on Step 1] 
    Step 3: [THIRD ACTION, building on Step 2] 
    Step 4: [FINAL ACTION — produce the final output] 
    
    Complete all steps before giving your final result."

-----------------------------
7. BEST PRACTICES
-----------------------------
- Keep each step FOCUSED on a single, clear action — avoid combining 
  multiple distinct actions into one numbered step.
- Order steps in the EXACT sequence required — if order doesn't 
  actually matter for some steps, consider whether step-by-step is 
  even the right technique (vs. a simple checklist/constraint list).
- Make dependencies between steps explicit when relevant ("Step 3: 
  using the result from Step 2, calculate..."), especially for 
  multi-part calculations or analyses.
- For very long or complex processes, consider having the model show 
  its output after EACH step (rather than only at the end) so you can 
  verify correctness along the way and catch errors early.
- Combine with Constraint Prompting to add format/length rules to 
  individual steps or the final output.
- If a step could reasonably be skipped or shortened by the model, 
  add an explicit instruction like "do not skip any step, even if it 
  seems straightforward."
- For processes with 8+ steps, consider whether breaking the task 
  into multiple separate prompts/turns (one per major phase) would 
  produce more reliable results than one giant step list.

-----------------------------
8. COMMON MISTAKES
-----------------------------
- MISTAKE: Combining multiple distinct actions into a single numbered 
  step (e.g., "Step 2: Clean the data, remove duplicates, and 
  calculate averages" — that's really 3 steps).
  FIX: Break compound steps into separate, single-action steps.

- MISTAKE: Steps that don't actually need to happen in a specific 
  order, making the "step-by-step" framing unnecessary or confusing.
  FIX: Use step-by-step only when true sequential dependency exists; 
  otherwise use a simple requirements/checklist format.

- MISTAKE: Not specifying whether you want to see output after each 
  step or just the final result, leading to overly long or overly 
  terse responses relative to your actual need.
  FIX: Explicitly state your preference ("show your work for each 
  step" vs. "only show the final result after completing all steps 
  internally").

- MISTAKE: Writing so many steps (15+) in one prompt that the model 
  starts skipping or merging steps due to sheer length/complexity.
  FIX: Break very long processes into multiple prompts/turns, one per 
  logical phase.

- MISTAKE: Vague step descriptions that don't give the model enough 
  detail to execute correctly (e.g., "Step 3: Analyze it").
  FIX: Be as specific as possible about WHAT to analyze and HOW, 
  within each step.

-----------------------------
9. EXAMPLES (BASIC, INTERMEDIATE, ADVANCED)
-----------------------------

--- BASIC EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"Goal: Convert a temperature from Fahrenheit to Celsius. 
Follow these steps in order, showing your work: 
Step 1: Take the Fahrenheit temperature and subtract 32. 
Step 2: Multiply the result by 5. 
Step 3: Divide that result by 9. 
Step 4: State the final Celsius temperature. 

Convert 98.6°F using this process."

Expected Output:
"Step 1: 98.6 - 32 = 66.6 
Step 2: 66.6 × 5 = 333 
Step 3: 333 ÷ 9 = 37 
Step 4: Final Celsius temperature: 37°C"

--- INTERMEDIATE EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"Goal: Review a short piece of writing for quality. 
Follow these steps in order, showing your findings for each: 
Step 1: Read the text and identify the main argument or purpose. 
Step 2: Check for grammar and spelling errors — list any found. 
Step 3: Evaluate clarity — is the main point communicated clearly 
within the first 2 sentences? 
Step 4: Evaluate structure — does the text flow logically from 
point to point? 
Step 5: Provide one specific, actionable suggestion for improvement 
based on your findings from Steps 1-4. 

Text to review: 'AI is changing everything in are world. Many 
peoples dont understand how much its already effecting there daily 
lifes, from the phones to shopping, AI is everywhere now and will 
keep growing bigger and bigger in the future for sure.'"

Expected Output:
A structured 5-step review: Step 1 identifies the main point (AI's 
growing impact on daily life); Step 2 lists specific errors ("are" 
→ "our," "peoples" → "people," "dont" → "don't," "effecting" → 
"affecting," "there" → "their," "lifes" → "lives"); Step 3 notes the 
main point IS attempted early but is muddled by errors; Step 4 notes 
run-on structure and repetitive phrasing ("bigger and bigger"); Step 
5 gives one specific actionable suggestion (e.g., "Proofread for 
homophone errors and split the run-on sentence into two clearer 
sentences").

--- ADVANCED EXAMPLE ---
Prompt:
"Goal: Perform a security review of a Python function that handles 
user authentication. 
Follow these steps in order, documenting findings for each: 
Step 1: Identify how the password is being compared/verified. 
Step 2: Check if there is any protection against timing attacks. 
Step 3: Check if there is any rate-limiting or brute-force 
protection logic present. 
Step 4: Check how errors are handled — do error messages leak 
information (e.g., revealing whether the username exists vs. the 
password being wrong)? 
Step 5: Based on Steps 1-4, list all identified vulnerabilities in 
order of severity (Critical/High/Medium/Low). 
Step 6: For the single MOST CRITICAL vulnerability found, provide a 
specific code-level fix. 

Function to review: 
def login(username, password): 
    user = get_user(username) 
    if user is None: 
        return 'Error: Username not found' 
    if user.password == password: 
        return 'Login successful' 
    else: 
        return 'Error: Incorrect password'"

Expected Output:
A structured 6-step security review: Step 1 notes plaintext string 
comparison (==) is used instead of a secure comparison; Step 2 
identifies vulnerability to timing attacks due to non-constant-time 
comparison; Step 3 notes complete absence of rate-limiting/brute-
force protection; Step 4 identifies a critical username enumeration 
vulnerability (different error messages reveal whether a username 
exists); Step 5 ranks vulnerabilities (e.g., Critical: plaintext 
password storage/comparison and username enumeration; High: no rate 
limiting; Medium: timing attack surface); Step 6 provides a specific 
fix for the most critical issue, such as using a constant-time 
comparison function with hashed passwords (e.g., 
`bcrypt.checkpw()`) and returning an identical generic error message 
regardless of whether the username or password was wrong.

-----------------------------
10. REUSABLE PROMPT TEMPLATE
-----------------------------
    Goal: [OVERALL TASK/OBJECTIVE]
    
    Follow these steps in order, showing your work/findings for 
    each:
    Step 1: [FIRST SPECIFIC ACTION]
    Step 2: [SECOND SPECIFIC ACTION]
    Step 3: [THIRD SPECIFIC ACTION]
    Step 4: [CONTINUE AS NEEDED...]
    Step [N]: [FINAL ACTION — produce/state the final result]
    
    Complete all steps in order before giving your final result. Do 
    not skip any step.
